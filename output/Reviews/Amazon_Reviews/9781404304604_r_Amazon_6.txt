Reviewer: The Grey Piper W.H. Hudson was an Englishman who grew up in what is now Argentina, and wrote a number of non-fictional nature studies on his South American home. Much of his appreciation for Nature comes through in this novel, with vivid descriptions of the life of the jungles. The style is somewhat dated and I would hesitate to call it a gripping page turner which will have you up until 3 in the morning when you fall asleep from sheer exhaustion. Like many worthwhile endeavors, it requires a bit of effort.Some of the characterizations of the natives may seem inappropriate or even offensive to the modern reader,but deal with it. The story itself is something of a symbolic romance between the civilized man and the wild, untamed girl of the jungle, who shares a preternatural relationship with the animals, a subtly erotic feminine Mowgli. As much as our Western hero loves her, so is she shrouded in superstitious dread by the native tribesmen, who consider her an evil spirit. I guess I can't help comparing this to the movie starring Audrey Hepburn, and I suppose I should anyway, since I'm guessing most people who have come looking for this book know it because of the movie! I have to admit, this is one of the very rare instances where I have liked a movie more than the book it was drawn from. Perhaps the story, being so symbol-laden, even surrealistic, lends itself better to a visual medium than print. Hudson certainly does expend a lot of prose in his careful descriptions of settings and people, which of course is expressed in a moment or two of camera time. The movie departs from the book in a few notable ways, too -- I'll leave the reader to discover them. Bottom line -- certainly an entertaining and engaging read, once you're accustomed to the style. But it is NOT the movie. Read less
