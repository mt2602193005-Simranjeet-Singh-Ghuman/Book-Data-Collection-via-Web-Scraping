Reviewer: Scott McFarland I'm not sure whether the brilliance of this collection will be easy to appreciate for those who haven't read many of Dick's novels, and haven't already acquired the taste for his unique mashup of high concept, psychological depth, dark humor, and intentional chaos. But for those us who know what to expect from PKD, I can't recommend this collection highly enough. It's my first reading of his short stories. I feel as if I've just read 5 or 6 of his uniquely dense and brilliant novels, put into a blender; it's a beautiful feeling. "Cadbury, the Beaver Who Lacked", "Strange Memories of Death", and "I Hope I Shall Arrive Soon" are exceedingly poignant. "A Game of Unchance", "Return Match", and "Not By Its Cover" are science-fiction of the highest order, supremely entertaining and wildly imaginative. Numerous other stories herein are very good, if not quite as great as those IMO. Given the eccentric profile that Dick cut during his last decade, "Exegesis" and all, it's tempting to think that his capacity for great writing and insight was diminishing. But it wasn't. This collection as well as novels like "A Scanner Darkly" and "The Divine Invasion" prove that even after he entered what I would regard as a particularly troubled time in his life, he was still able to write like no one else. Reading the latter part of this collection, I'm struck by the tragedy of his death at 53. This book is a treasure. Read less
