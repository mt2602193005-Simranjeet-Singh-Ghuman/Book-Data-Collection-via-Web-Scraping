Reviewer: Amazon Customer Mostly late 90s stories that do connect into a larger whole but also work as originally published, as individual pieces. There's an alternative table of contents to put the stories in sequence, if that's your thing. I read from start to finish, traditional style, and that also works - jumping back and forth in time in the (sort-of) narrative. If you're familiar with this author, you'll know of course that works. A couple of pieces tie in as background for Threshold - In the Water Works (Birmingham, Alabama 1888) and The Long Hall on the Top Floor. The middle section of the latter story appears in Low Red Moon but the introduction and ending in this volume greatly add to it. With some of the post-industrial settings and rough & violent characters (with mega-swearing throughout), you might think this was grim and depressing reading - not so. The quality of the writing lifts this into comfort-book territory and I can see myself returning to it again and again (shelved next to Shirley Jackson, when I come to look for it). Read less
