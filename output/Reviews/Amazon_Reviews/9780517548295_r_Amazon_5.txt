Reviewer: Slade Allenbury For two thirds of its length, "The Summer of Katya" is a leisurely-paced idyll about a young doctor falling in love with a mysterious beauty named Katya who has recently moved into a summer house just outside of the small French town where the doctor works. The doctor's attempts at forming an intimate relationship with Katya are largely thwarted by her twin brother, Paul, for reasons that are not initially clear but apparently too sensational for Paul to state outright. Is the brother himself in love with Katya? Are Katya and Paul engaged in an incestuous love affair of their own? And what about the father of Paul and Katya, an absent-minded professor and widower who may have a few deep, dark secrets of his own where Katya is concerned? For a long time Trevanian opts only to tease us with these questions while providing no answers to them. But in the last third of the book, the revelations come fast and furious. When the mystery of why Paul is so strenuously opposed to a union between Katya and the doctor is laid bare, the answers are juicy enough to satisfy even the most demanding fan of sensation fiction. In its final third the book becomes a splendidly lurid potboiler that, though it may stretch credulity just a bit, is thrilling to read. Trevanian's prose is elegant in its simplicity. This is popular fiction of a very high level of craftsmanship. Read less
