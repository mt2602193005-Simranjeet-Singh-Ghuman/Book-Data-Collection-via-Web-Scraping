Reviewer: ROBIN MCCALL The Captains is the best of this Brotherhood of War series, which is also the best military fiction series that I have ever read. The series is so well written that I am constantly buying new military history books to determine who is real and who is fiction, and how much the facts have been changed to create the story. The Captains is the best book in this great series because it combines the author's personal military experience with a realistic story about the Korean War era. The book talks about war and the military the way they are in real life, with people growing and dying, and new people entering as others leave or die. The quality of all of the books in the series is similar. For many people the best book is the one that addresses the time period that they were associated with the Army or the military. The books in this series combine the best of reality, the author's personal experiences, and the tall tales told to him by his many military friends, particularly flag officers and special forces personnel. Some of the stories are true, many are variations of the truth, and some are just great stories meant to entertain. Military readers like these books so much that they are actually hurt to find out that they are fiction, instead of being completely factual. This is military fiction at its best. Griffin tells us about the good and bad of war and the military, and he makes it easy to recognize the difference. These books can improve the attitudes of people who think their job is not important, or not part of the big picture, and they can help you recognize some of the absurdities of life. Read less
