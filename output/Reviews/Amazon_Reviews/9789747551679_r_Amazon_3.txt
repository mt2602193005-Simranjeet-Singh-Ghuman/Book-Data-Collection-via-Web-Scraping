Reviewer: Stranger I read the book twice, 5 years apart (just like translator Susan Kepner says many readers do). I got again engrossed in the daily lives of this family of a poor immigrant from China who works himself out of poverty through hard work and a little luck, but struggles to remain Chinese, especially with his and his Thai-born Chinese wife's kids. Even though I found him irritatingly stubborn and old-fashioned, I couldn't help getting to like him as he mutters his way through the daily mess that his kids cause because they want to live like modern young people, Thai or Chinese. The father is of course an allegory of the archetypical immigrant who struggles between two cultures, and in the end he accepts that he made a lot of mistakes and has peace with the world as it is. As I don't read Thai I can't judge if it is a good translation, but the fact that translator Susan Kepner and Botan (Supa Sirisingh) were/ are friends since a long time, I assume that the translation gets Botan's approval. One observation about the Amazon page though: "Follow the author" should in my view show Susan Kepner side by side with the author, Botan, and not just on her own. Read less
