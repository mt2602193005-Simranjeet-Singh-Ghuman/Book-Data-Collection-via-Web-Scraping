Reviewer: MaddalenaC I was fairly sure I had read the story a very long time ago, but couldn't remember the plot, only that the ending was sad. So I read it again The ending is more than sad; it is tragic.. I feel two ways about this book It was a page-turner, and the real mystery lasted until the end. I couldn't stop reading in pursuit of it. Trevanian must have done a lot of research into the Basque culture, and his glimpses of the festival attended by the principal characters are colorful and fascinating, especially for the way in which they bring out a previously unknown side of the normally reserved, albeit forthright in manner, Katya. The narrator is Dr Jean-Marc Montjean, who after serving in the great war has come to the village of Salies-Les Bains in the French Pyrenees near the village where he was born. He meets and falls in love with an innocent and plainspoken young woman who lives with her father and twin brother in an isolated house outside the village. The atmosphere of Etcheverria, the rundown house rented by the absent- minded scholar M. Treville, with his indolent sarcastic son Paul, and his look-alike innocent, and vivacious -at times - twin sister Katya is eerie. There is a sense of secrets in that house. Keep reading as I did in pursuit of the mysteries. Page turner notwithstanding, this is psychological horror. The ending is so traumatic that I now realize why I must have banished the novel from my memory. Now to set about erasing the mental images of the ending from my memory again. You have been warned. Read less
