Reviewer: No Theme Reviews I read this book when I was in high school. I was consuming everything scifi back then. I loved it. It was so well written and mysterious, that I kept reading it over several years. I'm 30 now and recently decided to re-read some of my favorite childhood books. This is one of those re-read books. First off, Klause's book is as fun to read now as it was when I was a kid. I expected the writing to be childish and boring, but I was pleasantly surprised to find that it was still interesting. Puck is a great character; she's a child but still mature enough to explore the mystery in this book without looking out of place. Hush is a very sympathetic character as well. He's interesting, relateable (without being overly so), and secretive. The friendship these two form is realistic as friendships go, and meaningful. The best thing about this book is that the main characters are round. Meaning, they change their attitudes, beliefs, conduct, understanding, etc., throughout the story, and at the end of the novel, they have grown (or gotten worse). By the end of the book, you actually feel as if you went through Puck's and Hush's mystery, that you formed a bond with them, and that you are actually sad by the end of their journey together. There's a lot of good that can be said about this book. My one irritation with the book is that it didn't really open a new universe to explore. That is, the ship is a microcosm unto itself; it doesn't really open up a new universe for you to be fascinated by. But that's ok for a young adult book. It's worth reading and re-reading. It's a book you'll enjoy, and something your kids can have fun with. There's a good moral throughout the story, wherein two strangers who are both outsiders learn about each other, friendships, and acceptance of not only others, but also of themselves. Must read. Read less
