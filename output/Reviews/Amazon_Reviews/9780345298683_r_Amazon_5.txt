Reviewer: R. Jones The plot of this novel is very simple - almost everyone on Earth disappears, leaving a handful of humans and a lot of robots. Five thousand years later some of the humans have developed psychic abilities, others have learned to live with nature rather than exploiting it, and the robots have created their own civilization. Then they all learn that the disappeared humans are planning to return, and they don’t want them to. That becomes the one big conflict in the book. The characters spend a lot of time thinking about themselves, their humanity (or, in the case of the robots, their lack thereof), and man’s place in the universe. It’s an interesting novel if you’re interested in that sort of thing, but not if you’re looking for a novel where a lot happens. Some of the science fictiony things that happen are explained (though never with any real science) but others aren’t. As the title indicates, it’s more about faith and religion than science, but presented in a science fiction tale. Personally I enjoyed it, though parts of it were a little frustrating. Read less
