Reviewer: M. A. Seifter One more excellent addition to my considerable store of Zola novels. I am very pleased with this novel, as it reveals, once more, Zola's encyclopedic knowledge of French society during the 2nd Empire, this time in his narrative of Octave Mouret and his splendid department store, Au Bonheur des Dames. Zola's plumbing of popular psychology is never far from the surface in his novels, and here he reveals the phenomenon of mass consumerism, such as we have it today, with hordes of middle and upper middle class women trooping into the great department store, ogling and purchasing according to their impulses of conformity and greed, while the self-satisfied manager gazes on with quiet joy. And yet, a little contrarily, even Mouret, who is a rather amoral individual, succumbs to the impulses of love and personal need for someone's connection, as he falls in love with one of his serving girls.
