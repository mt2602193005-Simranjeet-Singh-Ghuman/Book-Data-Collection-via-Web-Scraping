Reviewer: Professor Mark W. Tebbit This novel originates from one of the most notorious cases in English law, that of R v Dudley & Stephens, in which two men were convicted of murder for killing and eating a cabin boy in order to survive, after 20 days adrift in the Atlantic. The tale is told with great verve and ingenuity. It is in effect the story of the captain Tom Dudley, with all the background details meticulously researched. The author presents the full horror of both the ordeal and the killing, then the aftermath and the consequences for the two who had done the deed. He shows psychological insight and awareness of the moral complexity of the defence of necessity, which they both tried to run, unsuccessfully. For anyone interested in law or simply in human nature, I cannot recommend this book highly enough. Read less
