Reviewer: Anna O Georgette Heyer writes light hearted historical romance suitable for all ages. I have been a fan since I was a teenager, reading at night by torch light under the bed covers ( I shared a room with my siblings). The characters are diverse and entertaining, the men swoon worthy, the females charming and intelligent and the plot with twists and turns. What is so great about Heyer's work is the historical detail and authenticity, immersing the reader in the period. False Colours has less action than some of Heyer's other stories. This is more a situation comedy and dialogue driven. Unexpected, Kit Fancot arrives home from Vienna in the night drawn by a sense of urgency. His mother, Lady Danville, greets him on the stairs as he sneaks into the London house. Witty and humorous, Lady Danville's flight of ideas and non sequitur ramblings frustrate Kit's attempts to discover what has set his mother in such a state of agitation. It is not that his older twin brother, Evelyn Lord Danville has gone missing. It is that Evelyn hasn't sent word and is due to meet his intended fiancee's family on the morrow. To save face for Evelyn and Cressy (Evelyn's 'about to be' betrothed), Kit steps in for the evening. The impersonation snowballs and Kit is trapped in a role. Dire obstacles with a clever resolution. Narration by Phyllida Nash brings the story to sparkling life: the diplomatic and well spoken Kit, the charming Cressy, the bewitching and lovely (dowager) Lady Danville, her devoted long time admirer Bonamy Ripple in his creaking Cumberland corset, and a cast of loyal servants. Read less
