A hard one to rate. Probably 3,5 stars, though I may round it up after some further contemplation. I was here for Waterloo and that part did not disappoint, it was dramatic, tense, terse, uplifting and very sad, so 5 stars for sure. However, the "romance", pivoting on Barbara Childe, intended to be a female counterpart to rakish heroes, is a complete letdown. - 2 stars Interesting idea, but sadly a very unconvincing performance. If you give your "femme fatale" the character of a not very bright, immature 12-year-old, top it up with petty nastiness and nasty pettiness, don't expect me to take her seriously for a second, please. Not even if all other characters in the book (and there are a LOT of them) stand in a line and keep chanting "femme fatale, femme fatale..." at me all day. GH, where is the subtlety and the wit? Setting up another female character as Barbara's foil is a gross manipulation attempt, which I didn't appreciate.
