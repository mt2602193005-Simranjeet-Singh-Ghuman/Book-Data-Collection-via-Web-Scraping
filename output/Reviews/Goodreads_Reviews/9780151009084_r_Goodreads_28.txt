Set on the titular ship Star of the Sea in 1847, this book tells the story of a diverse group of people on a voyage across the Atlantic destined for New York. Through flashbacks, it also relates the history of the Great Potato Famine. Key characters include David Meredith (Lord Kingscourt) and his family. Meredith is a Protestant Englishman born and raised in Ireland, who inherited his title from his father. Due to the clashes between landowners and tenant-farmers, he is much maligned and marked for death. The family is traveling with the children’s nanny. A mysterious man walks the decks at night dragging his wounded leg. Grantley, an American writer, has visited Ireland and is outraged by the mistreatment of the poor and starving tenants. He writes articles for a newspaper and is hoping to publish a novel. We also hear from the ship’s Captain, whose journal documents his observations about the voyage and lists the many deaths of the passengers in steerage. O’Connor is a top rate storyteller. It is Gothic in tone, atmospherically written in the manner of the era, and reminds me of many 19th century novels (particularly Dickens – who is mentioned several times). It is engaging, particularly the first three-fourths. The last fourth seems a bit long and contains a surprise ending. I picked this one up on the strength of O’Connor’s recently published work, My Father’s House. I love it when I “discover” an author who has been writing for a long time and has a large back catalogue to tackle!
