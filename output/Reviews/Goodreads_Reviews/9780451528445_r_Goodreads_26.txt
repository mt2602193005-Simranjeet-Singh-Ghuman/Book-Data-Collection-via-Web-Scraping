I read the Gita through the official translation published by the Sacred Veda Text Review and Translation Team (Vedanuvada Samiti) of the Ministry of Religious Affairs of the Republic of Indonesia, which can be said to be quite new, from the year 2021. For some reason, I feel that this translation is not optimal because there are several typos that could have been avoided, such as the repetition of words. However, overall, I gained several insights into Hindu teachings from this book. The book begins with a question between Arjuna and Krishna (who is identified as God) at the start of the war. Arjuna wavers in his stance because he faces close relatives such as his teacher and friends. This then begins Arjuna’s journey of discussion with Krishna. In general, I feel that the teachings in this book are similar to Stoicism, such as focusing on self-control, detachment from emotions, and mastery over ego. One interesting thing here is that Krishna demands to be worshiped through prostration, which I feel is not practiced by Hindus in Bali, while Muslims around the world do this.
