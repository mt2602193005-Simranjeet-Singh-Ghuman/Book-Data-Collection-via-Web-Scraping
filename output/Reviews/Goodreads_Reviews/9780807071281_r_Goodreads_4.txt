I deeply (and jealously) admire Hiestand's gift for putting together two- to three-word phrases that express such precise meaning and feeling, such as the "village-scale" amenities in her part of the city, or red fire ants like "stinging jewels." And I like nonfiction that reads like fiction, that exists in the liminal space between fiction and memoir. The childhood half of the book is sublime. I couldn't give it five stars because swaths of the second half drag with minutia about the local water board and regional prehistory, which is about as boring as sitting through an actual meeting of your local water board.
