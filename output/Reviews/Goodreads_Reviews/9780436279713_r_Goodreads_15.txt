Ever since I read the 1959 classic Hawaii , I've been hoping for a similar experience with Michener's other works ever since, to little avail - and this latest foray was no different. I have started Caribbean several times, the most recent being when I was actually in the Caribbean itself to try and help boost my enthusiasm, but I just cannot get into it. It reads less like an epic historical fiction piece that it wants to be, and more like a collection of history textbook sections with some plot and dialogue mixed in. That, and I mostly find Michener's treatment of his indigenous characters a bit off-putting. They're somehow well-researched, yet oddly exoticized at the same time. It doesn't help that a decent amount of what has been presented as fact has already been called into question by more recent historical research - in particular his first chapter on the Arawaks and the cannibalistic Caribs (from what I have read, many experts feel that reports of cannibalism amongst some indigenous Caribbean peoples were just claims by European settlers to justify many of their actions). I think that I have tried this book for the final time. I am glad that others have enjoyed this, but I think it's time I realize that this particular title - and maybe most of Michener's work in general - just isn't for me.
