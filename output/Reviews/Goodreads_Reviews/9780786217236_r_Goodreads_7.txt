It was neat to read this book alongside Dancing After Hours because, in some cases, you get the germ of the short story in the memoir/essay. I've admired Dubus père for many years, consider "A Father's Story" one of the best I've EVER read, right up there with Carver's "A Small, Good Thing" and Kate Braverman's "Tall Tales from the Mekong Delta" (if you want to know my absolute favorites). Anyhow, it was good to get some insights into Dubus, perhaps more important since his son's memoir, Townie. Balancing the two sides of the man, his extraordinary ability to craft a story and his flaws as a husband and father is tough.
