this is another second read as I go through my Gore Vidal home collection. I read The Smithsonian Institution quite a few years ago ,and liked it, but wasn't sure what to make of it. For some reason I read it as straight science fiction with a hardon for Woodrow Wilson,, which of course, is a huge mistake. It's a great piece of political satire and old beltway insiderism. I think Henry Adams might approve. I"m sure some readers wouldn't approve, however, of 13 year old T's relationship with Squaw (Frank Cleveland), but hey.... BTW, this time around I love Grover Cleveland's rather historic role. He's one of my favorite presidents; maybe the last decent president we had. As usual ,the historical portraits are so telling. Not your standard American history read for sure--but I believe much more accurate than the official story crap that's fed in the schools until you get to college (at least) and more likely grad school. The only caveat I have is that I still find some of the book a little confusing. It's probably not GV's fault. I know nothing about the theory of relativity and string theory and other scientificisms in the book. I decided not to fear and just took them as they came, since I"m more interested in politics than the science of time travel.I did spot some of Adam's dynamo.
