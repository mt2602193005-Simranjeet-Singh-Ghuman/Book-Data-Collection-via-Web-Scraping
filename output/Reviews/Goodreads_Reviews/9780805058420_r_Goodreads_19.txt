Huge swaths of this book make absolutely no sense at all, but who expects Greil Marcus to make sense *all* the time? The incomprehensible bits are worth it for when the bizarreness works. His free form recitation of of Dylan Goes Electric myth is the only thing I've ever read that conveys the earth-shattering importance of the moment. I've always thought it sounded absurd, and, in retrospect, it is...but it wasn't at the time and only Greil Marcus can describe what it felt like then. All of American history fights to get mentioned. The stories about musicians Frank Hutchinson and Doc Boggs somehow include Appalachian miners strikes, The New Folk movement of the 50s, the Great Depression and modern country radio. It doesn't make sense, but that's okay. The weakest parts are those about the Basement Tapes. Partly because they are least comprehensible and partly because I don't really trust or believe him here. I don't hear what he hears. Is this really a moment of mystical inspiration, capturing on tape the very spirit of America before it was lost forever? Or a recording of a bunch of drunk guys jamming in a basement? I keep listening for the former, but can't find it.
