I read about a third and then skimmed the rest, partially because his other books say these things more succinctly and generally, and partially because I feel like this positive parenting comes from a perspective I don't connect to as much as other books. The language can be really patronizing and the scripts follow suit. So while I feel like there is value in knowing some of the principles in this book, they are better learned from other books. Specifically, I feel like books by Adele Faber are much better at teaching the positive parenting skills and principles and they come from a perspective of validation and teaching rather than using reinforcement to control.
