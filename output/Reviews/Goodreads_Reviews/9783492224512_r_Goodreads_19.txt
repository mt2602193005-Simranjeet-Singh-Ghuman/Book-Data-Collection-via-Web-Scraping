Three friends—rather hammy, a bit hypochondriac, a tad lazy, not very talented either, and to top it off, a touch disingenuous—accompanied by their stubborn dog, decide to canoe up the Thames to get some fresh air and unwind. Jérôme, one of the three companions, recounts their journey: a series of tribulations peppered with encounters, personal anecdotes, and touristy stories. Published in 1889, this book, although panned by literary critics of the time, was a huge success with the public. So, it's true, it may not be high literature, but it's hilarious! I burst out laughing several times, especially in the first part. Of course, you have to appreciate this kind of humor, mainly focused on situational comedy and told with that legendary British phlegm, that deadpan, nonchalant tone. An absurd story? Without a doubt. But also a good pretext for mocking the absurdity of certain human behaviors in the Victorian era, ultimately not so different from our own. This is certainly the aspect of the book I preferred. On the other hand, the historical-touristy-comedic anecdotes that follow one another sometimes feel out of place. But regardless of these minor flaws, our three gentlemen (and their not-so-gentle dog!) bravely face the Thames like a storm in a teacup! It's funny, it's entertaining, and it's exactly what I needed!
