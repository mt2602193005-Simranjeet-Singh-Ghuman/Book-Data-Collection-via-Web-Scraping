This is an okay book. As another reviewer mentioned, she bypasses the third choice (wait for a guy she can love who is also LDS) and that's a bit irritating, but I remember being at that age (Disclaimer: I'm LDS). A lot of LDS folk get married in their early twenties, so the pressure can be pretty intense from a social aspect. (The LDS church itself doesn't put any pressure on women to get married as soon as possible. The focus is more on finding quality men than get-married-now in their advice to women.) There's some unbelievable moments. However, Michael is very well done (especially his angry trip to the temple to see what all the fuss is about). You want good things to happen to him. In fact, of all the characters I think he's the one with the most depth. Emily is okay. She's a wimp who can't stand up to her husband, but that changes over the course of the story. Her husband, Ryan, also changes and I thought they made a good couple. However, I found the ending more than a bit of a cheat. It's like the author couldn't bring herself to end it the way it should have ended because that would have ruined the fantasy of The Perfect Guy. Emily's husband was not a bad guy. He was far from perfect, but he wasn't bad, just weak. When Emily stopped being such a doormat, he improved and respected her more (tends to happen). Their marriage was going well when he died. I would have much rather read a story about two people improving their marriage than a story about Mr. Imperfect dying and Mr. Perfect saving the day. I feel more strongly about this now than when I was single. However, it's a nice fantasy with a good, interesting hero (Michael). Good for killing time when you have nothing better to do.
