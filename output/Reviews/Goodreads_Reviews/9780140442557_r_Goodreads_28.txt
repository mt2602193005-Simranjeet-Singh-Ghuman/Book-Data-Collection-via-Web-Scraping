I'm honestly surprised by how moved I am by this book, how invested I ended up being in the characters. Arthurian legends are not really my thing, with the exception of Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, but this one had me hooked from beginning to end. A great kingdom of great men torn apart by great sins. As much as I came to hate Lancelot for his role in all of this, I found his repentance sincere and felt sorrow at his death. And yes, spoiler, he's dead. Everyone's dead. The whole damn kingdom was ruined in a brutal run of civil wars between people the reader comes to like but who have legitimate reasons to be at each other's throats. The only serious weakness is this story is Mordred, who suddenly is declared to be evil. A real missed opportunity, because there could be many reasons for him to turn on Arthur, and even if those reasons are not exactly noble, they could have been fleshed out. It was Gawain who really got to me. I know his adventure with the Green Knight is kind of an offshoot story and not part of the Vulgate Cycle, but that was the Gawain I saw go mad (again, with good reason) and go to doom against Lancelot. I am slightly concerned I may have ruined that other story for myself, because that is one of my favorites. "now I know that penitence is more valuable than any other thing. I shall never leave penitence for as long as I live."
