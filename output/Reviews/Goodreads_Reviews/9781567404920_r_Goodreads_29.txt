*3.5 I definitely enjoyed how blunt this book was. It wasn't sugar coating anything, and it didn't just talk about the tragedy or how Cassie had been a victim of a terrible shooting. It talked about how Cassie was a person just like any of the oter victims. She wasn't this perfect martyr or a perfect teenager. She was a girl who felt strongly about her faith and was not going to two shooters take that from her. I have two reasons why I gave this 3.5 stars. The first reason being the controversy over her actually saying yes. Not only is the controversy over Cassie saying yes, but also two other victims, Lauren Townsend and Rachel Scott. The controversy over whether any of them were asked about their faith and if any of them even said "yes." The second reason being my dislike for her mother and some of her views toward goth and alternative culture. This dislike is strictly my opinion. But, I disliked how her mother often put blame on music, specifically Marilyn Manson (who has taken so much hate and ridicule from this disaster). It was very frustrating for her to blame people like Manson, but it's more frustrating to think that thousands of parents are just like her. Music does play a giant impact on who a person is, but to put blame on the music or video game or movie industry for a shooting that happened is ridiculous. Her mother was often quite judgemental of the clothing she wore and dark eyeliner. Things like these are not to be blamed for depressive behavior. However, I fully respect her views and ideas about raising her children.
