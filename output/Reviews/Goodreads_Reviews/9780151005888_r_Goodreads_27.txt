My first Messud-but not my last. These two novellas are exquisitely written—one concerning a Ukrainian-Canadian woman named Marie; the other concerning a visiting academic in London—and her very strange downstairs neighbor. Marie is presented first as a weekly cleaning woman in Toronto—but her life contains multitudes, from girlhood in A hamlet, much of WWII in a German work camp; a daring escape; a displaced person’s camp; love; emigration; service; a son, a life. “The Hunters” is another portrayal of dislocation—with a riddle running through it. Although the ending is not all wrapped up in a bow, it’s an intriguing, twisty journey.
