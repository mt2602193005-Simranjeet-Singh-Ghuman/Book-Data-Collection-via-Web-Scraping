Whalesong is a perfect example of why children’s literature should never be discounted for adult reading. This delightful tale is not only an ecological fable but also a redemption allegory. The writing is lyrical and beautiful and reflects the author’s background as a poet. Hruna is a young male humpback whale who is new to the world and his surroundings. As he grows, he befriends a female whale his age named Lewte. He leaves his pod to begin his rite of passage called the Lonely Cruise, takes the Plunge, and encounters the dangers humanity brings to the ocean. I loved exploring, questioning, and struggling alongside Hruna as he comes of age. His friendships with two albatrosses, another whale, and dolphins brought warmth and wonder to the story, while his encounters with whalers revealed its heartbreaking realities. I worried for his safety whenever curiosity got the better of him. Like Hruna, I understand the feeling of a longing that cannot quite be put into words. This book is the first in a trilogy and is suitable for ages 10 and up. I recommend this story to readers who love whales, the ocean, ecology, tender coming-of-age tales, and spiritual allegories that linger long after the story ends.
