I settled on Ronald Wright’s ‘Time Among the Maya’ to accompany a six-week visit to Guatemala. While Mr Wright’s painstakingly comprehensive grasp of the Mayan calendar was occasionally beyond my limited layperson’s reach, the bulk of the book is an engaging if bleak travelogue - a snapshot of mid-1980s Central America. Given that Guatemala was embroiled in a murderous civil war at the time of Mr Wright’s visit, in which the overwhelming majority of victims were Mayan, the tone is understandably often solemn, even sinister. But ‘Time Among the Maya’ was an excellent primer on the history, culture and politics of the region. I finished the book considerably better informed - not only about Guatemala, but also about the Maya of Chiapas and the Yucatan peninsula. An afterword penned in 2015 offers some perspective on the harsh years of the mid-1980s, and a glimmer of optimism for the future of the Mayan people.
