Copleston was popular at Loyola University Chicago--and not just because he and it were Jesuitical. His "A History of Philosophy' was reputed to be a clear, readable overview of the field, albeit almost entirely focused on the West. The reading of his first volume on classical Greek and Roman philosophy confirmed this opinion, so, eventually, I proceeded to read more. While Volume I was good, Volume II, on medieval philosophy, was excellent. I'd been exposed to the medieval thinkers at seminary, but not so thoroughly, my interest being more in ancient and reformation thinking back then. Perhaps it was because I knew so little that I was so impressed by this introduction to the period.
