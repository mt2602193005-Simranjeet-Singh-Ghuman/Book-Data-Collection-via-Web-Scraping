A cleverly written novel about Aaron Bentham, an English professor at a New England college who is on sabbatical, writing a novel. The majority of this book is about the novel Aaron is writing, which is the retelling of his college days. The protagonist, Allard Benson, who is 21, aspires to be a writer. He is a WWII vet. His college friend is Harold Roux, also a vet, who is bald and wears a toupee. Harold is a diffident, priggish young man of 24. Allard is in love with Mary, a devout Catholic and a virgin. Allard is also in love with Naomi, a Jewish radical, who he has lots of sex with. Whilst Aaron is writing his novel, he is interrupted by a student who goes missing and a colleague and friend, George Buck, is in jeopardy of losing his job because he has not completed his Ph D dissertation, which he is contracted to finish. Added to these two issues, Aaron has forgotten to attend his wife’s parent’s fortieth wedding anniversary party. Issues in this novel include, the creative writing process, memory and its reconstruction, relationships, and trying to understand and make sense of oneself. Good plot momentum and interesting characters make for a worthwhile, satisfying reading experience. This novel won the 1975 National Book Award for fiction.
