Disch can be a challenging writer, and I had a hard time deciding whether to give this 3 or 4 stars. I decided on 4 stars because this is an audacious book, even though I got bogged down in the middle. The book, at least in paperback, is marketed as a horror novel, and that is very deceptive. Horrific and terrible things happen in the book, but that is not really the point of the novel. It has also been described as a metaphysical thriller and black comedy which are closer to the mark. The titular character of the novel, Glandier, is a murderer. We know because his wife, Giselle, is dead but conscious, tied to her grave. Giselle's mother, Joy-Ann, has terminal cancer, and she does not suspect that Glandier murdered her daughter. Indeed, he has helped her to cope with her cancer, and she never suspects that he is a monster. The novel takes off when Giselle is finally free of her grave, and Joy-Ann has died and gone to Paradise, a halfway stop to Heaven where spirits can still return to Earth to deal with issues. Ultimately, Glandier needs to get what is coming to him, but it takes the aid of several spirits and demon channeled through the ghost of Glandier's and Giselle's aborted baby. Despite the blurbs on the paperback, this never reminded me of King, Straub or similar writers of blockbuster horror novels. It did remind me of Philip K Dick (Counter Clock World), Rudy Rucker (White Light), Disch (Echo Round His Bones) and Beetlejuice! Of course, it is indisputably a Disch novel.
