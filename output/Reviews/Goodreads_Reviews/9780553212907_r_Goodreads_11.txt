All the world's a stage, and all the men and women merely players. They have their exits and their entrances, and one man in his time plays many parts... As the quotation implies, Shakespeare's As You Like It is a comedy of life. It demonstrates how human vices and virtues contribute towards social drama. Consequences of right of primogeniture set the stage for the play. A younger brother usurps a dukedom, banishing the rightful heir while an elder brother, breaking the promise to his late father, acts tyrant to the younger brother. Their actions unleash a chain of events. The character response to these events forms the main part of the story. In addition, themes of love and marriage serve as pleasant fill-ins. The play is not the best of Shakespearean comedies. The story is ridiculous in parts and the flow is incoherent. But, the unmistakable wit and humour of Shakespeare compensate somewhat for what is lacking. Some of the intelligent conversations provide much food for thought. The play consists of some interesting characters. Rosalind is the most interesting because in the play, she plays the part of both woman and man, exhibiting strength and vulnerability. Celia is equally likable. Her unwavering loyalty to Rosalind is admirable. Both are resilient women that joined Shakespeare's list of heroines. Orlando is also likeable. His goodness coupled with Touchstone's wit and humour positively contribute towards the play. The play had enough comedy to entertain me. I only wish the story was done better.
