Trigger warnings: alcohol abuse, suicide, overt racism, racial slurs, Stolen Generations, death of a parent, violence towards Indigenous people, systemic racism, war, animal death. I read Morgan's original work a good 25 years or so ago when I was in early secondary school. I remembered essentially nothing about it, other than it was the first time I'd ever read a book about an Aboriginal Australian written by an Aboriginal Australian. So I'm thrilled that there's a young readers edition of this available now to introduce Morgan's classic text to a new generation. That being said, this deals with a lot of very dark topics, so take the "young" part of young readers with a grain of salt - the publisher's website recommends Year 5 to Year 9, but I'd say it's more like Year 7+. It's equal parts Morgan's own story of growing up not knowing her identity and oral histories of her mother, grandmother and great-uncle, growing up with that identity intact. It's a very emotional book a lot of the time, and a compelling story throughout. I flew through it, and part of me feels like I should go back and read it more slowly to fully appreciate Morgan's writing.
