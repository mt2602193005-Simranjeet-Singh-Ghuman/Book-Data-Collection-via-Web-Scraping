This should have been fascinating but instead ended up being uninteresting and tedious. Two things that Alexander does in contrast to the best historical nonfiction that I've read: 1) Rather than focus on key characters, she covers the entire cast without focus. That is one of the reasons Erik Larson's books work so well, because he delves into great detail on two or three key figures so you care about them and what happens. Instead, what you get here is everyone covered in equal proportion, and when you have an extremely large cast of figures that makes it impossible to care about any of them. 2) She focuses more on diary entries and nautical journals rather than the human emotion of what was happening. This also makes the book feel like an encyclopedia entry rather than a story you should care about. In nonfiction, just as with fiction, we have to actually care about the people we are reading about in order to enjoy the book, so if you're never given that opportunity you just aren't as invested as you otherwise could be.
