I knew there had to be a word for feeling nostalgia for a place and time you've never known, and there is.....anemoia. That's what I felt reading this. Born in 1916, Laurie Lee grew up during the last gasp of his Cotswold village's life, before progress after WWI began to change things quickly. He writes of the old ways poetically and lovingly. He and his 7 siblings grew up without a father, who had abandoned the family for a life in London, but his scatter-brained mother was more than equal to the job of raising them. Plus, it takes a village, yada, yada..... I first read this sometime in the 1980's when Time-Life issued a series of older books under their imprint. It was good enough for me to hang on to, and when I came upon a film one day on Amazon Prime, I watched it and immediately decided to reread my old copy. Both the film and the book were worth my time. The author left his village one day and walked to London, then went to Spain to fight in the Spanish Civil War. He wrote books about both those experiences, plus many more about his later life and travels as well. As soon as they fall into my lap, I'll read those too. This was a beautiful place to start.
