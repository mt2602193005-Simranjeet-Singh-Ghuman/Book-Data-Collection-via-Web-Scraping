I am endlessly intrigued by the many relationships between form and content. This is the second novel in a row where the content doesn’t interest me, but the form does. Here the form is primarily Erickson’s writing, the magical way in which he makes ludicrous stories and characters interesting enough for me to get through, in this case, over a hundred pages before the content became unbearable. It’s a matter of rhythm, repetition, snappy dialogue, and a combination of noir and Kafka that, for the most part, make his dark alternative universe work. If he’d been writing in the 70s, I would have been a devotee.
