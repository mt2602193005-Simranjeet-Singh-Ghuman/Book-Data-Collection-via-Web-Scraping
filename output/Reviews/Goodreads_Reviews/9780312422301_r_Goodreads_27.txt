“Just don’t let up or you’ll get dragged beneath the wheel,” warns Hans’s teacher. This is one of Herman Hesse’s earliest novels published 15-20 years before his great novels, Damien, Siddhartha, Steppenwolf, and Narcissus and Goldmund. I practically inhaled them at a young age in the late 1960’s when they were all the rage. Beneath the Wheel doesn’t begin to compare to the complexity and depth of his later novels. It’s the coming of age story of Hans, a young teenaged boy living in a small town in Germany, who is recognized by his school teachers and the local pastor as the only boy in town with academic promise. Once set on the path of the local “wunderkind”, he is severed from his childhood and programmed for academic success. At his local school and later at the academy to which he is accepted, he is weighed down with social pressure, high expectations and an immense amount of work, all much too burdensome for his weak body and even weaker disposition. Whenever faced with any type of social pressure, Hans will always choose the path of least resistance and succumb to it, even when it changes his circumscribed path and subverts the academic future he seemingly craves. He doesn’t seem to possess any form of agency. Hans is only ever happy in Nature and when he returns to his town, an academic failure, that is where he will find respite. No longer of any interest to anyone, a disappointment to the adults and the brunt of ridicule from his childhood peers, “his betrayed and violated childhood erupt[s] like a long pent up stream” with dire consequences. Despite his teacher’s warning, Hans is in effect dragged beneath the wheel. It’s a very didactic novel, and certainly not a great one.
