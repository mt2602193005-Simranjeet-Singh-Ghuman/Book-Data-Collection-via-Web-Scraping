Quite baffled with some of the high praises this book has received. I personally found it just continuing the mediocrity of the first trilogy. A lot of plot points are introduced, even when plot points from the previous trilogy remain unresolved. The most glaring of which is the demon - kinda felt baited by that as nothing more than an excuse to write another trilogy. I guess the next book will bring the demon back? I found just 1 thing commendable with the book - and that's the portrayal of how an incurable plague takes over a nation. It's this unbeatable disease that paralyses the human world but two things cheapen it - there's no real loss (aside from 2 side named characters and commoners) and the massive nationwide pilgrimage for salvation (which is a way too clean-cut and unrealistic way to resolve a plague). It's a recurring theme in this book - a problem, something happens, and then it's closed. Distrustful northern neighbours, have a fight, have a trial, and brotherhood. Staged pawri escape, political confrontation, book closed. An entirely new group of evil elves and walking dead, but nothing happens (why not just leave it to the next book?). Demon and a new child ranger, but nothing happens. Friends hurting relationships with each other, but all is forgotten. Catch up with a girl and fall in love immediately, plot device for the protag to drag her around, but nobody cared about her presence. Brothers Repentant brutality, nothing happened after. De Unnero defeated, but very unsatisfyingly done. Pony is basically turning into Elbryan with all her monologues. And now she ventures into politics.
