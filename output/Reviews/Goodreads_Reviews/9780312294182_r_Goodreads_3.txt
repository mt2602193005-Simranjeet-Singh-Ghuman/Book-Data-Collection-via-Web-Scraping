A very dense coverage of the Troubles and interesting to consider from Coogan’s point of view as a Nationalist and someone obviously friendly with and admiring of key figures like Gerry Adams, but I found it really difficult to follow and read past Coogan’s biases after a few chapters, especially in the light of other more recent accounts such as Patrick Radden Keefe’s “Say Nothing”, which was much more sympathetically written with the human costs of the conflict in mind and especially eye-opening about the aforementioned Adams and the stories told about and within the IRA since the ‘90s peace process.
