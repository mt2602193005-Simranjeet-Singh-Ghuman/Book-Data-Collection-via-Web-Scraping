I must hedge here a small amount, as I can't quite commit to saying that it was "amazing", but it most certainly was one of the most interesting and engrossing books on food I have read. So we can say, 4.5 stars, eh? Broken down into three separate sections, examining the life and styles of three different modern, American Chefs, all of whom are successful and in love with what they have chosen to do, Ruhlman explores what makes each man (in this case) get up in the morning, every morning, and prepare food for others. I will not pretend that this is a book for everyone. To really understand some of the mindsets, passions, and mental hurdles that happen in a professional kitchen, you have to have worked in a professional kitchen, and so while Ruhlman may have been hoping to write for a larger audience in general, it is to a more specific, narrow wedge of the population who can get down in there, into the grease stuck between the six-top and low-boy, and actually feel what Ruhlman is saying. That is not to say that you won't enjoy this book if you haven't spent time behind the line, because Ruhlman is the kind of writer that will take you there, but I think that those who have will find that Ruhlman can take them back to those apprenticeship days, to those stock-straining,fine-dicing, non-stop-ticket-churning moments when you have ask yourself if this is REALLY what you wanted to do. I enjoyed the way Ruhlman writes - he loves food, he gets food, he gets cooking food. He even got me dreaming of whipping up emulsions, and salivating for the French Laundry.
