Teen suicide, teen sexuality, teen angst, curiousness, atheism, questioning, coming of age…all under the Victorian evangelical eye? Frank Wedekind is the man! Hahaha. I finally came around to reading, “Spring Awakening,” which one of my favorite musicals is based on. I knew going into it the musical took its own interpretation of the texts, which I think was necessary in order to relate to the modern generation that it was written for. However, I question why the topic of rape was left out in the musical. Maybe for the seriousness of it all, but if it was included it would have provided more depth and conversation around the characters. We look at Wendla and Melchoir as a romance in the musical, when in reality Wendla suffers because of Melchoir’s actions. I think when one reads the original text of the play you have to read it as a complete opposite of the musical and to not expect a similar story. It’s a tragic-comedy of its time and not a morally driven play. The play and the musical are different, and portray differently, but I think that is okay in the end. The comedic innocence in the play is not really found in the musical, and I appreciate the levity that it brings. I have high regards for both works of art, and love the stories that each portray individually. I highly recommend reading the play if you’ve seen the musical to fully appreciate the words and the characters better.
