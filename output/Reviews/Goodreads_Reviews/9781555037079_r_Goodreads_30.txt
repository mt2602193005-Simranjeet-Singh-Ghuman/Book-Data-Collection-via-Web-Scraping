In this true, first-person narrative, Kitty De Ruyter relates her experiences of being captured and detained in a Japanese concentration camp during World War II. Reminiscent of Hillenbrand’s Unbroken (but published many years earlier), it contrasts the heart-breaking torture she and her mother endured with the amazing account of the forgiveness she was able to show for her captors at the end of the war. The things that allowed her to endure the excruciating years of imprisonment—her foundational understanding of the Bible, her great faith in the Lord, and the impact of her mother’s teachings and example—were the same things which enabled her to forgive. As brutal as Unbroken’s Louis Zamperini’s experiences were to read (and to watch in the movie), there are scenes in this story that were even more horrific because they were told from the first-person point of view. I read the book and also listened to it on audiobook and was profoundly moved.
