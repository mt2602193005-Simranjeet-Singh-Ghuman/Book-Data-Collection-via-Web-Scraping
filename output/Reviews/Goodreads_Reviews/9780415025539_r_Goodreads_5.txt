In “Learning from the Patient” Casement provides a masterclass in the craft of interpretation of meaning by therapists. One of the trickiest parts of therapy work…and one of the least taught. I wouldn’t read this book if I wasn’t working as a therapist. The author spends far too much time focusing on the Kleinian obsession with biological expressions of the unconscious finding doubtful meaning in bowel movements, urination, breasts, and the promptness of his patients. It’s as if the patient’s entire world revolves around the therapist and everything they do, say, dream, or experience as a bodily function is influenced by the therapist. The author’s conception of therapy represents an absurdly obsolete male dominated past where the therapist acted as an authority on everything patient related. Hopefully another author will pick up Casement’s work and place it in a more contemporary, less absurd context so that the value of his approach can be fully realized.
