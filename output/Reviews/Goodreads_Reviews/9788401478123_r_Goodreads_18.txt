I'm less surprised by all the differences between this first book in the series, and later ones, when I find that this book was written 53 years ago, and the last , Midnight Fugue (#24), came out in 2009. Between the two the characters change, somewhat but not completely, and to my mind believably, and so do their relationships. The first novel in the series that I read was "The Wood Beyond" (#14 from 1995), and I have to admit I've been hooked- I suppose the time structure, music references, the introduction in that novel of the activist who became one character's longterm love interest, etc., didn't hurt that. Judging from the reviews of this novel and having just finished reading it - I tend to agree that if I had begun with it, I might not have been quite so compelled to continue. Enjoyed it alot knowing what's to come, but if I'd thought the series would be more like this, and that Dalziel and Pascoe would remain like this in themselves and in their relation to each other- if I had thought that, I'd probably have done a dnf as I do for other series. The writing quality's quite good here as for later instalments but not quite as good; maybe it doesn't -quite- deserve the 5 stars I gave, at least in itself. Hrm. ... Well. So don't begin with this one- you might enjoy one of the later ones better, and then come back to this. Even #2, which introduces one of the most important other main characters of the entire series (and another very important secondary character who shows up time and time again, though not as often..!) - is worth reading earlier, maybe first...
