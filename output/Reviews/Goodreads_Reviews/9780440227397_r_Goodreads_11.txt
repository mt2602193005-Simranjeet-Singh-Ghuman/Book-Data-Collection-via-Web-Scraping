Fox’s novel follows a boy named Jessie Oliver who lives with his mother and sister in tough conditions in 1840 New Orleans. He is later suddenly kidnapped and taken aboard a slave ship that is traveling to Africa to capture potential slaves. While onboard, he must deal with the ship’s dreadful living conditions and the frightening shipmen who mistreat him as he will be a fife-playing dancer for the slaves. When the journey continues later with slaves onboard, Jessie will witness just how heinous they are treated while the shipmen will also become more threatening. “The Slave Dancer” is a very harsh and challenging story that takes place during a very unsettling time in US history. Jessie’s story is one that gets emotionally riveting when he’s out on sea against his will and we see the unimaginable that he must see before and after slaves are captured and the way they’re treated. It also never try to tone down just how it felt in real life for those who were treated like that back then. It thankfully by the end does give Jessie and another character a ray of hope and survival. It definitely is more suited for older middle-grade children. It’s a powerful and bittersweet story that gives us a look at the past and it never skips a beat. A- (91%/Excellent)
