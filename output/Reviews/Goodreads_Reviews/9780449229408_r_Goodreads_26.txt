This is the 21st Georgette Heyer book for me. I've thoroughly enjoyed every one, though I've observed there's often a similarity in her heroes. All are wealthy and intelligent, and many of them are also bored, spoiled gentlemen, showing an almost misanthropic disdain for most everyone, especially their relations - who, admittedly, usually exhibit varying degrees of avarice and/or foolishness. Not so here. It's my new favorite Heyer. The title character, Sir Waldo Hawkridge, may be excessively rich and smart, but he's unfailingly patient and polite with everyone - including those who don't deserve it. Moreover, he's a philanthropist. When he inherits an estate unexpectedly (one that was coveted by the rest of his extended family), he travels to see it with the intention of turning it into an orphanage. He brings along his younger cousin, Lord Julian Lindeth, who's quickly smitten by the neighborhood beauty, seventeen-year-old Tiffany Wield. Along with her ethereal countenance, Tiffany is blessed with an obscenely large dowry. Unfortunately, her angelic appearance masks a volatile, self-centered personality, and only her companion, the admirable Miss Ancilla Trent, is capable of preventing Tiffany's childish temper tantrums. The storyline demonstrates the usual Heyer brilliance, with both Sir Waldo and Lord Lindeth the prey for marriage-minded matrons and their daughters. There's a full array of well-drawn characters. The plot builds, developing the relationship between Sir Waldo and Miss Trent and simultaneously showing Tiffany exposing more and more of her true personality when things don't go in her favor. Wonderful writing, characters, and plot. Immensely satisfying read!
