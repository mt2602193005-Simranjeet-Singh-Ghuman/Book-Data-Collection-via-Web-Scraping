2023 Reread You know, this one is *almost* better than Squire. Something that the laurel of 2002 would never have said, because oh how she hated this book as a teenager and refused to reread it for 14 years. 2016 Reread Another one of those books where time makes a difference. When I read this book when it was published, I was young, military-mad and saw the world in black and white. I hated that Kel was assigned to a refugee camp. I saw it as Wyldon keeping her safe and behind lines and out of the fight. I wanted war and fighting and battles and heroism and going after the Nothing Man, not digging latrines and whiny refugees. And I thought that Kel became something high-handed and lordly, something different than in the previous three books. I hated this book and cursed Tamora Pierce's name when I finished it. For the record, Squire remains one of my favorite books of all time. Fast forward 14 years. I read the first three to my wife, who loved them. Then I started this and stopped. Just couldn't go on. Until a couple months later my professor assigned a Margaret Edwards book for class, and I looked up the lists, saw that this one worked, and said, "Well, let's rip that band-aid off and read it again. It's time." It was time. This book is wonderful. Kel has changed, but that is because she has grown up . She remains true to herself, and becomes a great commander. And of course, the supporting characters are lovely. Tobe is a fantastic addition to the crew. Tamora Pierce, you are amazing. Although, I still don't think I can forgive you for Mastiff . Perhaps in another 10 years I can reread that one and find peace
