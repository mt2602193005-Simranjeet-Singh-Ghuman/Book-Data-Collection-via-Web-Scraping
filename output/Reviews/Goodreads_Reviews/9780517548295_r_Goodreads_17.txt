The narrator, Dr Jean-Marc Montjean is 45 as he speaks from August of 1938 in the Basque town of Salies-les-Bains, recalling his summer of 1914, just before the Great War started. As a recently qualified medic, he is taken on as an assistant in the town clinic, where he specializes in the discomforts associated with menopause . A young woman turns up at the clinic to request help for her twin brother who has fallen and hurt his shoulder, Montjean falls for her. The mystery element of the novel soon imprints itself; why is the young woman’s family concealing themselves in a far flung corner of France, and why are the brother and father so determined Montjean shouldn’t spend time with Katya. There is a lot to enjoy here, life is the small town, and a very compelling mystery. But the highlight for me is that the horse and cart trip the family and Montjean take to the village of Anos, up in the Pyrenees, where the Festival of the Drowned Virgin is celebrated. I believe the bridge, from which the virgin was tossed, is still in existence today.
