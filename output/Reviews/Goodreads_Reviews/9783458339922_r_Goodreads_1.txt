One of her later novels and now sadly neglected, it would be nice to see this one being brought back into print. This starts off with a house party at Daisy's (Lady Midhurst) country residence. It's here that rumours first start about her daughter Terry's affair with a married friend of hers. She doesn't think this could possibly be true until it's confirmed by Terry, where upon she flees to her Jasmin Farm in France. She is soon followed by Mumsie, the vulgar, social-climbing mother of the cheated on wife, hoping to propel herself into society and get whatever she can out of the situation. She is hugely disappointed to find no glamorous party on the Rivera, but a grieving Daisy in a simple farm house. The first half, with the house party, went on for too lond and had too many superfluous characters which seemed to add nothing to the plot, (one was a ridiculous German Count, so I wonder if he was put in to express her anti-German views; the novel was written in 1934, just after Hitler came to power). Apart from Terry, none of the characters were likeable. Daisy had such a horror of sin because her husband kept cheating on her and yet both she and Mrs Leigh were described as cold. Neither were loving wives, and Daisy, on abandoning her daughter to gossip muses that "Sin was sin, she passionately assured herself, and of all the sins most hideous was the one that masqueraded as love. Her whole life had been spent trying to save people from it". Mumsie comes across as greedy, grasping and vulgar. However, I did find the second half very funny when these two got together, in a rather farcical way and found it interesting that the only sympathetic character was the 'husband-snatcher'. Elizabeth herself led a fairly complicated life.
