I used to play chess as a kid but stopped for nearly 30 years. Now my son is obsessed with the game, so I picked this up to get back into it and help him improve. This book is pretty basic, but Seirawan explains everything really well. He breaks down the fundamentals into force, time, space, and pawn structure. Each concept is clear and practical - you learn why trades matter, how to move pieces efficiently, how to control the board, and why pawns are more important than beginners realize. What I liked most is that Seirawan doesn't baby you. He teaches algebraic notation right from the start and expects you to use it, which is the right approach if you actually want to get better at chess. The book ends with annotated games where he explains each move using the concepts from earlier chapters. This helps you see how everything connects in real games. The writing has personality and some humor, which makes it easier to get through than most instructional books. If you're learning chess or getting back into it after years away, this is a solid place to start. It gives you a real foundation to build on instead of just random tips and tricks.
