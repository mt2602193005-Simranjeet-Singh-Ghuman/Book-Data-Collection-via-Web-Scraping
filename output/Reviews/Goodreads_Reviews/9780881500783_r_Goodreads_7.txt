This is the fifth Asey Mayo mystery. It was published in 1938. I read a handsome 2005 reissue by The Countrymen Press. The back cover has this description from Marilyn Stasio, the longtime mystery book critic for the The New York Times. "The Codfish Sherlock, Asey Mayo, a salty Cape codger who entertains summer visitors by solving picturesque crimes.". That's pretty good. A rich Cape Cod homeowner rents his mansion to an exotic beautiful blond who goes around dressed in silky robes. She has a troop of green uniformed guards and butlers and a stern housekeeper. It stirs up gossip. The rich guy's dead wife, who hasn't been seen on the Cape for years, gets strangled in the guest cottage. Her two adult children are suspects. Taylor is very good a throwing in fun stuff. There is a conman who is trying to find his elephant. There are three young boys, The Pilgrim Camera Club" who consider themselves to be junior newshounds. We get a couple of good car chases, some bulldozer work and some spy stuff. There is, for the first time in this series, a sense that dangerous things are happenings in Europe that America may be getting involved in. All of this stuff is wrapped up successfully. This is a solid addition to the series. One Pet Peeve. A doctor and the housekeeper and Asey discover a dead body. Taylor says, "For several minutes Warner and Asey and Mrs. Monfort stood and stared at the limp figure in the stained and bedraggled brocade pyjamas." I don't mind "pyjamas". That was an accepted variant in 1938. I do mind the idea that three people could discover a dead body and could stand and stare "for several minutes." That is an eternity if you just discovered a body. Despite what horror stories and mystery stories claim, people never stare speechlessly at any surprising thing "for several minutes."
