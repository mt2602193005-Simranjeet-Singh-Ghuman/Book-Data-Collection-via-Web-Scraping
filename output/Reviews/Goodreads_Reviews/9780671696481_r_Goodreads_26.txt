The typical Dick Francis hero this time is an artist who paints horses. He's drawn into an Antipodean crime investigation after his cousin's wife is brutally murdered when she interrupts burglars in her home. I was expecting the cousin to be a horse trainer or something similar, but no, the connection to racing in this book is almost non-existent. As a painter of horses, the MC occasionally goes to the races as a spectator, but that's all. He's in Melbourne on the day of the Melbourne Cup, but he's using it as a distraction for his investigations and only listens to the race on the radio. There's nothing wrong with this, but I like Dick Francis because of the horse-racing angle, so I was a little disappointed. Otherwise it's serviceable 1970s crime thriller, and I did appreciate the preface where the author explains how the idea for this book and its artist characters came to him.
