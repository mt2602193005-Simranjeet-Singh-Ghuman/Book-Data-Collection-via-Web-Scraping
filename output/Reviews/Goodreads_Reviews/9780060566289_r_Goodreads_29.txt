I'd read a few Edward P. Jones stories before, for various classes, but this was the first full book I read of his. 14 stories in all, they take place in D.C., and the stories work sort of chronologically, from the youngest character in the book to the oldest character in the book. Some characters even reappear, and all do in his most recent story collection, All Aunt Hagar's Children. Jones is certainly best known for his novel, The Known World, and All Aunt Hagar's Children, but I personally found Lost in the City to be exceptional. In the way only he can, Jones crushes what feels like full novels into 20 and 10 page stories. If I had to pick a favorite, it would be The Store, but if you want just a small glimpse of his masterful writing, check out The First Day, which I believe is available online and is only 5 pages. The opening line, "...long before I learned to be ashamed of my mother," is one of my favorites. The way he begins and ends each story is unique but so well done, it feels like he knows a secret that short story writers have been chasing after for eternity. A wonderful work, and I can't wait to read more.
