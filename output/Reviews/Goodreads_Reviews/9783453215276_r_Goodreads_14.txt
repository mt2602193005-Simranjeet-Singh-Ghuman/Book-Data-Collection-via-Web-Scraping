Dying Inside is a very good, very literary novel about aging. Many people say it's his best, though I prefer The Book of Skulls. It's couched as science fiction because it's about a man losing his telepathic ability and it's set in the near-future world of 1976, but you could lose the speculative trappings and still have the same essential story about a man trying to come to terms with becoming older. There are some very amusing bits, especially his discussions with his therapist, and there are some sexist and racist bits, which are probably pretty accurate depictions of the thoughts of an average man of that age and station in 1976. It's very good, very interesting, but probably not the kind of thing you'd ever want to re-read unless it was to study Silverberg's craftsmanship. Dying Inside was serialized in Galaxy magazine July-October of 1972, released in hardback by Scribner's later that year, and then in paperback by Ballantine (before it became Del Rey) the next year as part of a matched set of Silverberg titles that all had really ugly Philip Kirkland covers. It was on the final Hugo and Nebula awards ballots for best novel of the year, but lost to fan favorite The Gods Themselves by Isaac Asimov, who hadn't had a new sf novel out for many years. Silverberg's The Book of Skulls was also on the same ballots, and I suspect that split the votes.
