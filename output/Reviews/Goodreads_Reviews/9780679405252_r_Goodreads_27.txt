In this early Barnes novel, three conflicting first-person accounts combine to tell the wry, bittersweet story of how one marriage fell apart and another began. The epigraph is a Russian saying: "He lies like an eye-witness." This sardonic dictum establishes that the first-person statements that follow will be unreliable, no matter what the speakers aver. True to the title, Barnes emphasizes the (faux) oral nature of his text. The reader takes the place of a priest or therapist, hearing verbal confessions. This is no simple love triangle; rather, Barnes delivers a masterclass in creating voices and interrogating unreliable perspectives. (More of a 4.5 , really.) See my full review at BookBrowse .
