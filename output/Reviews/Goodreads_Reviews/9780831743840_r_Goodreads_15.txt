I went into Dragon Tears expecting at least one character to grab me, because that’s usually a given with Dean Koontz, but this ended up being the first time that never happened. The setup is there on paper: a police detective, a bizarre countdown, a strange and menacing antagonist, and a story that blends a procedural feel with supernatural elements. Stuff happens constantly, the threat keeps escalating, and yet none of it ever really hooked me. I kept waiting for that moment where I’d feel invested in someone, anyone, and it just never came. The characters felt more like pieces being moved through the plot than people I cared about, and the whole thing ended up feeling oddly hollow. The best way I can describe it is that reading this book felt like eating off-brand marshmallows. It looks right, it’s technically edible, but it’s missing whatever makes the real thing good. I didn’t hate it, and it’s not badly written, but it felt like a throwaway Koontz novel, something to fill space rather than leave a mark. I finished it more out of habit and respect for the author than actual interest, and when I closed the book I felt nothing at all, which is not what I come to Koontz for. For me, that lands Dragon Tears at a firm 2 out of 5 stars.
