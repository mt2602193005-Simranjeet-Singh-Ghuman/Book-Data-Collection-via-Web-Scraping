Initially disappointing, ultimately mind-blowing. Quite the trajectory for three short works that can't quite be called novels (tho apparently they are lol). Unamuno's style is blunt, devoid of any extraneous details or information; the interactions between characters & the choices that define their lives are in the spotlight here, the result being brief & somewhat merciless portraits of relationships & machinations that highlight overpoweringly the unnerving, unrelenting, & perhaps sinister power of women - at least in the first two. It makes for an intense, claustrophobic experience, but one that breaks thru to a spectacular & revelatory realm with the final piece. Ironically, this story (Nothing Less Than A Man) is probably the most brutal & tragic, but for me it raised the collection to that lofty level of pure art. Unamuno's creation Alejandro Gomez is one of the most striking & memorable characters I've ever encountered, & his overwhelming presence & the forceful proclamations he makes in even casual conversation are a constant source of surprise, wonder & joy. I often found myself cackling at his audacious & unmovable simplicity, & the vividness with which the author conjures his whole being with such short strokes makes his final fate that much more dramatic & unexpected. The introduction by Angel delRio, which I read after finishing the book, puts into sharp focus the full scope of Unamuno's brilliant achievement.
