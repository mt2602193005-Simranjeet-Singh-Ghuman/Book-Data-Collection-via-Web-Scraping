Jeannie is the recipient of a lamp that, of course, contains a genie who will do anything for her. Unfortunately, everything she wishes for manifests in a way that makes things way worse for her, not better. What good is unlimited magic if it always seems to turn on you? I would have given this more stars if the book hadn't consistently felt like a setup to tell a joke. First of all, the genie's "humor" often rests on kids getting a reference that wasn't current even at the time, and secondly, Jeannie always seems to phrase her wishes in such a way that they are guaranteed to backfire. The whole thing made me incredibly uncomfortable, because she clearly wasn't enjoying the way the magic affected her life, but every chapter banged along putting her in impossible situations as if we, the reader, were going to enjoy seeing her suffer. When a contrived reason popped up for why everything went so poorly for her, I wasn't very surprised. I don't like reading books that feel so much like they're dedicated to a relationship between the narration and the reader, leaving the character out of the loop.
