I think 'The Red and the Black' will always be read and admired for two reasons - the complex personality of Julien Sorel and the author's attitude to his characters. A carpenter's son, a good-looking, intelligent and ambitous Napoleon aficionado, a future priest (or a military officer if he is lucky), a social climber, a charmer, a pragmatical and hypocritical manipulator, he would have gone to high places and achieved a lot. Unfortunately, the other part his personality makes him a truly tragic figure. 'The Red and the Black' is an atheist novel and Julien, its protagonist, is also an atheist in spite of the fact that he is just 19. At the same time he is romantic and passionate, far too passionate and impulsive for a future priest. Often his passions control him and eventually lead to a disaster. Also his immense pridefulness makes him vulnerable to constant feeling of inferiority. He is a priori suspicious about everything he sees and hears, he is resentful of the unjust class society where he is determined to make a career. Julien is full of contradictions, he is torn between his pursuit of power and pursuit of love. Love is extremely important to him as he has never been loved as a child. The description of Julien's relationships with Madame de Rênal and Mathilde de la Mole and his ever-changing emotional state are worth mentioning, although I sometimes hated him for selfishness in this respect. So, Julien is full of contradictions but that's what makes him unforgettable and that's what makes him a criminal and a victim in the long run. The author's tone is ironic throughout the novel (it is a satire of the French society of the 1820-s), sometimes adds an air of a comedy or a vaudeville to it and this prevents the reader from perceiving the novel as a crime story and a tragedy.
