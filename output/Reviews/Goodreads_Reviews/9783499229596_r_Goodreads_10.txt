4.5 stars rounded up to 5 Polismördaren (eng: Cop Killer, dt: Polizistenmörder) is the excellent 9th instalment of the Martin Beck series (1960s-70s) and would have been my vote for the best one of the series...until it fell apart somewhat at the end. The title for Best Beck stays with Den Skrattende Polisen (eng: The Laughing Policeman) and this one gets silver. What a load of fun this mystery is for Beck fans! Not only are characters from previous mysteries brought back in surprisingly inventive ways, but we are also treated to seeing where Beck's original team have ended up 9 years after the first book. The writing is superb, of course, and even better in the original Swedish. The mystery is very well done...until the last 30-odd pages, where the authors painted themselves a little bit into a corner and had to sprinkle some "lucky hap" fairy dust to get themselves out again. (Forgivable to a point.) One thing to note: Over the course of the series, the plots and characters become more and more political, as the authors used the series as a way to express their anger and dissatisfaction with the state of Sweden and the Swedish authorities at the time. At book 9, the ranting is fairly loud. In some ways, that makes the later books stronger, more interesting and the plots more compelling. In other ways it makes them one-sided and at times, they contain characters that are not real characters, but vehicles to demonstrate a point or act as a mouthpiece for the authors' political convictions. Today, many of these rants read like world-building, the painting of how the world of the characters works, and not as lava-hot rage at a reality the authors themselves were living in. Given that, this book can simply be read for the story, but also as a historical snapshot of a time and place that was suffering from very similar problems we still are today, some 50 years later.
