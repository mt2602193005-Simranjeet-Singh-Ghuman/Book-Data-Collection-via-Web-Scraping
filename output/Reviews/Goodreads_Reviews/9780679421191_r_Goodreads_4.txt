Davis is a journalist who made his name with “Hammer Of The Gods” about life on the road with Led Zeppelin back in the day as they say to borrow a phrase that was old and tired before it’s time, HOTG is considered one of the notorious day-in-the-life of a rock ’n roll band at the top of its game. This is the same band BTW made notorious for the “Mud Shark Incident that Frank Zappa parodied on the “The Mothers of Invention Live at the Fillmore” from 1971 but I digress. In “Jajouka” Davis recounts multiple trips to the village in Morocco where the famous musicians from the Ahl Sherif tribe reside. The writing is weak but it tells an interesting story of the collision of various members of the beat generation (Brion Gysin, William Burroughs and other expats including Paul Bowles best known for his phenomenal novel “The Sheltering Sky” about which Bertolucci made a movie of the same name. In the late 60’s shortly before his death Rolling Stones founder Brian Jones made a trip to Morocco and was introduced to musicians of Jajouka by Brion Gysin hence the title of this book “Jajouka Rolling Stone”. Jones clearly made an impression on the musicians he met with as they created a song commemorating his trip and kept a poster with his image in the village Madrasah even up to the time of Davis’ trips into the 90’s. Jones for his part even produced a record from a 1968 performance during his visit (“Brian Jones Presents the Pipes of Pan At Jajouka”) in the interest of getting thiem attention outside of their narrow north African world and the album was re-released in 1995. The book is essentially a recap of several trip s that Davis took the village that involves a lot of internecine details about rivalries among the musicians that breaks down between the older more traditional players and the younger performers who number fewer each year as western influences call and military service intervenes. It is the encounters with Gyson and Bowles which are few that make this book worth reading. Show more
