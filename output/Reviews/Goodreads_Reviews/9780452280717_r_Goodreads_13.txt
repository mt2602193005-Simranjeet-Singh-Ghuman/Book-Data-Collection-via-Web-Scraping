This book has an amazing story, which happens to be true, making it even more amazing. The author is a Holocaust survivor, and she recounts her youth in Eastern Europe, the Hungarian march, time at a concentration camp, and then in the camps afterwards waiting to head to America. Truly blows my mind that this was only two generations ago. The author's love for her mother and father, and their fierce, undying parental love is humbling. When you factor in the amazing love story, and her reunion with her "one true love" some 30 years after the Holocaust at a chance meeting in a Budapest hotel is truly incredible. I highly recommend this book; parts of it aren't easy to read, but that makes it even more important that we do so.
