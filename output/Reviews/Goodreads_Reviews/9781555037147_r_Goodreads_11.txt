This is the first Anita Stansfield novel I have read. I doubt I will read another. I was really in the mood for an escape romance novel, which is rare for me, so the story-line proved enough to keep me turning pages. But the writing wasn't great. Certain words and phrases were overly used ("tersely," "poignant," "touched his/her face," etc.). Some of the dialogue was corny. It seemed like the author couldn't think of anything else for the main character to do other than laundry--constantly! She had three children. I have ten children, and even I don't do THAT much laundry. I didn't find the main character's personality completely likeable, in the beginning she was sort of a bland doormat, in the end she was a bland and proud idiot--though less of a doormat. Her children were present as a job and a responsibility, but there was no emphasis on her relationships with or her love for her children--they were just inconvenient obstacles to her romantic pursuit. Furthermore, that the main character had a neighbor/best friend who was ALWAYS available at any given moment to babysit, thus providing all of the time the main character needed to develop her romance, seemed too convenient, like a lazy and unrealistic solution on the author's part to the needed plot scenarios. The writing was all devoted to the plot, zero to the setting, so it lacked richness. To me, this book read more like a rough draft--a great plot with potential, but lacking true depth, richness, believability, and even basic editing improvements. The book felt like a weird experience, sort of like a food that tastes alright, but doesn't turn out to be what you were craving.
