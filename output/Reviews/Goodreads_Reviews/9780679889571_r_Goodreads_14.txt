Here's a book that hit me just at the right time. As we consider moving, I'm well aware that we have TOO MUCH STUFF. This is something that will likely strike a chord with many big bears and a few little ones as well. The book is told in a way that's maybe a little preachy, especially with the phrase "those in need" because it gives the idea that people who have little want your junk. I don't say that to be harsh, but because this happened to me. When the children were young, we lived in the country. My then husband was a pastor, and we had very very little money. So what happened is well-meaning people would take stuff they didn't need anymore and drop it off at the parsonage for us to use. The problem came when word got out, so then whenever there was a rummage sale or someone died, people would then bring EVERYTHING that hadn't sold, and leave it on our porch when we weren't home. During the summer this became hundred of pounds of wanted stuff - most of which was broken, stained, or not usable. I really really wish that this book had addressed a simple point - NO ONE WANTS YOUR TRASH. If it's broken or stained, poor people don't want another fixer upper. Please just throw away stuff like this. Especially puzzles that don't have all the pieces, or games that are missing parts. Really, it's not useful. ASK before bringing stuff. Hospitals frequently don't want used toys. And most toy drives don't want them either. DO take things to thrift stores and organizations like Goodwill. This is a great book to show a child the importance of culling out stuff. But it needs to be updated on what to do with that stuff. I love how they spend time with the people at the nursing home. I love how generous they are. This is a good book. But when you read it with your child, take it to the next level. Sort your stuff, and find it a new home (responsibly).
