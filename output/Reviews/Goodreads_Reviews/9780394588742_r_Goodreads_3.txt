This is incredibly, overwhelmingly sentimental about motherhood, family, and the rural past, so if that is not your thing, steer clear! But I enjoy a dive into sentimentality at this time of year, so I found it charming even though I am well aware that the world she is so nostalgic for is a retroactive imagination rather than a past anyone could have lived through. There are poems (mostly James Whitcomb Riley and Edgar Guest), personal stories, pen-and-ink drawings, and of course recipes -- many of which I am hoping to try, since they are a sort of cooking I grew up with, straightforward Midwestern farm food. She wrote a number of other cookbooks and I am looking forward to trying them out, although I suspect they will be much the same in terms of the sentimental nostlagia.
