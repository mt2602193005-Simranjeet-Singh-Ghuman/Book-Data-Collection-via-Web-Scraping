It starts with a personal ad: Unattached Englishman. Mid Thirties, personable, fluent French, seek interesting and unusual work, preferable in the Aix/Avignon area. Anything considered except marriage. With the placing of this add Luciano Bennett, a down on his luck and down to his last franc ne’re-do-well living in France changes the course of his life. Before long he has a response from the ultra-wealthy Julian Poe. Poe’s proposal is very simple and simply irresistible. Bennett must impersonate Poe in Monaco for six months, leaving enough of a paper trail to convince the French authorities that Poe’s legal residence is in Monaco, not France, so Poe can avoid France’s exorbitant tax structure. It is a simple tax dodge that will allow Bennett to live in the lap of luxury for six months and get paid to do it. But when things look too good to be true they usually are. Before long Bennett finds himself between a rock (Poe and his killer henchman, Shimo) and a hard place (the Mafia). Both want Bennett’s hide or his life or both, whichever is easiest. Along the way Bennett teams up with a beautiful side kick, a former sergeant in the Israeli army, seeks solace from a group of gay corporate ex-pats masquerading as winemaking monks, and learns that the French Police, a Corsican hitman, and the French Army have all joined in the hunt to bring him down. The antics are rollicking, the ride is delightful, the dialogue spins off the page and the wrap-up is neat and tidy and the whole of the cast descend on a tiny Provencal village much to the entertainment of the bored locals. ©2015 M.
