I'm not sure how to rate this book. On one hand, this story was so extremely and unbelievably horrific that it had my emotions all over the place. How do you rate that? I was angry, amazed, sad, horrified, and ashamed. Ashamed, because after learning of the author's constant struggles during and, even emotionally after the war, how dare I ever complain about such trivial things such as.... long lines at the grocery, being without power after a storm, or the numerous other stupid things that we tend to complain about. The story was depressing, yet for some strange reason, I kept reading it. It just left me shaking my head. However, it was the constant and unwavering faith of Betty's mother that stays with me.
