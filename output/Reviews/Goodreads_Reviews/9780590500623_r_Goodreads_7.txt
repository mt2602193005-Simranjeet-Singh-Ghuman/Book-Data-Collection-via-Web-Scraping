Karen and friends deal with an overly competitive bully of a swimming coach. The resolution felt random and way too unrealistic. Plus, while some of the elements may have been justified in this particular situation, there are so many ways the adults in this story could have handled it all better. - Watson and Mr Barkin just happen to drop by that day? - The stuff they said to the coach turned out to be right and good for the kids' welfare, but it could also so easily have been the usual helicopter parent meddling where they shouldn't. I thought the coach folded WAY too quickly. - And the community centre boss immediately fires the coach? AND makes him apologize to the kids? Again, it maybe justified in this occasion, but with how often parents interfere without good reason (think intense soccer parents or a parent pissed their kid isn't given enough spotlight), I think the boss actually should've had a bit more faith in the coach, or given him more time -- heck, SOME time -- to change. Still, a good way to show kids that bullying is not okay, even from an adult, and they should turn to trusted adults if something makes them uncomfortable. I also think the book does really well at showing how Karen's own perception of the coach changes over time; I find that very realistic. I also like how even the new friend who's a fantastic swimmer has her own weakness (scared to dive headfirst), and that even if Karen and that new friend (forgot her name, sorry!) are both stronger swimmers than Terry Barkin (also yay!
