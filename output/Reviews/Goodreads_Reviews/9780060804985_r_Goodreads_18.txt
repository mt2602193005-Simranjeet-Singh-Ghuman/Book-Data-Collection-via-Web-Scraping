The person who wrote the blurb on the back of the book did this book a favour by making it sound as though the book has a plot. The last half or so of the book has a plot. The plot arrived on p. 170, and I wouldn't have recognised it as such, necessarily, if I hadn't read the blurb on the back of the book. Don't read this book if you like plots. Read it if you like P. G. Wodehouse-type descriptions and banter. Thirkell is particularly good at interrupting quotations with thoughts that ironise what the speaker is saying. Read it if you like smiling at pages, or sentences. I do. There is a lot of silliness in this novel, froth. But suddenly & unexpectedly near the end of the book Mrs. Middleton acquires seriousness and dignity, and the book becomes not just empty narrative or characterisation or whimsy calories but something that more deeply addresses the human heart.
