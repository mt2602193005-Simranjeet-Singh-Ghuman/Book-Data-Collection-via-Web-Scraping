Oh, those great Victorian Times, when money and marriage were of utmost importance. Do you marry for love or for money and position? A man with no family money or hope for any could look for an heiress to help him out if he got tired of relying on family and friends. But an unmarried women was shameful and became little more than a servant if she had no income of her own. Do you marry for love and struggle all life through, or opt for a comfortable, respectable existence with a man you don't love? Or not marry at all? That's the question that Trollope explores in this first of the Palliser novels. We also get a look at the social mannerisms and strictures of the time, as well as a peek inside the politics of being a Member of Parliament. (Spoiler alert: politics were just as dirty then as now) As an added bonus, since it's Trollope, we get a riveting story, so addictive that 830 pages just fly by. We get wonderful, unforgettable characters, rascals, wretches, good people and bad. Some people get what's coming to them, others learn, finally, finally, to make the right decisions. We also get Trollope's droll humor, his interjections as the author into the story, and some comic scenes to lessen the tension in parts of the story. And in this series, we get Lady Glencora Palliser and her irrepressible personality, and the promise of much more from her and her husband, Plantagenant. My favorite line, which I'm stealing: "I wouldn't pick her out of the gutter with a pair of tongs!"
