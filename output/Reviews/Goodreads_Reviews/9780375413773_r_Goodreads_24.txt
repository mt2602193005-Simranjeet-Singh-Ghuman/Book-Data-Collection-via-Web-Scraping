Bailyn, a genius on the founding of America, writes five essays over a period of years on various topics related to the Founders. There are two main themes: the founders are "truly creative people" and their ideas are "full of inconsistencies, logical dilemmas, and unresolved problems". I prefer the word "ambiguities" as used in the title to any of these seeming pejoratives to define the complexities with which the founder's wrestled. In a nutshell, the founders are well-read, educated, provincials well-equipped to deal with the problems of independence and self-government that Providence has placed on their door step. Bailyn deals at length with the ambiguities in Jefferson's life and the peculiarities of Franklin, who comes off splendidly and leaves the read wanting more, despite Adam's concerns for Benjamin's moral failings. The essay on The Federalist Papers is a commentary on our current political environment and proves the work still speaks to us today. The final essay covers the influence of the founder's work on nations in Europe and South America as they study and in some cases apply the work of the Americans to their own political aspirations. Well written, well illustrated...an enjoyable read in every way.
