__________________________ In a New York Nanosecond. I like Jack Finney. I was sure had read Time and Again many years ago, probably back in the 1970s. After reading a few pages, I knew for sure I had not. In fact, I had never read any time travel story quite like it. The background for the story is based on some actual speculation by Albert Einstein in which he pontificated that everything in the universe was created in a single nanosecond. You were born then. So was I. So were our parents. Julius Caesar crossed the Rubicon then. The dinosaurs were wiped out by a comet at the same instant. The first stars came into existence. Our sun blew up and wiped out the human race. You are actually stuck in a slice of time known as Plank time, which is a trillionth of a billionth of a second. [It’s not my fault you’re stuck. I’m just the messenger.] According to the story line, people can cross from one time slice to another if they have the knack and practice a little self-hypnosis. ★★★★ It is clear that Jack Finney did a hell of a lot of research on the 1880s, and he seemingly makes use of every iota of what he learned in the book. He describes the streets and buildings of 1882 New York City in great detail. He describes the women’s dresses and the men’s hats. He quotes freely and at great length from period newspapers. He even uses authentic period photos, drawings, and newspaper pages from the 1880s and 90s to illustrate the book, and in some cases says that those images show protagonist/narrator. He goes on about what people eat. In the acknowledgments he says that maybe he did too much research. Yes, he did. The novel is stuffed with it.
