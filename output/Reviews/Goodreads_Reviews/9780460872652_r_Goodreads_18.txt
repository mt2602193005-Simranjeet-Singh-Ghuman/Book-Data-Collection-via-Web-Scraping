This book has ranked in my Top 10 since I read it as a college sophomore. Truly, I thought it was right up there with Augustine's Confessions , The Consolation of Philosophy , Pride and Prejudice , and A Midsummer Night's Dream , and it's a big part of the reason I became a medievalist. I wondered if it would hold up, now that I've read many more sagas, taken an entire seminar on Norse Literature, and married an expert on Old Norse. It absolutely holds up! Egil's Saga has all the virtues of a really lively biography with the added intrigue that it was written 800 years ago. Not only do you get an in-depth look at someone's life (fascinating in any era), but also an incredibly clear window into a society that existed so very long ago. I can't think of any parallel body of literature that presents such a full picture of a time so distant as the sagas do. The narration is at times as dry and humorous as anything: this is true of sagas in general but particularly true of Egil. It's just fun to read and as a whole beautifully constructed. Egil's Saga is literary enough and fine enough to make me feel like I discovered a Jane Austen writing in thirteenth-century Iceland. And because the real Egil was an acclaimed poet, the book also features much of his poetry. I love when a book weaves poetry and prose together (a big part of the reason I love Boethius, Shakespeare, Job, and Jonah). Still in my Top 10!
