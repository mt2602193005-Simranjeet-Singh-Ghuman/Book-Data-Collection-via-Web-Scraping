Silent Invaders, Robert Silverberg’s 1963 publication, is a very Poul Anderson kind of book. First of all, this is a very early effort for the Grandmaster, being published when he was just 28. (Of course he published his first work when he was 19). Secondly, the theme of a secret agent operation that pits two sets of aliens on a futuristic earth would fit perfectly into Anderson’s Polesetonic series. With the telepathic and gestalt theme this is also reminiscent of Theodore Sturgeon’s More Than Human . Not one of his best but pretty good, and like so many gems from the 60s, it is a trim and neat short novel, weighing in at a welterweight 154 pages.
