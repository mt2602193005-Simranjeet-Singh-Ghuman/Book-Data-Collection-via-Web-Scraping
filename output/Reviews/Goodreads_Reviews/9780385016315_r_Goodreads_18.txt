Copleston is well-read and informed on the major philosophical developments of the medieval period. He stitches together the different currents of thought of the period, and how each philosopher developed their views from previous thinkers, and where they did not. This includes the interplay between Ancient Greek philosophy and medieval. I enjoyed the proofs for God’s existence. Although I found none of them convincing, well at least not watertight like a proof should be. Proof of such kind only exists in the realm of mathematics and the formal sciences. Other topics include the nature of god, the soul’s immortality, the question of evil and morals from a Christian perspective, creation from eternity, free will, and many other topics. This book is more or less encyclopaedic in its breadth and deepness. Also included is some philosophy from Avecinna and mamoniades who weren’t Christians but were important thinkers in this period nonetheless. I really enjoyed it but found it exceedingly challenging at the same time. I’d recommend it for someone who wants a good overview of philosophy from this timespan. You don’t really need much philosophical background but it would of course help you make the most of it if you did.
