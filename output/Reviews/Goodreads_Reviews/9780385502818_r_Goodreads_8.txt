Crusader by Michael Eisner is a thoughtful and engaging story that weaves personal reflections with sharp insights into leadership, ambition and the bond between two men. The narrative follows two young Spanish noblemen, Francisco and Andres. Francisco returns home a shattered man after the fall of Krak des Chevaliers. This is his account of what transpired and the role he played. For me, the book’s most compelling moments come when Francisco opens up about his struggles and choices, creating an honest, almost conversational tone about the battle of Krak des Chevaliers. It’s not about achieving success but also about the journey covering risks, mistakes, and perseverance. While some sections can feel more reflective than action-packed, the overall impression is lasting, leaving plenty to ponder, especially for those interested in overcoming human frailty, greed and betrayal.
