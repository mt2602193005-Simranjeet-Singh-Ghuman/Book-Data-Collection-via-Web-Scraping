The motherless Montoya girls' aunt wants them to learn to read. At first they're not sure whether this would even be disloyal to their uneducated mother...but we all know the American Girls stories weren't going to let the anti-literacy side win, although that happened in some real families in 1815. As always in this series, the purpose of the story is to give children information about a period in history, with extensive notes and gorgeous color pictures of the things people used and wore. I liked Josefina better than the other American Girls because her stories always came out with a Spanish edition for parallel reading. All the Girls are very nice characters who have nice learning experiences about things that were going on at their period in history, though. My sisters, in grades six and seven, thought this series was childish when it came out; it's really ideal for grades two through four, but some sixth grade students still seem to like it.
