This was a disappointment. The amazing story behind The Bounty, her captain, her mutineers and their varied fates got lost in Alexander's retelling. In her efforts to stick closely to the available journals, news articles and letters written at the time, the excitement and urgency of the mutiny was bogged down with inconsequential details. Most of the book recounts the court martial proceedings that took place in the years after Bligh returned to England. The exciting chronological events that led to the mutiny and the amazing survival story of Bligh and the other officers is quite secondary to her factual, long-winded accounts of who is related to whom and their corresponding CVs, accounting for their previous sailing experiences under whichever captain. As an academic thesis, perhaps the inclusion of the many poorly-written and often confusing ship's log quotes would be important but as a non-fiction book it amounted to a slog to read through. I expected her to shed light on the 'true story' by connecting the dots between the actions and the journal entries. Knowing how well she wrote The Endurance: Shackleton's Legendary Antarctic Expedition , with her close attention to those journals, it is odd that she completely missed the mark with this adventure gone wrong.
