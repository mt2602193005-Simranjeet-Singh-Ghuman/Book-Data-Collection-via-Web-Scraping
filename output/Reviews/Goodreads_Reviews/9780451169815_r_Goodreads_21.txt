Convictions Based on Dreams – Not a Work of Fiction! I stumbled on to this book about fifteen years ago when I read The Innocent Man by John Grisham. The book itself was a little hard to get ahold of at that time, but I knew I had to read it after finishing Grisham's book that mentioned it, so eventually got it through a library loan. It is basically a true crime book about a murder in Ada, Oklahoma, in which two young men were convicted based on testimony of dreams they had in which they recounted aspects of the murder under interrogation. (The things they recounted from their dreams ended up not syncing with what actually happened after the body was found.) Nonetheless, the convictions have stood for forty years. One of the men, Karl Fontenot, has since been released; however, Tommy Ward is still in prison and will likely remain there. This case is one of two apparent wrongful convictions at the hands of lawmen and prosecutors in Ada, Oklahoma. The book and the facts in the case were well-researched and documented by Mayer. It is probably more direct and to the point than Grisham's account of the other case; however, they are equally compelling and enthralling. After many years, I finally got my hands on my own copy of the book, and it stands proud on my bookshelf next to "The Innocent Man." As an Oklahoman, these are two true crime stories that hold a lot of significance and have become personal favorites.
