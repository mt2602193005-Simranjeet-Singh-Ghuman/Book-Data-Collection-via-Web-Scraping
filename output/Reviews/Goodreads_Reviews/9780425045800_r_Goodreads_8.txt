A solid collection of stories from Varley's future-history universe. Notably, a large proportion of them explore themes of growing out of childhood, which in Varley's Seventies-inflected transhumanist society is very much a state of mind. "Goodbye, Robinson Crusoe" and the title story are standout examples. The Anna Louise Bach, policewoman of Luna, stories are also good, although taken together the Bach stories make the Moon seem like a really fucked up place. (Varley gets points for making his setting seem strange even after reading over a dozen stories about it, and even though [sex] Change ubiquity is perhaps his only sfnal innovation.) The weak link of the bunch is "Beatnik Bayou," mostly because it has a lingering trace of the "something not okay" that made it impossible for me to recommend the first collection of his I read. Let's just say Varley's projected sexual libertinism feels weird coming off the page, though these stories seem to justify it better. 3.5 stars, rounded up to 4.
