Considering the recent controversy about Dr. Seuss's racist Asian cartoons and the decision, by Random House, to discontinue the publication of six of his children's books, I was fully prepared not to like this biography, written by his close friends. To my surprise, I regained a measure of respect for his innovation and contribution, albeit contentious, to children's literature, particularly for early readers. In fact, I was surprised and delighted to learn so much about his life and work in La Jolla, California. Having grown up there myself, I knew he had lived there for most of his adult life, but this biography provided so many interesting details about places, events and people that I could hardly put it down. It wasn't surprising, however, to learn of his reclusiveness and his unconventional approach to building a career. Perhaps his lackadaisical motivation to meet other people's expectations (and deadlines) somehow coincides with an "artistic sensibility" but in Geisel's case it seemed extreme--almost like the Cat in the Hat and the Grinch. That makes sense, since they were his brilliant creations. I appreciated that the authors did address some of Geisel's foibles that led to the decision to stop publication of some of his books. A generally positive, relatively balanced, fascinating biography of an iconic and enigmatic children's author.
