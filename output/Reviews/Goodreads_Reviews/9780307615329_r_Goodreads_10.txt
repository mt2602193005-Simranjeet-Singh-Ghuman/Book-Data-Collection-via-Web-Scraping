I needed a comfort book so I picked this up the other day. I loved the Trixie Belden series as a kid. I wanted to have lots of brothers and sisters and a best friend and ride horses and live somewhere where it snowed in winter and have a mother who could whip up an apple pie in minutes. In my mind, Trixie and her family were “real” Americans (unlike my immigrant family). In many ways these books have NOT stood the test of time; the social mores of the 1950s are irksome. So many gender stereotypes. On the other hand, Trixie herself is still appealing, a tomboy who doesn’t do as she’s told. So I’ve got mixed feelings, but they’re still a comfort read.
