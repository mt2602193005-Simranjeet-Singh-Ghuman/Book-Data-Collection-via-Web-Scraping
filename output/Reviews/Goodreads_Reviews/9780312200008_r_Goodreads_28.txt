Somewhat disappointing compared to the previous Bess Crawford I read , but still passable. Rutledge's conversations with Hamish, the Scottish soldier in his head, become tiresome and contrived after a while. For a hundred or so pages, the book meandered between the same five or six characters without advancing the story in any significant manner. Still, the prose is well written with a sense of atmosphere. I particularly like the descriptive text capturing many small details about the time and place.
