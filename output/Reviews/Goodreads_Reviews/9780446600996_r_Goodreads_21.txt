Book 4 of the Seaforth series continues the story of the United Nations Navy in space. While the story continues to be entertaining and action packed, I consider it to be showing signs of weakness. Structurally, the flashbacks are intrusive and interrupt the flow of the narrative without being critical to our understanding of the plot. The struggle of conscience for Capt. Seafort seems overdramatized from the point of view of a modern, secular reader, with the Reunification Church being surprisingly Puritanical and dogmatic, but without showing us what their dogma is. Where is the equivalent of the Council of Nicaea? where is the Martin Luther of the 22nd century? We need more "meat" to understand why we should care that the Captain thinks he's in conflict with God. Also, we have been watching the aliens, who appear like large fish in outer space, attacking the humans from time to time but without a solid tactic or obvious motive. After seeing them for the third time I began to wonder about them. Are they a naturally occurring creature or a ship with some other type of creature inside? Why are they trying to exterminate humans, if they really are? Where do they come from? It's time the author dropped a few hints at least. Finally, I continue to think the psychological conflict of the Captain is overstuffed. It's a cliché in mystery stories and in some military fiction that the veteran hero has a resignation letter, which he's kept in his files for over 20 years and looks at every time his patience with his job wears thin. Of course Capt. Seafort is too young to have thought in that way but this aspect of the story seems hackneyed and well worn. The series started with a bang and volume 2 was even better, but the next two books have fallen from their apogee and need fresh ideas to invigorate them.
