Through Moon and Stars and Night Skies is a narrative about a little boy sharing his story about his journey to meet his new adoptive parents. He shares about his life before his new home and his scared nature of meeting his now beloved parents. He discovers the trust he has with his new family. As a literacy teacher, I would use this text to talk about poor families who have to give up their children because they can’t afford to take care of them (ex. the boy in the book was from a country that was poor). The students would interact with this story by discussing how important it was for the little boy to get adopted, even though he was scared. We could also discuss ways that they could help families. Ann Turner, the author of this book, is an American writer and poet. She does a wonderful job at telling the story from a child’s eye view. The illustrator, James-Graham Hale, does a great job at telling the story through his images. This text is different from most adoption stories because it talks about a young child being adopted at an older age than an average baby adoption. It gives children a broader understanding of adoption and it’s different views.
