1st Read: I greatly enjoyed this book. Ba Jin gives a unique look at how modernist values appeared in places outside of Shanghai and Beijing. It was cleverly written and I would highly recommend. 2nd Read: It's been a few years since I last read this. If anything, I enjoyed it more the second time through than the first. While some critics (CT Hsia) don't think as highly of this work, as compared to his others, I find it to be brilliant. Even though it is set in a time and place separate from us, Ba Jin portrays such fantastic modern humanity. Ba Jin does an incredible job allowing the reader to sympathize with "rebellious" youth (his own stance), but allowing nuance. While we agree with Juehui in principle, there are times when his own bias/privilege and treatment of others lays bare his own hypocrisy. And while I wanted him to be able to stand up to the older generation, I couldn't help but feel sympathy for his grandfather's plight and desire to have a strong, prosperous family. This is a novel that still speaks to our contemporary societies. The tensions are still there and reading Ba Jin's tale is a great way to experience these with new eyes.
