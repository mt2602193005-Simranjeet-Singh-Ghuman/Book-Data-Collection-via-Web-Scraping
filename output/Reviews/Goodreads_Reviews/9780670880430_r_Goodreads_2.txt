The author uses a question-and-answer format to describe her experience of losing her vision over a two-year period as an adult. Inspired by questions asked by students at schools where she has given presentations to raise awareness of the normalcy of capabilities of blind people, the book covers topics such as the way vision loss changed her; how she decided to partner with a guide dog; romantic relationships; parenthood; and the spectrum of ways sighted people treat her, from accepting to ignorant. In the introduction, the author tells about a time she was walking down a street using her long white cane. A little girl asked her mother why the lady was using "that stick." The mother shushed the child and hurried away. The author notes that this sent a message to the child about blindness as well as other disaabilities. They were a subject to be avoided. The author's book goes a long way toward dispelling that kind of attitude, answering questions with honesty and poise. This little book would make a great conversation starter in any classroom.
