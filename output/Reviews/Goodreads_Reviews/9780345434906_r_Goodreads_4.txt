James Herriot was always an author I really liked, so it's interesting to have some background information and a few backstories of people (and animals) who became characters in his books. Jim Wight does not quite have the flair of his father, but occasionally he tells a story or quotes a letter and that certain something shows through. In a way the biography seems to be a way of making up for the fact that Jim feels he did not recognize or acknowledge his father's writing talent enough. I think he gives a fair portrait of Herriot (real name: James Alfred "Alf" Wight) and some perceptive insights into what made the man tick. It's interesting to have backstories of some of the originals behind Herriot's more remarkable characters. All the same, it was a bit disappointing to know that the names of Alf's colleagues were not really inspired by Wagnerian opera!
