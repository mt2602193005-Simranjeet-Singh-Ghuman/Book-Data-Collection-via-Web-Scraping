An unprecedented flood immobilizes a northern Minnesota town (this sounds strangely familiar). Only the house belonging to 80-year-old Agatha McGee, Jon Hassler's most enduring character, is high enough to avoid being flooded by the Badbattle River. Agatha provides a refuge for seven other women, ranging from a teenager to a couple of her fellow senior citizens. I hope if I'm ever stranded by a flood with seven other people they'll be more interesting than the characters in this book. "The Staggerford Flood" almost seems to be an epilogue to Hassler's earlier Staggerford novels, but it lacks the wit and sparkle of those earlier visits.
