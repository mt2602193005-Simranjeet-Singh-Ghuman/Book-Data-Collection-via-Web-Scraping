I debated about whether this deserved two stars or three. I opted for three because a lot of it is well-written from a literary point of view. Silverberg writes action scenes well. He writes dialogue well. What he doesn't write so well is plots. Or pacing. Without getting into any plot details, my problem with the pacing is that the chapters are set many years apart. You could meet a character in his teens and then in the next chapter, without seeing him in between, he's in his 30s. The book covers a long span of time, so I suppose Silverberg thought that was necessary. To make the point he's making, I suppose it is, but what he needed was a longer book, or even a series. Compressing time that much hurt the characterization and made me feel nothing for the characters. As far as the plot goes, I think the book ends in the worst possible way he could've ended it. It made little sense, and left zero impact. I've liked some of Silverberg's short stories, so I'm not giving up on him, but this was a disappointment.
