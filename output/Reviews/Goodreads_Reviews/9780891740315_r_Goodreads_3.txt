Nostalgia overload! Caen, who I'd guess most people who moved to San Francisco since the turn of the century have never heard of, is "Mr. San Francisco," a moniker he pretended to abhor but clearly secretly coveted. This collection of short pieces and trivia represents some of Caen's best SF writing, and gives today's reader insight to what the City was like in the mid-70s. It turns out that while much has changed, the City retains its penchant for both nostalgia for a better past and a belief in its current manifold beauty. I wonder what Caen would think of all the changes to his adopted hometown in the last twenty years - two massive booms since he put down his pen. No doubt he'd lament all the changes, while celebrating that which has remained classic San Francisco: its weather and fog, its natural beauty, its openness to misfits and characters. He'd complain about all the millionaires driving up rents, about the busses driving people to the South Bay, about the intractable homeless and mental health crisis. He'd bemoan the building of the Salesforce Tower, while acknowledging that it's part and parcel of progress. He'd hardly recognize the restaurant scene. And he'd be in utter shock to hear that the Giants and Warriors have become dynastic powerhouses! If you're relatively new to SF (I moved here in 2014, so consider myself very much a newby), Caen is an excellent time portal!
