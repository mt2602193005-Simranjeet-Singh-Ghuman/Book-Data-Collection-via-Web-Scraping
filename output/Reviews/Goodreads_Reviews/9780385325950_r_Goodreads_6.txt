This book makes me want to find a door near here . I want Aslan to come to me and tell me what to do. The story itself makes me see that people can be foolish and irresponsible, but also mature far beyond their years. Kathrine often got on my nerves with her idiotic ways, but then I remember that she is only 15, and she cannot be expected to do everything the right way. In many books like this, where children are responsible for things they shouldn't be, like their own well-being and that of their siblings, the oldest is incredibly mature and acts like an adult. At times, I wanted Kathrine to act like that, she was the leader, she should fix everything, but I'm glad she didn't. Because in real life, she probably wouldn't. These characters have faults, which makes the book more powerful: the people feel like real people . Tracey often irritates me, what with her often popularity obsessed thoughts. But I lover her anyway, because she's family. And Douglas, brilliant Douglas, has little common sense at times. If I had to choose a favorite, it would be Douglas. He tries to protect his sisters, but he is only 14. He has got much too much of the world on his shoulders. And that makes him admirable, because he never gives up, and rarely gets frustrated. Little Alisa is the kind of person I would love to baby-sit, and hate to have against me. She is determined and intelligent, a dangerous combination. The belief she puts in Narnia and Aslan makes me believe in magic too. I love Narnia just as much as she does, and her young magic gives new life older magic. It makes me hope. They are in such a dark place, and still she hopes, so I can too.
