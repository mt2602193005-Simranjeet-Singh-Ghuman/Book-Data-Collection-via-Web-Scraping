I'll skip the synopsis. Honestly, James Patterson DOES what he's supposed to, and that's to craft a gripping, thrilling read and this story SOMEWHAT delivers. It's less than superior to other Alex Cross novels. I feel that (after reading a few Cross mysteries) most of these villains (The Wolf, The Mastermind, The Weasel, etc.) feel the same way. They're cardboard. Colonel Geoffrey Shafer (aka The Weasel) is somewhat developed, although he still (for some reason, in my opinion) moves stiffly. I felt kind of cheated after completing the novel. It was extremely rushed and I swung through it without a single jaw-dropping moment that I've experienced in other books by Patterson. What I did like, however, is how his maniacal behavior gradually progresses throughout the novel. Another thing I loved was Alex's continuous relationship with Christine (they're such a great couple!) which I think is realistic and fun. Overall, I felt this book was less than good with a few great parts but not enough to balance out the terrible parts.
