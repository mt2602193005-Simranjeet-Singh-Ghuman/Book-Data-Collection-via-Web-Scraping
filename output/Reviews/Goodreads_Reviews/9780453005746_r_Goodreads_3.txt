Haunted houses are one of my absolute favorite tropes in movies and books, and what I love most about them is the atmosphere and the set-up; those early chapters where the characters find and settle into their dream home by the sea/at the edge of the woods/in a quaint rural village, etc. That cozy period with just the slightest threat of uncanniness or foreshadowing of the horrors to come, for me, that's usually the best bit. So I didn't mind that pretty much nothing happens in The Magic Cottage until about the 60% mark. I know that won't be true for a lot readers who will be bored by all that set-up. I enjoyed most of my time with this book, and I was actually surprised by how tame this was on the horror front from what I've heard about James Herbert. I did think the ending was a bit silly and predictable, and I didn't love the infantilization of Midge by MC Mike throughout most of the book (he literally refers to his 30-something partner, an accomplished artist, as "Pixie" throughout), but it's a lot less offensive that what you get most of the time from the male gaze of this period of horror (and even now). And I loved Rumbo.
