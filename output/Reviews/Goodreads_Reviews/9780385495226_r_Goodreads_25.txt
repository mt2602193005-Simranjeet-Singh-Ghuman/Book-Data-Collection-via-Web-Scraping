Book Six in Non Fiction November, Before Covid, novels and movies about viral outbreaks were a far fetched work of fiction seeming highly unplausible despite the many attempts to base them on rational scientific basis, I remember seeing the Dustin Hoffman movie 'Outbreak' and how since then I always remember it whenever someone coughs or sneezes in a movie theatre. Another approach taken by authors like Michael Crichton's 'Andromeda Strain' (still in my to-read list) made the virus an extraterrestial visitor that comes back with our own probes, or in other cases down with a meteorite. In the end, it all seemed like sci-fi being introduced in more complex, realistic manner to suit an increasingly demanding and critical audience... until covid came and proved it all correct, the spread through our flights networks, the impossibility of crowd controls and ineffectiveness of lock downs in some countries, and my always present cinema cough scene... Before Covid, there was Ebola, yet for humanities good fortune, it was not as fast spread as Covid, because it had a mortality rate of up to 90%, and contrary to fiction, when the real virus came, no one knew where it came from. This book is not a work of fiction yet reads like a best selling novel, it is an actual yet shocking recounting of the Ebola viruses (yes there is more than one) history, starting with the first recorded outbreak in Germany (not Africa) in the 1960s. The average person knows about Ebola, but the what, how and when of Ebola are not as common knowledge as its name. Well, this book takes care of that, and in a simple entertaining manner without using complex terminologies or scientific dry language. MiM
