A ripping good yarn! Mr.McMurtry spins a tale of the American West circa 1830’s somewhere on the Missouri River. Mr. McMurtry is a skilled story-teller with wonderful dialogue and vast settings on the prairies – an unforgiveable landscape with the approach of winter. We are given a cavalcade of diverse characters, but we never lose track of who is who. The basic story is of the rich aristocratic Berrybender’s from England making their way along the Missouri River sight-seeing the American frontier. The main character is Tasmin (the rich aristocrat’s eldest and flamboyant daughter) who on occasion strikes out on her own, becomes infatuated with a stoic frontiersman, marries this rather primitive fellow and subsequently becomes pregnant. I did find Tasmin’s on and off again relationship with this Jim guy (aka Sin Killer) stretched my credibility - how could this upper class independent woman find happiness with this dim-wit, mealy-mouthed American. They were both independent – she intellectually and he a self-sufficient, but illiterate prairie food-gatherer. As for a correction, there were French-Canadians guides on this well-stocked expedition who were referred to erroneously by the author as “Quebecois”. “Quebecois” is a term that came out of 1960’s Quebec. In the 1800’s these explorers would have been called “Canadiens”. Also this is the first of four volumes – so the ending is sort of open-ended, but we are NOT left in the middle of a dangling mystery! An entertaining story in every sense of the word!
