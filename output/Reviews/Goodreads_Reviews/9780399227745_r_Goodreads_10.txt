Eric Carle is out of touch. This is the tale of a firefly who travels through the night, rejecting opportunities to interact with dogs and cats and lanterns and an owl. It finally spots a number of other fireflies who pay it no attention whatsoever. The Very Lonely Firefly concludes: "Now the firefly wasn't lonely anymore". When it finds a group of other fireflies, it is no longer lonely. Do the fireflies greet the protagonist? Do they so much as look at it? No, Mr Carle. This book has no business dealing with loneliness.
