An Awfully Big Adventure follows Stella, an innocent but simultaneously precocious teenage girl who begins work in a local theatre company in Liverpool in 1950. She is thrown into an adult world of intrigue, in which she witnesses tragedy, experiences sexual harassment, and navigates her relationship with her worrying guardians as she faces personal changes. Throughout it all, she is seen as rather an odd duck, a curiosity to her parents and fellow company. Her girlish obsession with the haughty theatre director shapes her actions and views of the people around her, resulting in bleakly funny but very dark drama. Beryl Bainbridge, known as a repeated Booker short-and-long-lister, is tricky with An Awfully Big Adventure . You won’t understand the first pages until you have read the last. Bainbridge is not always forthcoming and makes the reader piece together information, with detail added later on as she so pleases. Everything falls together in the last ten or so pages, and, let me tell you, it is shocking and heartrending. I received an ARC from McNally Editions in exchange for an honest review—thank you!
