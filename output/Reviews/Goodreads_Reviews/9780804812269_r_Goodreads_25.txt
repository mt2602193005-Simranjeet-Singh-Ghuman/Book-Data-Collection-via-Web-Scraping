This is a really nice collection of not-recent-at-all "modern" stories. Most of the stories have a reserved character. There's often not a lot happening, but they're almost all enjoyable reads. There's a huge disconnect between the academic style in the introductions, with all the editor's taxonomization and snobbery, and the simple language and subject matter of the translated stories they describe. This collection certainly isn't the chore that the textbook-style language of the editor makes it out to be! But we can't shit on him too much, considering he also did about half the translation work. Favourites: I liked almost almost all of the stories in this collection. My top pics are On the Conduct of Lord Tadanao ( a noble discovers impostor syndrome ), Letter Found in a Cement Barrel ( over-the-top grimness ), The Priest and his Love ( a priest gets rugpulled by worldly temptation just moments before achieving nirvana ), A Man's Life (a prison story) and House of a Spanish Dog ( a goofy inexplicable animorph story - along with Tiger-Poet ). Least favourites: There aren't many stories in this collection I wouldn't re-read. My least favourites were mostly boring (in my opinion) romance dramas, with the top spot going to Shotgun . Downtown and Under Reconstruction were also in this category, but not tedious enough to skip in a re-read. The cop sitcom story Nightingale didn't do much for me.
