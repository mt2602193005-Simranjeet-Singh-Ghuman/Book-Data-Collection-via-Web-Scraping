"Rachel Fister's Blister" has always held a special place in my heart, and I think the title explains why. I have owned this book for as long as I can remember, and even have 2 copies signed by the author. This story is about a little girl named Rachel Fister who found a blister on her little left hand toe. The story is funny and wacky, and written in rhymes, which makes it fun for any child to listen to. The illustrations look like they were done with paints. They follow along with the story very well and help visually show the children all the wackiness that it happening with the turn of every page. The whole story revolves around her family, friends, police, firemen, etc. trying to make it feel better. Eventually, the Fister's ask Queen Alice what they should do, and she tells them "use your lips." While the queens advice leaves everyone stumped as to what she means, it is Mrs. Fister who understands her. This can teach children that sometimes a mother's touch is the best form of medicine there is.
