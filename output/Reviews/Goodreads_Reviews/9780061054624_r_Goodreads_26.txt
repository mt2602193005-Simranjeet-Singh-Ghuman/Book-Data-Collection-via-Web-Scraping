Stormwarden showcases Janny Wurts at her strongest in terms of worldbuilding and prose. The setting is intricate, atmospheric, and clearly the product of careful thought, with a depth that suggests a much larger, even sci-fi, mythos beyond the immediate events of the novel. Wurts’ writing is often elegant and evocative, and at its best it lends the world a sense of weight and history that is genuinely impressive. Unfortunately, these strengths are not matched by the rest of the book. Characterization is the novel’s weakest element. The main female characters, in particular, are either underdeveloped or largely unengaging, or both, while the male characters fare only marginally better. Most of the cast feels strikingly passive, reacting to events rather than driving them. Taen for example pushes the narrative, but is really nothing more than that. The pacing also suffers as a result. Much of the story unfolds slowly, without sufficient emotional or dramatic payoff to justify its length. While the final two chapters stand out as the strongest portion of the novel—finally delivering tension, clarity, and a sense of direction—they arrive too late to redeem what precedes them. In the end, Stormwarden left me with mixed feelings. I greatly admired the craftsmanship of the world and the quality of the prose, but the lack of compelling characters and narrative drive made the reading experience uneven. Despite the promise shown in the closing chapters, I am not inclined to continue with the rest of the trilogy. I almost DNF'd at about half, but chose to push through. I am actually doubting if I regret this or not.
