A fact filled biography of a man that stands squarely apart from them. The most impressive aspect of the book is the sense of cultural and technological context it provides, almost placing you in Blake’s messy workshop amid dreary 19th century london. The details of Blake’s life and how these may have impacted his work are well documented and thorough. However, though persistent and commanding in his praise of Blake’s work throughout, there wasn’t space for a chapter on entirely why Blake’s work has endured and transcended his own time. This would be forgivable in a biography of Edward Heath or any rather prosaic character of history, but unfortunately when writing about Blake there must be a confrontation of his work, or at least an attempt to decipher some of the mythology for the reader. The effect of this omission causes this book to feel like a whole series of waypoints pointing to a locked door. There is constant gesturing to Blake’s greatness in description of his works, but scarcely any depth as to why. Ultimately as a factual biography it is effective. But what’s pertinent about Blake is not the details of his life, it is the content of his work.
