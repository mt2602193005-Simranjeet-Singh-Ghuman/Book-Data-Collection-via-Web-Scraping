3.5* - good read, but by no means amazing. Don’t get me wrong, I enjoyed getting to know the author Ma Jian, and hearing about his weird and disturbing goings on as he travelled China, and the fact I’m soon travelling China myself means it was a fascinating historical and cultural exploration of the country. But at times I felt his writing lacked any clear chronological path or narrative, it was more a jumble of disconnected stories, and too many names and characters he hardy explored but spoke often about. As he himself is a self proclaimed poet, I think his writing was too abstract and self absorbed, which came off as a bit grandiose and arrogant to me at times. Maybe I just didn’t like Ma Jian, and that has skewed my opinion slightly. Anyway, a good book, but I wouldn’t necessarily recommend it unless you want to find out more about China. Bits I found particularly fascinating/disturbing include the leprosy camp, the opium rehab centres, the deserts of inner Mongolia, and the exploration of Tibetan / buddhist culture at the end. Some beautiful moments thinking back!
