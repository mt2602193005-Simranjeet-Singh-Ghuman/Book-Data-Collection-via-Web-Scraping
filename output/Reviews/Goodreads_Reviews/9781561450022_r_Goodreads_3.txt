Terry Kay has written a heartfelt and endearing short novel about aging, loss, and family. He lovingly explores how the grief process can be difficult yet, as a natural path in life that should be met with dignity. Sam Peek’s wife of 57 years passes at the beginning of the novel and their 7 grown children are just not sure how he is going to cope. When a white dog that seems to be a stray makes its way to Sam’s doorstep, life and grieving become a bit more bearable for him. White Dog is a mysterious entity that Sam’s children think is all in his mind. Sam knows somehow that the dog is there for him and his well-being and comfort. Kay gets all of the nuances of this period of Sam’s life just right. My own parents are the same age now as Sam and I see the similarities in not being able to do the things they used to yet longing to be young again. Sam’s memories return and he enjoys those moments again that seemed so fleeting. Terry Kay has said that the novel began as a nonfiction work in honor of his own parent’s marriage. He has written a very moving story and he adds the right amount of humor along with creating authentic parent/child relationships. Fabulous dialogue and nothing out of place or out of the ordinary. Just a fine story that will tug at your heart and possibly bring a few tears amidst the smiles. This story celebrates all that is good in life!
