In the 16th century England two boys were born. One was destined to become the future king, another was a son of very poor parents. The lives of Edward (Henry VIII’s son) and Tom unexpectedly intertwine when the poor commoner’s son enters the palace and meets the young prince. The two boys look very much alike as it turns out. Swapping clothes is enough to make the pauper look like prince and the prince resemble the pauper. Clothes determine one’s identity in the eyes of their contemporaries. The echoes of this mentality are felt today. Tom is also a very intelligent boy which will help him deal with the unprecedented situation. Edward will learn a lot from his ordeal of living with beggars and thieves. Not all thieves are bad, and some of them have been forced to lead such a life by the royal laws. This story must have been quite entertaining for the time when it was written. It is still quite entertaining today. This is a clever story. Mark Twain offers some sharp comments about ‘noble’ birth and social hierarchy based on birth and pedigree and not on merit and talent. Twain’s story has a happy ending. Both boys are rewarded in the end. Kindness is also rewarded.
