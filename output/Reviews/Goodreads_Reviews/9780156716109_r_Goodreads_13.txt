Really 3.5 stars. There's a lot in this to be appreciated, but his point of departure--civilization turning its back on the humanistic in favor of technics and machines--stretches credulity. Not that this has not happened to some degree, but as it unfolds in Mumford's capable prose it comes across as a sharper dichotomy than it is. And then of course his elevation of heliocentrism as a new "religious" apprehension...too far a bridge for me to cross. I really wanted to like this more. His City In History delighted me...45 years ago. I almost hesitate to revisit it now. Don't get me wrong, I believe Mumford was in the right arguments, with a viewpoint important as a counterweight to the usual acceptance of Things As They Are, but I don't have the patience anymore to filter out the unsupportable.
