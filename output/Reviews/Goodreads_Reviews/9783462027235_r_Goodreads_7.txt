Erich Maria Remarque is best known for his classic masterpiece All Quiet on the Western Front . Arch of Triumph may not be equal to that but it is very good, a beautifully written novel that stands on it's own merit and one that I enjoyed reading from start to finish. The setting is 1938 Paris, nervous about the unrest in Europe prior to the start of World War II, and filled with expatriates and refugees of many nationalities. Ravic is an accomplished German surgeon, and having fled Nazi Germany, he is living in Paris without passport or documantation. He finds work by performing surgery for two, less than average, French doctors. Really his main goal is to avoid capture and deportation, and survive the coming maelstrom of war. Amid all this turmoil, just when he should least expect it, he falls in love with Joan, an actress. The characters are few in this novel, really Ravic and Joan drive most of the stories plot, so Remarque has time to fully develop these very interesting characters and this intriguing story line. The reader can feel the tension of the city and fear of it's people in the words of Remarque, and you are left with a feeling of hopelessness for everyone. Remarkable book, I loved it. 4.5 stars. Review revised November 2017.
