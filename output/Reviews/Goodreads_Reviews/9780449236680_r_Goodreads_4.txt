Candystriper to Outpatients. Reminiscent of Robin Cook’s “Coma” (but preceding it), this tense thriller centres on the ICU of a major LA hospital. The patient in room 6 is the female victim of an attempted murder as her car is forced off the road. Kept alive by mechanical ventilation, in spite of what appear to be non life-threatening head injury, she shows no signs of recovering. Engaging the aid of a team of psychologists, Dr Rand is startled by their conclusion that fear is keeping the patient in persistent state of deep unconsciousness and that she is only too aware that someone is trying to kill her. She communicates (and some suspension of disbelieve is required here) through the automatic writing of a receptive nurse and the plot follows the search for her nemesis who has evidently not given up on killing her. I like the way the author writes from the unemotional point of view of the medical staff (there’s a deal of technical stuff but this is immaterial to the plot) and their puzzlement at the unfolding events.
