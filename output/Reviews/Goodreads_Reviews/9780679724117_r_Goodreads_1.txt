intermittently good stuff but not up to his usual level of excellence in baseball analysis established by the annual abstracts in the late 70s and 80s. Structure is a bit odd, going through "teams" [mainly chrono notes throughout the 1989 season], "people" [extended essays about individuals around the game -- very good stuff here on the Dowd Report re Pete Rose gambling scandal, plus one on a scout's life in the game], and then "players", consisting of an exhaustive compendium of then-major-leaguers, some with a bit of detail but others as little as a line like "no prospect" or "one of the best pitchers in the league" and all with an eye on advising you about your fantasy teams. It's funny to see what well-informed people were saying about young players at the time -- he touts Randy Johnson as a "great draft pick" for your fantasy team, noting that there's a risk but that [on the upside] he COULD be a Bob Veale-, Sam McDowell-type pitcher. I dare say Randy Johnson's career paid off on that promise and was just maybe even better than that of Sudden Sam. Nobody hits on all of them, though -- he didn't think much of young Tom Glavine -- "long-term future not as bright" -- and ranked him behind at least 3 pitchers on same team and in a group with 3 others. One of the ones he trailed was Smoltz, but still........
