This was book 2 in a two-novel book, so I had zero idea this was part of a series. Neither the MMC nor the FMC had much depth, and it took forevvvvvver for anything to happen. Way too many side characters that I forgot (and no longer cared) who was who. By the time readers actually meet Flynn and Caitlin, the kidnapped pawns, we don't care about them, either. They have no personal value. As for the 10-second encounter in the end: "Oh, you're Quinn. We were spies together once." "Sure, I never put it together that Trace O'Hurley is my wife's brother, and you are named Trace O'Hurley, who looks just like her." (Some freaking intelligence worker YOU are, dude.) I've said it before about Nora Roberts' writing: Please, please, please stop giving us omniscient points of view that shift from paragraph to paragraph (and sometimes sentence to sentence). She thought ... he thought ... that random character thought ... It's lazy and unprofessional writing. Cannot believe this made it past an editor and actually got published as is.
