I liked this book a lot. It's about four kids whose mother gets depressed and starts drinking, then starts neglecting them and they have to fend for themselves. I've seen a lot of comments saying that the protagonist acted dumb and that they would do it differently -the thing is, she is 15, she is suddenly unfairly forced to be the adult in the house and take care of her siblings and she doesn't want to tell anyone because her siblings scare her by telling her that if CPS is called they will be removed from the house and separated and she cannot tell dad because dad is garbage - he visits the kids a few times a year and he doesn't even want to see or take the fourth kid out to the museum because she is not his -the teacher that ends up helping them was rightfully very angry with the dad for acting like this - but it sadly never really goes anywhere. And you are looking at this with adult eyes, of course as an adult you would act differently because you are more mature and have different experiences. Kids get reported and they move to dad and step mother - not because dad would request dad but because step mother insisted that they should take them - making the dad that much worse compared to everyone else. I also loved the references to Narnia, because young kids faced with trauma really do disassociate and try to find new magical worlds they can go to - so Alissa is acting like every little kid whose mother is constantly drinking, doesn't have money for food and whose electric just got turned off because the bills are not paid. I also loved that each of the siblings has a unique personality and that they are not just carbon copies of each other and that each of them deals with the trauma differently. It's a unique little book and I didn't expect that I would love it as much as I did.
