Barbara Lavette, a grieving widow in her sixties, is living a simple life in San Francisco. A strongman in the local political scene pressures her to run for Congress. She loses again and again, but he keeps pressuring her to run. Meanwhile, she resents everyone in her family for marrying people she considers beneath her, often due to their race. In fact, the very obvious racism and classism in this book really detracted from the story. Barbara's son wants to be close to her, but does nothing but criticize her. Barbara has a grudge against her brother and forbids anyone she knows to associate with him, but allows her campaign manager to accept a large contribution from him. The book is full of contradiction and prejudice. I accidentally returned this to the library before I finished reading it, but since I could not enjoy or respect any of the characters, it was no great loss to miss the ending.
