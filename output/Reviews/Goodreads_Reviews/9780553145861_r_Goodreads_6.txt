Waltz of hearts, Whenever there’s even a hint of music or anything relating to it, in any of Barbara Cartland books, you can be sure to find someone from Russia or Hungary in the mix. Sometimes the heroine has Russian or Hungarian ancestry and is musical, sometimes the hero is from Russia or Hungarian nobility and therefore predisposed to appreciate music on another, almost esoteric level than his English peers. No matter, the heroine is almost always impoverished, and there are constraints against her union with the hero. Almost always Barbara Cartland manages to produce something like a magician pulling a rabbit from a hat, that would redeem the heroine, either a secret about her exalted lineage is discovered at the last minute, or someone dies and her father inherits the family title and fortune, and she moves up the social ladder or her real father emerges and he’s minor royalty so his by blow would be acceptable for the aristocratic hero. Or so he says. And loose bits are tied neatly and satisfactorily for all concerned. This one is one of those where the father suddenly inherits a marquisate. Cute but a template nevertheless.
