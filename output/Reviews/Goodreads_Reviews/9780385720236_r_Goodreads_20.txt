In early 80s in China, when not so many years had passed after chairman Mao’s death and the horrors of cultural revolution were still fresh in memory, communist marching songs remained the soundtrack of many rural towns. At the same time, the cities were already undergoing big changes, as Hong Kong popstars were secretly played through cassette players and uniformed partymembers were gradually replaced by suitewearing businessmen. It was a perfect time for a rebelling artist from Beijing to hit the road and look for Buddhist enlightenment in the most remote and forgotten parts of China. After almost getting killed several times in the desert, jungle, and mountains for various reasons, the author finally arrives at the destination: an excellent travel book, worth all the trouble at least from reader’s perspective.
