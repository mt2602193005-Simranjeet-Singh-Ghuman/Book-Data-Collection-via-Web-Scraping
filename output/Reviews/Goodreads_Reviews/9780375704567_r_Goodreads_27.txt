"Find out something new, or little-known and share it with your readers" was Faulks's response to a reader question about the underground war being fought in the trenches of the First World War in Birdsong. Taking his own advice, he sees to it that I'm now quite clued-up on the 1960 Nixon/Kennedy battle for the White House, the layouts of Washington DC and New York, and the machinations of the UK Foreign Office, the FBI and Soviet close espionage as the Cold War approached its apex at the end of the 1950s. As ever, this serves as background to Faulks's needle-sharp observation and articulation of personal joys, dilemmas and deeply-buried psychological issues, bound in a wrapper of a love affair played out as a mid-20th century Montague-Capulet tangle, and a conflict between passion and duty, with a bebop and hard bop soundtrack.
