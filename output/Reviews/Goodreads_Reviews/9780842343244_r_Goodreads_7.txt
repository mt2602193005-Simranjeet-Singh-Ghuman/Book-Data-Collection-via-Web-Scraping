Tribulation Force felt like one of those “bridge” books to me, and I’m okay with that. I didn’t love it the same way I loved the first Left Behind, but I can see what it’s doing and why it needs to exist, especially knowing how long this series is. It picks up right where things left off with Rayford, Chloe, Buck, and the rest of the group starting to come together and actually form what becomes the Tribulation Force, trying to survive and make sense of everything while Nicolae Carpathia continues to rise in power in a way that’s clearly not good. There’s a lot going on in this one. More moving pieces, more shifting dynamics between characters, more of that global tension starting to build, and I found myself turning the pages faster than I did with the first book because of it. That said, I’m not going super deep on this one like I normally would. It feels too early to really judge it on its own because it clearly exists to set up what’s coming next. It’s one of those books where you can tell the payoff isn’t here yet, it’s coming later, and this is laying the groundwork. And honestly, I’m fine with that because I’m in at this point. I’m committed to seeing this whole thing through. It was fun, it kept me engaged, and it did exactly what it needed to do to make me want to pick up the next one right away. I’ll probably come back to this later and look at it differently once I’ve got more of the full picture, but for now it did its job and I’m already ready to keep going.
