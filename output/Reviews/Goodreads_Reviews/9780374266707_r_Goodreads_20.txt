Not so much Derry Girls as Oban Girls. Five Catholic schoolgirls, the Sopranos in the choir, travel to Edinburgh to take part in a choral competition/drink as much as they can and get off with any bonkable boys. Their ambition is not to win the competition, but to get back to Oban in time to go to the local niteclub. The novel was written in the mid ‘90s and I wonder if Warner was influenced by the five Spice Girls. If so: Orla – Miracle Spice. Cured of cancer by radiotherapy (according to the doctors) or a trip to Lourdes (according to her parents), Orla tried to have sex with a patient in a coma in the hospital just so that she didn’t die a virgin. Manda – Slutty Spice. Growing up in poverty, all Manda wants is to get pregnant by a local lad and settle down. Chell – Animal Lover Spice. Once accidentally killed a litter of puppies. She has lost two dads at sea and now her sister has had a baby with her (non blood relative) uncle. Kylah – Rock Chick Spice. In a grunge band with three lads that she hates. Fionnula – Sapphic Spice. Fionnula (the Cooler) is coming to terms with the fact she likes girls as much as – more than – boys. The book is a romp, but also quite serious on the failings of faith schools and the limited worldview of small town people, especially what girls can expect – having a baby or working in a shop. It’s not, however, a Guardian writer's type view of the working class, you don’t feel sorry for these girls, you envy their grabbing at life. Given that Warner wrote a sequel to Morvern Callar (These Demented Lands) and that these girls would now be in their 40s, I suggest a follow up. I want to see what happened to these crazy chicks after they leave Sister Condron/Condom and Sister Fagan (the Pagan).
