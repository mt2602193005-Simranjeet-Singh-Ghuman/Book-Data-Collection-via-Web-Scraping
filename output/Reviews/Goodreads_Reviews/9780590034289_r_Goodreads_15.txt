As evidence for Arthur C. Clarke's assertion that "any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic," Chitty Chitty Bang Bang certainly begs the question. I read this book a few times as a child and enjoyed it very much, but I never mistook the car's obvious sentience as proof that it was magic. I suspect Fleming never did either, but that he thought children would; or that he simply wanted to avoid explanation, and thought that calling something "magic" is authorial carte blanche where exposition is concerned. Clarke's "law" is nonsense, of course. It's a zombified relic of colonialist ideology toward indigenous peoples, muddled up with Golden Bough -type mythological reductionism. Chitty Chitty Bang Bang is nonsense too, but a different and far better kind (if you overlook its claim about magic): it's bedtime-story nonsense.
