Out of Egypt is definitely on my best books list of 2025. I came to it thanks to a recommendation by a friend. Aciman takes us on an epic journey through his richly peopled life in Alexandria till the age of fourteen. The narrative includes many odd and wise members of his family, his two grandmothers memorably nicknamed as the Saint and the Princess, his mute mother who can communicate herself quite well, his businesman father who works at a factory, his adventures in an Islamic school (the Victoria/Victory College), the many domestic helpers and their colourful personalities, his uncle Villi who reinvented himself so many times to finally retire as an Englishman in Surrey, his many tutors - from Abdou's son Ahmed to the Italian tutor, Mario Dall'Albaco, the other tutors Madame Marie, the pretty Roxane, his summers at the beach house in Mandara until one day they were forced to leave Egypt as a result of the policies of the then president, Gamal Abdel Nasser. Shifting identities and moving countries are essential survival strategies for this Jewish family. An exquisite and moving and nostalgic portrait of a lost world. I found the second last chapter, 'The Lotuseaters', to be the most evocative. The writing is so cinematic (as fans of Call Me By Your Name will know), I was mentally making notes of the directors who could potentially bring each chapter to screen. If you read nothing else this year, read this. Looking forward to reading My Roman Year next, which continues Aciman's story and which was published 30 years later in 2024.
