A series of poison pen letters have incited suspicion and tension in the village of Prior's Umborne. Nigel Strangeways is asked to look into the matter, as one suicide and an attempted suicide have occurred as a result of the letters. But even as Nigel quickly realizes who has authored the letters, other undercurrents in the closed community lead to murder. Now Nigel must assess the members of two families with a dark history between them. Two brothers with attachments to two sisters: all with possible motives for the murder. Did someone work alone, or were they collaborating? Whose alibis are legitimate and who is lying to protect someone else? How do the poison pen letters factor into the murder? Nigel senses he must solve the murder before the tensions in the village boil over. This is not one of my favorite Nicholas Blake mysteries. The psychology employed by Nigel Strangeways and the formidable Superintendent Blount of Scotland Yard is often antiquated. The characters are every bit as stagey and cartoonish as in an Agatha Christie tale. One villain in particular is so odd (in appearance and obsessions) he might be easily transplanted into a Dickens story. And the mystery appears to hinge on a timetable, only for that to be made nonsense by some fairly simple (overlooked) evidence.
