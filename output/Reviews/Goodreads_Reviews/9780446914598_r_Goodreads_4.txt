"You are, and always have been, my dream." Noah Calhoun returned from WWII to a rundown plantation home. As he works to restore it, he thinks about Allie - a girl he had fallen in love with but lost contact. Allie was the daughter of a wealthy family, and Noah very much wasn't...so despite their feelings, they didn't stay together all that time ago. However, a newspaper article and Noah's restoration project brings Allie back to him, and they will need to decide - is love worth it all? "You can't live your life for other people. You've got to do what's right for you, even if it hurts some people you love." Noah and Allie - I've gone back and forth on this relationship so many times. They belonged together...they were the worst possible fit...but one thing is true...the way I just absolutely bawl at the end of the book every single time. This was one of the first books I remember crying over - their enduring love and how it impacted their lives over the decades. Definitely recommend this one if you have a full box of tissues nearby.
