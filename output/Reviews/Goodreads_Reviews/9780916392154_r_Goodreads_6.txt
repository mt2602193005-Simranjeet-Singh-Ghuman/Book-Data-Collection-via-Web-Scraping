I both LOVE this and kind of resent it, because Dickens wrote about injustice, and he saw justice done as a result of his writing. Flash-forward to today, where it's all being undone... certainly where *I* am . A nitpick I have is, the way the text describes it, Dickens had photographic memory or close to it, and he simply recalled things he'd seen and people he'd met and feelings everyone had. Imagining doesn't come into the picture as much if it's just basic recall and changing a few details. I imagined (haha) that Lewis Carroll or J.R.R. Tolkien might've been a better subject for the value of "imagination" than someone who wrote mostly slice-of-life exposés about England's darker corners. Then again, that was my complaint about The Value of Believing in Yourself , that it didn't REALLY describe Louis Pasteur believing in himself other than REALLY HOPING his idea worked, or else that boy infected by rabies would've died. I mean, I *sort of* think this would've been better as The Value of Creativity , since he brought his memories into existence , but I *guess* Thomas Edison is a better candidate for that. I don't know. I do know that this is a lovely and inspiring biography about Dickens (even if it claims he hallucinated a bookworm talking to him, haha), and I would definitely recommend this to younger readers... certainly more than some of the other books in the series . Dickens might be a bit farther out than particularly young readers might understand, though, especially since things like debtor's prisons have been done away with... in England, anyway. Edit to add: Unimaginatively, there's a The Value of Imagination 2: The Story of Jim Henson ! Couldn't think of another word, huh?
