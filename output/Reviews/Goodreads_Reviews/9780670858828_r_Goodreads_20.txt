“His embrace was again a public one, the hug and hard kiss on the mouth from the comrade-husband; his presence before her bounced off the excited glare of her face like the flash of a piece of glass in the sun” (96). Her metaphors are so appropriate to the literal situation she is writing about and the characters involved. She portrays human relationships with such a nuanced comprehension of the way our interiors operate and the ways in which we communicate it takes my breath away: “With him there was no haste in communication; in every encounter between human beings there is a pace set that belongs to them, and that will be taken up in its own rhythm whenever they are together“ (259). Her ability to actually go from the conceptual to the page is astonishing. Plus plot and putting the reader right in the middle of the narrative. The moral compass is explored in a very specific time and setting. Not a breezy read but that’s a plus; it’s interesting and rewarding and satisfying and really makes you think.
