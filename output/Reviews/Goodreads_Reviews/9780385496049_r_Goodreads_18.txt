The topic of the partition interests me as both sets of grandparents and my parents experienced the brutality of leaving their homes and trying to survive as they crossed the border to India. If you are hoping this book is entirely about the partition, I hate to inform you only the last 100 pages or so is about that aspect. I really tried to enjoy this book, but it took me over 4 months to read. I never got into the story or the characters. The story is about a woman named Roop who marries Sardarji as a second wife. The first wife Satya hates her and resents her husband. Roop tries to win the affection of her co-wife Satya but fails, so she focuses on keeping Sardarji interested in her. That’s really the story told in over 460 pages. Here are my issues with the book: -The point of view changed constantly. This usually works in other novels although I couldn’t appreciate the effort here. -There were way too many characters that didn’t really need to be mentioned. At one point the main character sees a childhood friend of hers as an adult and I couldn’t even remember who the friend was. -I found the writing was too descriptive -The main character (Roop) is shown as a strong, opinionated and driven girl who grows up to be a submissive woman who hands both her kids over to the older wife (Satya) with no fight?
