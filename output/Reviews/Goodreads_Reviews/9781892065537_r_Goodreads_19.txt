I'm not sure if it's Andre Norton's writing, or the fact that this book is based on an artists' rendition of a world that is very detailed and interesting, but the story seemed quite disjointed. I will admit haven't read a lot of Norton's work, but she was quite prolific, and I intend on trying another one or two. The book itself was interesting - however, there seemed to be a lot of worn out clichés wherein the main character was the underrated nothing from nowhere, the amorphous "bad guy" was dealt with in a way unclear to the reader, the random showing up of people, and the odd defection of his brother. Overall, it was worth the read, especially as I'd read Mark of the Cat previously, and it cannot stand alone, but not something I'd recommend highly.
