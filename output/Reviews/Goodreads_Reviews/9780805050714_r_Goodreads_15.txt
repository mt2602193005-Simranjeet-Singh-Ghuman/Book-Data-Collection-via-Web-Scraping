Somehow, this is what I wanted out of JG Ballard’s prose. It’s dreamy, dystopian, surreal and poetic. There is no denying Erickson’s gift with written word, and his ability to sew threads of stories together into some sort of grotesquely beautiful quilt. Students of fiction (especially those interested in the kafkaesque or magical realism) would do well to read Erickson and consider a master at work. But, this novel succeeds through its style more than its substance. The girl’s subplot is compelling storytelling. The rest of it… well, a mysterious setting and dream-like logic can only carry you so far. To be clear, I did enjoy this novel. It is difficult enough to demand your attention, and every literary device he employs is exceptional; however, the story did not pay off meaningfully nor do I feel that Erickson’s messaging about self, or love, or loss was universally or even personally groundbreaking. Authors like Haruki Murakami and Amy Hempel pull that off. It’s a shame Erickson couldn’t in this novel because this has all the trappings of a modern classic. It just never gets over the line. True 4 stars.
