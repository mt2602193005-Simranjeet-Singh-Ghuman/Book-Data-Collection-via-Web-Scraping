Historical romances featuring a poor or servant hero are hard to find amid a sea of books about dashing dukes and wealthy rakes. The ones that do exist often cop out with a storyline about mistaken identities or "the hero is actually an aristocrat in disguise," or else are horribly unrealistic. But this hero is a genuine working class man who starts out as a lowly kitchen boy. I liked that the book depicted the very real class differences and the morality of the time, and that the characters' actions and behavior were appropriate for the period. Admittedly there were times I wanted to shake the heroine, or strangle her parents (I still don't think I can forgive them!) yet their actions were still understandable given the social mores of the day. The descriptions of the settings and various events were lovely, almost like looking at an old painting, and gave me a strong feeling of the spirit of the age. Unfortunately this sometimes translated into feeling a bit "detached," like I was reading an account of something that happened long ago instead of feeling "in the moment." Jens was a great hero, strong but not a bossy "alpha" type, a dreamer but still down to earth and aware of the consequences of their actions. Lorna was immature sometimes but sweet, wearing her heart on her sleeve, just as you'd imagine a sheltered eighteen year old falling in love for the first time. I also liked that although they were attracted right away, they still fell in love gradually instead of immediately falling into bed.
