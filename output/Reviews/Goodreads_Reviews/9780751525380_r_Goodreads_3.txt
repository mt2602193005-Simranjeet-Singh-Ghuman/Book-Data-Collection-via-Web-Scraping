This book covers about ten years, from the mid 1870s to the mid 1880s, when Women's Rights were an enormous issue. The story focuses on members of the extended Morland family, placing them against the backdrop of history. But, if you haven't been reading the series from the beginning you may not quite understand who all the characters are and how exactly they are related. In London, Venetia is still determined to become a doctor, despite all the opposition. Her younger brother, Marcus, is happily employed in the Army; while their sister Olivia is a lady-in-waiting to the Queen. But both Harry and Augusta soon join the rackety friends of the Prince of Wales. Meanwhile, in Yorkshore, at Morland Place, George and Alfreda continue their rebuilding plans, along with courting friends in high places, as Alfreda strives to regain what she believes is her rightful place. George's sister Elizabeth finds her marriage to an older clergyman stifling, but at least she has her daughter for love and comfort. Naturally there are births, deaths, and marriages — as well as love affairs — which all have their effects on the family. Despite its length, this book is hard to put down. Recommended.
