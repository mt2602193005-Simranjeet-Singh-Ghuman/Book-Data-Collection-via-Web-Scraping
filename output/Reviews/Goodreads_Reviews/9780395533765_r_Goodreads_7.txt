What is most unique, and valuable, about this book is the unique approach. Instead of the common guidebook which gives thumbnails of illustrations and information about individual species of birds, this book goes much deeper and is more technical. The first seven chapters cover principles and pitfalls of field identification; terminology of bird anatomy; plumage (what you see); behavior and voice (what you observe and hear); moving beyond species level; and finally, techniques and resources. Twenty-four chapters provide detail about specific groups, for example: Learning to Identify Gulls, and Learning to Identify Sparrows. Exceptionally well-indexed and illustrated, this is a must have for any serious birder or bird photographer.
