The problem I've always had with James Bond is that, as a character, he seems a bit of a misogynistic dick. Here he's slightly less misogynistic (only just though) and quite clearly not the most observant spy as he doesn't seem to be able to read women well at all. Then, when thinking about having sex with Vesper, he decides it would have "the sweet tang of rape!" Even James Bond in the movies wasn't that bad. It was interesting to read about the origins of Bond and the novel was well written. The card game didn't particularly grip me but the kidnapping, and subsequent torture, were extremely well written and grimace inducing. The twist was well done but maybe telegraphed slightly. I liked how Bond dealt with the kidnapping and how maybe it would change him. And the ending showed the reason why he is such a cold character. I'm also aware the book is of a different time and that most spies, in novels and films, are usually portrayed as bed-hopping and women using (it's not a category I read often) it's just that here Bond doesn't really do anything well apart from win a game at a casino. I loved the film much more than the book although the film is my second favourite Bond (On her Majesty's Secret Service is my favourite) so maybe that put me off slightly. There were lots I enjoyed about the book and I read this in about 3 hours. I also enjoyed it enough that I'm going to continue making my way through the Bond novels
