Normandy, France 1789. Lucien Bouchard is the younger son of the Count de Bouchard, and he recognizes the seeds of rebellion in the air and decides to build a new life in America. He lands in New Orleans, and then decides to settle in what is now Alabama. The land is still an untamed wilderness, and Lucien is wise enough to realize he can't tame it alone - he needs to work with the natives to build his dream of a great plantation. The book covers the bulk of Lucien's adult life, his marriages and children, the Indian wars and yes - slavery. Lucien is wise and kind and good and wants to free them all, and I thought his dilemma fighting that losing battle was one of the more interesting things in the book. Windhaven Plantation is the first in a fourteen book series focused on the Bouchard family, and much of this book read like a primer setting the foundations for events in the later books. Despite the appearances of the cover, this is very much a historical novel, and I would not recommend it to those looking for a romance. As much as I do like historical details, I felt at times there was too much and I found myself skimming a bit. Perhaps those more familiar with the southern states when still under French and/or Spanish control could grasp it better than I was able to. My only other complaint would be that Lucien was too wise, too kind, too all-knowing and despite being fresh off the boat he could win just about any battle thrown at him. It wore a bit thin after a while. 3.5/5 stars and I do plan on continuing on with the series.
