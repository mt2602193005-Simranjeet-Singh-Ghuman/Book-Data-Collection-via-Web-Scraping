What an incredible book, not just a story that embraces the social conditions and living realities of the Irish during the years of famine, rent raises and heartless evictions, but it is narrated through the vehicle of a reportage by a NY Times journalist, who writes a book called An American Abroad, Notes of London and Ireland in 1847. The version we read is the commemorative 100th edition, revised, unexpurgated and with many new inclusions. Throughout the text are what are often macabre depictions of the Irish in their many states of suffering and accusation as a species. The story is of a murder that occurs on a ship the Star of the Sea and before it sets sail we meet various characters from Connemara, west Ireland, of both classes, rich, poor, honest, criminal. "Humiliation their only inheritance, and denial the coinage in which it is paid." Each chapter is displayed with a bold title and a short summary description of what is within it, a kind of documentary, journalistic style of the mid 19th century - certain chapters presented as articles, or log entries at sea with compass points, times. I thought it was so well done, it invokes the period of history, The issues, the characters and the journey, while narrating a compelling story, and succeeds in surprising the reader. An excellent accompaniment to 'Land' by Maggie O'Farrell, read after that novel on the recommendation of O'Farrell in an interview with Irish Times Women's podcast.
