2.5 rounded down What I didn’t like: This should have been exactly my kind of novel: gritty, darkly comic, morally complex and rich in period detail. Yet I never connected with it. Bainbridge’s habit of dropping the reader into conversations and relationships before fully establishing them left me feeling disoriented. I understood more in hindsight, but I don’t think a novel should rely on hindsight to become emotionally or narratively satisfying. I also remained at a distance from most of the characters. What I did like: The prose is lean, the post-war Liverpool setting is vivid, and some of the deadpan humour is genuinely funny. Overall I appreciate that many readers admire Bainbridge. For me, though, reading this book admiration never became enjoyment. I will try another of her novels though.
