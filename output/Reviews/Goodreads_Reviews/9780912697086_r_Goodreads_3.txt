2.5, rounded down despite the author's clear, paternal affection for cheating in baseball. Ah, the pre-steroid era... Much of the book isn't super relevant anymore. The first couple chapters could be summed up as "We don't have Statcast yet, so." The real issue is that the organization is all over the place. There's no real central thesis beyond "Baseball needs more than just fastballs," which doesn't really need to be proven. And the rapid succession of name drops within chapters makes it hard to see where he's going. The high points are the many great photos of old ball players, though their placement seems somewhat random. I also did love his brief history of banned pitches.
