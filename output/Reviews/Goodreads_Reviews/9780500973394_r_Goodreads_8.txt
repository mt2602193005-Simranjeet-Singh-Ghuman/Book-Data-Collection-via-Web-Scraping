the first book I considered a favourite that, on reread, no longer really holds the same esteem. a humument is wonderful and parts of it are very emotionally resonant with me - in general tom phillips' art style is to my taste. however, the project itself, with its revisions, is muddled and, despite what phillips says, is impossible to read as a conjoined text with a real narrative. this makes sense - phillips is not a writer, and his artwork is easy enough to enjoy assuming none of it has meaning relative to the pages before and after. but he is not quite able to shake silly impulses - very early into the book, phillips "discovers" 9/11 in a victorian novel - it's cheesy and undermines the "toge and irma" conceit. i discovered this book in university, recommended by a professor, and approached it as a complete text, but it's closer to lyric poetry than narrative, and as such the overtures to narrative look lazy and half-formed. still a gorgeous work of art, and one i'll return to. this was a library copy, but i'd like to cut up a copy with scissors and leave only the pages i like. or, alternatively, cut out everything i liked and stick it into a notebook of my own. if i could paint, i'd love to do something like this, but i can't, and must suffer from phillips' whims and occasional misjudgment in creating something transcendent.
