A 3.5. I have mixed feelings about this book, but the good outweighs the meh. Fist, it's SO Santa Barbara. Sometimes endearingly, other times eye-rollingly. (Westmont College is mentioned about four times, at times a bit derisively, and that college is too complex to reduce to evangelical narrowmindedness. It had a women's studies department at one point, and not for teaching home ec.) And although the text includes a thoughtful exploration of what it really means to be going through certain critical moments in the Episcopalian Church in the 1990s, church politics wear me out. Finally, Gallagher has this annoying habit of including names upon names as she spins her various yarns. First and last, if she knows them, and often several in a single paragraph. I don't know these folks. I don't know if I'm supposed to remember them. I can't remember who visits the soup kitchen, who is in church leadership, who is a church member with what horrible disease. Am I suppose to remember? I assume not, then later someone reappears and I feel a little behind. BUT. Gallagher has fresh perspective on liturgical time, a proper reverence for ritual (especially when it goes imperfectly, which really seems quite appropriate), fabulous writing when not bogged down in names, and touching vulnerability. She offers up startling gems of wisdom and insight, to the point that I stopped reading to get up to find a pen and underline. Worth the read.
