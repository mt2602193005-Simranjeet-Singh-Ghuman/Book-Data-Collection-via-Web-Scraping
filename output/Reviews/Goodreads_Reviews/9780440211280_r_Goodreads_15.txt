"We shall have our manhood. We shall have it or the earth will be levelled by our attempts to gain it!" After reading "Soul on Ice," I see what the New York Times meant in hailing it as "an authentic voice of black rage....it's one of the 10 best books of the year." (1968) This read is very complex in the array of emotions it conveys. You can feel embarrassed, outraged, disgusted, informed, inspired, understood, and chosen all at the same time. Though you may not always agree, and often times I didn't, he is masterful in how he explains his points. "The White Race and it's hero's," "Rallying Around the Flag," and "The Initial reactions on the assassination of Malcolm X," read like they could have been written today! Eldridge Cleaver's writing is amazing! I see how his role was influential to the Black Panthers, but I also see how as Huey Newton mentioned, he could have done so much more even! (I also haven't read much else by him, and this is my first in depth exposure to him, but I also hear that his later life took quite a turn.) There's alot to unpack in this one, and just like after reading "Revolutionary Suicide," I am inspired to learn more! Lastly, it's interesting reading about how some of these revolutionary minds felt about one another. Eldridge Cleaver has some strong commentary on James Baldwin, and clearly admires Malcolm X (who wouldn't!) There are also some things to be critical of: his thoughts on homosexuality being one. Can't wait to reflect and journal some more about this one!
