This was unlike any British Police Procedural that I’ve read. We are introduced to Ray Flowers and those who live in his village, his late Aunt Mathilda’s home, where he has gone after a horrific attack that has scarred him. In parallel we learn of the life of Kitty, the ghost he first learns about through his late Aunt’s journals, who once lived at the cottage. Fascinated, Ray decides to investigate Kitty’s death. At the same time, there is the investigation into his attack. The characters are very well written. The story was told in alternating chapters and for the first half of the book was a slow read. It is Kitty’s story that is truly tragic and becomes what kept me reading for the second half of the book. This is the first time I’ve read this Author and will read the next book because the background story, Ray and the other characters in the village have good foundations for a good series. The treasure at the back of the book is worth reading, even just for amusement. It is the most comprehensive glossary of terms to ‘translate’ to US Readers (I’m not one), which I find interesting because you rarely find that in an American book, even when they are using specific regional language that doesn’t make sense to International readers. It was an unexpected treat at the end of a sad story.
