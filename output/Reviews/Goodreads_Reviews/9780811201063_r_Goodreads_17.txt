A friend of mine sent me a passage from this book and I thought, oh! He's cooking with gas: The land of opportunity has become the land of senseless sweat and struggle. The goal of all our striving has long been forgotten. We no longer wish to succor the oppressed and homeless; there is no room in this great, empty land for those who, like our forefathers before us, now seek a place of refuge. . . . Where is the democratic spirit? Where are the leaders? That turned out to be on page 20 and was sadly the book's high point. Henry Miller travels around America and hates what he finds -- fair, I suppose...except he sets out determined to hate it. He is angry that he has had to leave France. Why did he have to leave France? It's 1939. He's completely indifferent to the Nazis and the horrors they are currently committing; he doesn't like Hitler but he thinks Hitler and Roosevelt are basically one and the same, which, oy. He'd fit right in today, wouldn't he? This is the attitude with which Miller embarks on his travels: I have never found a park in America that filled me with anything but sadness or ennui. Bro, that's a skill issue. He goes on to have some Tumblr-story-type conversations with a few random Americans, but mostly seems disinterested in the people he meets. He's more interested in hanging out with a few select artists, whose work he praises -- but without ever conveying what is wonderful about their art. By the time I got to the end, where Miller is lamenting how beautiful the South would be if only slavery had been allowed to continue, I was beyond done. But Miller adds a grace note by listing, "for the reader's delectation," descriptions of some of the winners of Guggenheim fellowships for the year he was denied one to write this book. In the context of a better book, this might be boldly cheeky, but in the context of this one, it's merely the whiniest most petty bitch behavior I have ever seen. Really, Anaïs??
