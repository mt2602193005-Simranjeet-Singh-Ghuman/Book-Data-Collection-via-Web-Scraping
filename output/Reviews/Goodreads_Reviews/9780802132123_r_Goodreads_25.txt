Things I knew before picking this book up: - it’s considered a classic gay novel - it’s the first in a series of 5 following the character of George Miles - it was probably going to be quite sexually explicit and shocking. Things I felt while reading this book: - often really bored, confused or frustrated by the switching of viewpoints - angry at the objectification of George (not so much by the characters in the story, more by Cooper himself) - angry at the lack of agency George has as a character - angry at how George is constantly described as pure and beautiful when that image of pure beauty is pale, blonde and thin. My main issue is that I just didn’t believe any of it. It’s a story of abuse. But the abuse happens in a world where virtually every young man is gay, where their sexuality is not only just assumed but also accepted. Honestly the scene where George’s ex-girlfriend talks to him about how she knows he now likes boys and how he’s clearly going to become boyfriends with one of their teachers. It’s laughably inaccurate to real life, condescending to one of the very few female characters in the novel, and deeply troubling as it depicts a picture of abuse as if it’s just acceptable and fun. Which made it impossible for me to believe anything.
