This is a very sweet adoption story. Though the specifics are vague, it appears this child is adopted as a toddler so he is well aware of his surroundings in South America (?) and of all the fears and excitements that come along with the big change from the orphanage to his new parents' home. Throughout his long trip, he holds the photo of his new parents, so he recognizes them when they pick him up at the airport, but that doesn't diminish the trepidation he feels at being suddenly embraced, however lovingly, by a new life. However, he soon begins to learn his adoptive mother's smile, he loves how his adoptive father rocks him in his chair... And it is the big red dog who really helps him connect at first. Slowly, the fear melts away as familiarity grows, and by the end of the tale the loving family unit is established. I thought this was a beautifully told story, both in terms of word choice and sentiment. I appreciated how the frightening aspects of adoption were not brushed away, but that the tale had a hopeful tone throughout. It's also a helpful addition to the adoption-themed stories because, unlike most of those I've read, the child in this story is not a baby when he is adopted so it presents another facet for parents depending on their own adoptive child's age.
