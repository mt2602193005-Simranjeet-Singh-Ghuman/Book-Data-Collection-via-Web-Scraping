Brian Bóruma mac Cennétig, also known as Brian Bóramha (Brian Boru), was the High King of the Irish during the years 1002-1014. Boru is held in high regard by both the Norsemen he fought against and by the native Irish whom he tried to bond into one entity known as Ireland. Morgan Llywelyn's "Lion of Ireland" reminds me very much of Mary Stewart's Crystal Cave series about Merlin and King Arthur. It is a historical fiction with an air of magic and fey in the background. This excellent story traces the youth of Brian, as the son of Cennétig mac Lorcáin, King of the Dalcassians. Yet, Cennétig is a vassal of the King of Munster. At this time, the Irish were under assault, as were the English, by Viking invasion and settlement. Operating out of Dublin, the Norsemen are terrorizing the island. In time, Brian will rise to become King of Munster and then, eventually, High King of Ireland and one of the greatest Norsemen-killers in Irish history. I shall not spoil this story for you. But it is a great one. The characters are memorable, though not always likable, and the actions that unfold show how fractious and divisive Irish politics were during this time. I really enjoyed his writing style, and this story was hard to put down. If you are a fan of the Mary Stewart series or would like to know about the story of the famous Brian Boru, then you can't go wrong with this superb book. A welcome addition to my library of good historical fiction.
