Better conceived than executed. The setting is a colony planet that was settled by ships carrying settlers in suspended animation. On landing, the crew establishes themselves as rulers and the settlers as semi-slaves, or at least repressed and exploited. Even more so as the settlers are used as a source of organs for transplant to extend the lives of the crew. Capital punishment is imposed for even minor offences in order to keep the transplants flowing, which is sold to the populace as essential for law and order. Of course there's a group plotting its overthrow and our protagonist stumbles into it. It's raided and many of the would be revolutionaries arrested. Our MC escapes and in the process discovers a psychic power, a kind of invisibility. He learns to depend on it and sets out to rescue his newfound comrades, or at least the women in the group that he's taken a shine to. An additional complication is the titular Gift From Earth that arrives part way through the narrative - a range of synthetic human organs. Will this make the transplants redundant? Prompt the revolution? The story's pretty straightforward from there on, a pretty reasonable but, exotic setting aside, unremarkable action thriller. What sets it apart is that it discusses what happens after the dust settles. There's a realisation that things won't change overnight and the necessity of an alliance with a crew leader. So better than many but not quite enough to crack four stars.
