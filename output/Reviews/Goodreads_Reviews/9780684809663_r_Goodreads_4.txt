I expected more from this book. The sub title is "A Menoir of Growing up With Janet Flanner and Natalia Danesi Murray. In truth William Murray did not grow up with Janet Flanner. He was already 14 when she came into his mother's life and even during their relationship which lasted until Janet's death, she was frequently absent. I had hoped for an interesting story about a boy growing up with two mothers when gay relationships were not at all acceptable. Instead the story revolves in large part around the struggles the two women, and later William Murray himself, engaged in as they tried to earn their livings through writing. I did find a lot of the details about working for The New Yorker to be of interest, but over-all the book seemed to be about financial worries and even in that realm I could not be sympathetic as Natalia, at one point owned an apartment in New York and a house on Fire Island as she spent a large amount of her time living in Rome. Jannet Flanner lived in hotels where all her needs were catered to by the staff- not what I call financial difficulty.
