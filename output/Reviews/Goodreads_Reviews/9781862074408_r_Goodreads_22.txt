A tightly written yet charming memoir of Diana Athill’s career as an editor at André Deutsch during the middle of the 20th century. The first half provides a chronological account of her career, the second profiles the six authors whom Athill considers to have had the most significant or lasting impact on her. It is set during a period of gentility and privilege in the publishing world: Athill retired in the 1990s, just as the digital revolution was reshaping the industry. I enjoyed this gentle read and yet was confounded by the number of typographical and editorial errors. Missing commas, “that” instead of “than,” and, bizarrest of all, the assertion that Columbus landed on the island of Dominica in 1643! How did so many errors slip through? Perhaps someone assumed that “Britain’s best editor” would edit herself, obviating the need for proper scrutiny. Whatever the reason, I felt obliged to knock off one star for this unintended irony.
