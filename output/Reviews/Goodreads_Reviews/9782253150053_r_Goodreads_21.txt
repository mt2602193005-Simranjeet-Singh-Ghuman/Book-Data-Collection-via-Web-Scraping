Oh, wow. I was not expecting to find this book, more than 10 years after its publication (and references to events current at the time), still so unbelievably relevant and meaningful today. But I think this is required reading for anyone today. ANYONE. When you look at the world as it is today, and all the controversy over Islam and the Middle East conflicts, it's hard to understand where it all came from, and this book breaks it down, and theorizes that it all has to do with the question of one's identity used as a weapon against those who are different and perceived to be a threat. Even when they aren't! And it also offers some suggestions for how we can all accept our multiple identities and not feel threatened. I should also state that I am not generally a fan of manifesto-type books (Aimé Cesaire, Frantz Fanon, Zola's "J'accuse," etc). I get bored several pages in; I just can't take the neverending ideology and indignant declarations. But this, on the other hand, actually hooked me and drew me in and I could not agree more with everything Maalouf has to say. Highly, HIGHLY recommended!
