It's a fantastic story, with rich and original characters that will stay with you for a long time. I didn't rate it higher because everything felt too angsty and unnecessary complicated. There was not a single conversation that didn't sound like a philosophical debate. I wanted more simplicity and directness. Regarding Phoenix, it was cruel to introduce such an enigmatic character and then let him die . TW: when Henry was 14, his father brought home a sex worker and stayed watching while she raped (I have no other way to call it) Henry . Longer blurb: "NO RESPECTABLE MAN WILL HAVE YOU," HER MOTHER SAID. Rachel Parrish knew better. While growing up in her mother's house of ill repute, she'd learned that anything could be bought . . . even the kind of life that most people take for granted. Determined to leave her past behind, she heads for the Wyoming wilderness and advertises for a man to share her future . . . a respectable man who is desperate enough to overlook her parentage. The perfect man answers her ad and marries her --- in the name of his angry, less forgiving brother. Henry Ashford, identical twin of an English earl, has battled demons all his life. Exiled from England and desperate for an inheritance, he cannot claim unless he meets the earl's terms, he arrives in Cheyenne to demand his birthright and escape the nightmares of his past. Instead, he discovers that his destiny is bound to a captivating woman who challenges his anger and tames the demons in his soul . . . until he learns of the one betrayal he cannot forgive.
