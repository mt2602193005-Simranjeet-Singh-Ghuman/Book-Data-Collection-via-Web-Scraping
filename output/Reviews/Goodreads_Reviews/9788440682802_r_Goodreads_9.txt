Barely 3 stars. In the story, we follow Nigel an astronaut who gets to make first contact with alien artificial intelligence. Then we get to explore the consequences of the contact and subsequent missions. However, there are reasons this forsnt hold up well. I found the frankly false and made up medical information (dying from lupus) the most distracting factor of this story and really made me question the crediblity of the author on anything he presented as fact including his physicis. Next, the unlikability of main character was also very distractinng. Im not talking about my dislike but rather how often it was mentioned by other characters in the story over and over and over again. Member of his thruple semed to want to control his grief and his other emotions, NASA evaluators continually told him he wasnt qualified for missions and newspapers kept telling him he didnt think like a scientist yet they kept sending him on missions. You just couldnt get away from it. Classic case of being told rather than shown. Overall, this story spent too much time on not-science in a very unbelievable and often distracting way. I would recommend passing on this one.
