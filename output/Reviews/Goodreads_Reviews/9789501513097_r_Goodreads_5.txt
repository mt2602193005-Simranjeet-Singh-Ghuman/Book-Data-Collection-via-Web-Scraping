Read: 3 stars Re-read: 3 stars Okay, I've read this book many times over the years. Previously I didn't love it because it was a western. Well, now I like westerns and I still don't love it. Why? Well, let me tell you. 1) Weird plot happenings that didn't add to the story and just made me go huh? For example, 1) twin villain near the end seeking money and revenge, well, mostly just money. The hero and heroine could have easily gotten together without it at that point; 2) the heroine's panther. really pointless other than to show that the big gunfighter is scared of a little ole panther. WTF? Who wouldn't be scared of a panther living in some crazy girl's house!! Why are these things in the book? They aren't cute or interesting and didn't advance the plot that I could see, just annoying. 2) Parents that don't speak for 10 years while living in the same house and make their daughter (heroine) be their conduit for conversation or sniping, as it mainly seemed to be? WTF. That's just irrational, not to mention child abuse. 3) The heroine's meddling. yes, I guess it was supposed to be cute and quirky but it was annoying. And when it came to the hero, pretty much emasculating. She's so determined to protect him that she won't let this famous gunfighter have his own showdown??? Give your head shake, girl. Why don't you just put a dress on him and call him missus. 4) The hero's lost family. It was okay, I guess but I would have been okay without it. Not everything needs to be neatly wrapped up by the end of the book. Despite this, there were things I like about this book. Mainly Angel. He's a great hero, loved him in the previous book in the series, loved him in this one. And I liked the idea of the heroine who's unremarkable at first but who grows on the hero until he pretty much thinks the sun and moon rise and set especially for her. And I'm a sucker for marriage of convenience/forced wedding plots. It's all so 1800's crazy.
