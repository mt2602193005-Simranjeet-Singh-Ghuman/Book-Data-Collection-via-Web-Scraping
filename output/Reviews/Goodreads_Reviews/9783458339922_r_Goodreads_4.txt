The story begins at a house party given by Lady Midhurst, an aristocratic widow of irreproachable virtue and fabulous wealth, devoted to her only daughter, Terry. At the party is her old friend Andrew Fairlie, who has been invaluable to her since the death of her husband in WW1. At the house party is Andrew’s beautiful young wife, Rosie, who has never been invited to a party with her husband before (she is not quite of the right class). The first part of the book moves rather slowly and it is a while before we find out what is going on. Most of the first chapter, for instance, is devoted to people complaining about the fact that unripe gooseberries have been served for dessert at every meal in various forms, none very appetising. We never really get to the bottom of the gooseberry thing. The story proceeds slowly and it isn’t until halfway through the book that we actually get to the Jasmine Farm and find out what its significance is. The book is very funny, mainly because of the character of Mumsie, Rosie’s mother, who is pure joy. I would have liked the whole book to be about her really.
