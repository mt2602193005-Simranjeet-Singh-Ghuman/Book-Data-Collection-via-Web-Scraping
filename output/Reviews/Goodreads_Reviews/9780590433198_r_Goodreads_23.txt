After finding Over Sea, Under Stone woefully boring, I'm glad I gave this classic series one more try. It feels like this is the true start to the series, written several years later and followed in rapid succession by the rest. I would advise others to start here; the prior book's concluding event is mentioned in passing but is not necessary for this story. This is the epitome of the Chosen One trope. Our eleven-year-old protagonist is pre-ordained for his role, is led by the hand along his new path, is told what to do every step of the way, and all he has to do is remember what he was told five minutes prior and understand to apply it. He takes little independent action, needing only to accept affairs as they are. Despite this deficiency of character agency, the book is captivating. It's largely archetypal, built on a high level from numerous traditions of fantasy and folklore. It dreamily conveys wonder and primal magic with a decidedly British fae atmosphere. It's utterly plain in its delineation of good vs. evil, white vs. black. The portrayal of a large family engaged in British country life forms the heart of its charm, and I enjoyed the sibling relationships more than in the prior book. I can't see children reading this today. It will remain a classic of children's fantasy literature but I don't expect it to take in readers of its target age any longer.
