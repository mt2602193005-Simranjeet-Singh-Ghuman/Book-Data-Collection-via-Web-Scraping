I fear I may be a little biased on this book, having spent many a Friday night in my 20s at the legendary Jabez Clegg nightclub in Manchester, only to belatedly find out that Jabez Clegg is the extremely likeable 'good egg' protagonist of this novel set in the late 1700s/early 1800s. Having been brought up with a grandmother who spoke almost exclusively in Lancashire dialect, I enjoyed the many dialect passages which certainly added to the flavour of the prose (confused only by the choice of 'oi' in e.g. 'loike' which I think would be better represented by 'aah' to capture the long drawn-out vowel in 'like' rendered as 'laahk' in dialect). There were lots of dialect words I haven't heard in a very long time so it was lovely to see them on the page all these years later. I feel like this book is a lost classic - I wish we had been introduced to this book at school as (certainly for Northern souls) it felt as relevant and humorous as Jane Austen, and contains lots of history that is in danger of being forgotten, such as the Peterloo massacre. Exiled Mancunians may also enjoy making a little map in their head of all the different places in this book - I recognised most of the road names and place names. Summary - a gem of a classic, for Northern folk in particular (by 'eck!)
