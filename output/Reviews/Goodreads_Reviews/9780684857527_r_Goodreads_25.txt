Superlative. Brilliant. Excellent. Best western of all time? It sure as hell is the best one I’ve ever read. And more than just the greatest western novel of all time, it is the most epic. I think of other great western writers like Cormac McCarthy, Charles Portis, Glendon Swarthout and Louis L’Amour who excelled in shorter works, but McMurtry gave this the War and Peace / One Hundred Years of Solitude treatment and left nothing out, this has all the elements of not only a great western novel but of a great novel period. There is love and hatred, indifference and unrequited affection. Violence and death. Friendship, loyalty, duty, crime and punishment, endurance and austerity. There is humor and loss and injustice. McMurtry leaves nothing off the table and this is exceptional in most every way. This novel about a cattle drive from southern Texas to Montana in the late 1870s becomes a morality play about most everything. In Gus McCrae, McMurtry has given us one of the great American characters, rivaling Tom Sawyer, Captain Ahab, Jay Gatsby, Nick Adams and Holden Caulfield. Sharing the stage with Gus is a cast of players that encompasses the spectrum of emotions from heroic, to comic to villainous. In Blue Duck, the author has provided us with an antagonist every bit as nefarious and scary as hell as any other bad guy to be found in world literature. Also noteworthy is Clara, who serves as a perfect foil to Gus and Call, and McMurtry himself provided the explanation that while Gus and Woodrow were inspired by historic figures, they were patterned after Don Quixote and Sancho Panza and knowing this made perfect sense. Highly, highly recommended.
