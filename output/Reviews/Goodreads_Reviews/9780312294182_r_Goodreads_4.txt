The Troubles is a beast of a book, covering the main 30 years, with some additional parts included for older historical context. It's a difficult slog to get through; not only because of the complex nature of the subject matter, but Tim Pat Coogan's writing style isn't always the most accessible, which is surprising given that he's a journalist first and foremost. He sometimes gets lost in the mind-numbing detail that he delves into, with so many tangents that veer off here and there. The date history jumping to and fro also makes it difficult to follow at times (one page, you can be in 1972 and the next, either in 1986 or back to the 1920s). The painfully slow pacing of the chapters can also make it feel never ending too, as do the copious acronyms. That said, it's still worth a read for the sheer amount of detail, and perhaps for the commentary that other Irish writers have either ignored or skipped past. Coogan gets a lot of flack from academics and from writing from a Nationalist point of view, but if you're interested in Northern Ireland history that's focused on the Troubles era, the IRA, and further exploration from the Catholic side, it feels like it fills in more of the gaps. Definitely not for the beginner reader though!
