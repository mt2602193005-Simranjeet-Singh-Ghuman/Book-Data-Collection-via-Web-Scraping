In this epic fantasy novel the adventuress Aleizon Ailix Ayndra meets the conquering Ailixond, Lord of Safi on the road to Haramin, and rises to a position of influence. The story interweaves the politics of sex and power into a canvas of ancient history. The tale is clearly inspired by the exploits of Alexander the Great, to whom it is dedicated. The heroine also bears an intriguing resemblance to the author, with a number of points in common other than their names. This trilogy apparently is the only fantasy fiction that Spedding ever wrote, though she is credited with several non-fiction books, some written in Spanish. Last time I searched, she was resident in Bolivia and in trouble with the authorities for alleged marujana possession. The opening scene, in which some minor characters forage for cabbage and cook a meal, immediatly establishes Spedding as a talented writer and stylist. The story, full of desperate events, it told with a kind of dry humour. Definitely worth a read.
