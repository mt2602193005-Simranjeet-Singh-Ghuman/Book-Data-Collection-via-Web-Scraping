As much as I love Burroughs, I have to come to a conclusion. He’s not the easiest writer to enjoy; and I’m happy for it; His prose is as raw as it minimal and just as poignant. The book is a fragmentary collection of his dreams. Along side any notes of relevance to his life or creative process. Surreal dreams of beautifully, damaged boys and ceaseless, relentless interior of the dead land. In my opinion, and in now way of being critical, these pages were the hardest to read of all the books I read of him. Six books including the out of print “ The book of breathing”. So much that it reminded me that I tried reading the junky three times in my late teen years until I was able to enjoy the process of settling into his rhythm and mayhem. But, it’s the last 20 pages, where he discusses his real life, eluding a car crash, the suicide of his friend, the memory of his Cat Ruski, drifting on the lake and hoping the aliens are never misfortune enough to be discovered by the self devouring awfulness and self destructive race , that made me realize how emotionally raw he has always been behind the stone cold visage of Aquarian nonchalance. I adore him, and this sentiment never felt truer since I read “The cat Inside”. I’ll keep the book close to to my bed because I adore the old man, and reading his dreams do keep me, if only fleeting me, with his company.
