This is a book that I would have been unlikely to read, but it was a selection for a local book club and I liked it. There were some issues that prevented it from being rated higher, mostly a lack of editing. There were so many themes and a few info dumps that were in need of a red pencil. The story itself takes place in Buffalo at the beginning of the 20th century. At this time, Buffalo was a huge center of shipping and wealth. It was also the site of the Pan-Am Exposition, a display of wealth and the wonders of the modern world. There is also the development of hydroelectric power at nearby Niagara Falls. Louisa Barrett is the headmistress of Macauley, a girls' school, and is influential in the education and development of these young women. She has a unique position due to her school and is treated almost as an equal to the men of the school board. A mysterious death piques her curiosity and sets off a chain of events that has repercussions for uncovering a secret that she's spent years concealing. There are a lot of important themes - class struggles, the subtle and not-so-subtle sexism and racism that was part of the fabric of life, balancing progress with responsible use of natural resources. I almost think that the story would have benefited from a narrowing of focus. Overall, it was good, especially if you're interested in the early 1900's period.
