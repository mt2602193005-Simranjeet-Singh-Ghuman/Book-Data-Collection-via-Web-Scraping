This is a 1934 novel of two parts. The first part set at an interminable country house party and then a luxurious London mansion features a revelation about two members of the group that causes the hostess to flee to her provincial farm in France where in the second part she tries to reconcile herself to her past trials and this current ordeal, whilst at the same time fending off various ghastly visitors. For me this lacked the lightness of touch we have come to expect from Elizabeth von Armin, the characters all rather grotesque who didn't quite ring true and despite a passing mention of concentration camps there was no sense of the rising tension growing in Europe and as a consequence we are left a conclusion that felt rather hollow. (Knowing as we do that everything is going to come crashing down in very short order). Not one of her best works I'm sorry to say.
