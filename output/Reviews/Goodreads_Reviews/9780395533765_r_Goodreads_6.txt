This is a book for an odd niche. It covers the most difficult IDs in birding. Each section is succinct and efficient, so as a guide to, say, the medium-sized terns, it's a quicker (yet still detailed) guide than Terns of North America (but if you really want to learn terns, you'll need that book, too). In that sense, this book is geared for birders moving beyond basic field guides, but not quite going for family-specific guides. The big upside: Kaufman does an excellent job of delineating what you need to pay attention to and what you should be wary about (the way lighting can affect traditional field marks on some birds, for example). It's guidance from someone who has done it all and who writes from in-field experience. I do wish the book had color images (maybe the new edition does?), and I think there have been some relevant taxonomic changes since this one was published. Even so, it's a valuable book, and I'll likely reference specific chapters as (or hopefully before) needed.
