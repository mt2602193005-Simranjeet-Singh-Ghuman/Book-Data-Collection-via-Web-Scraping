Red Dragon must rank as one of the greatest police procedural novels of all time. I have not read that many. But the sheer amount of evidence (especially forensic) and array of methods (including profiling and behavioral science) used by the team of detectives to track down a serial killer makes Red Dragon very very absorbing. Thomas Harris has some ingenious ideas. I feel like I could do a decent job investigating a murder case based on what I read in The Red Dragon. Harris also creates some intriguing characters - a serial killer for whom we are supposed to feel some empathy on account of the fact that Harris dedicates many chapters to his terrible childhood where he is subjected to unspeakable humiliations by a sadistic grandmother and ignored by his socialite mother. There is the strange and feisty blind woman who falls for the serial killer. On their first date, she enjoys it when he is excited by watching her fondling a tigers testicles! The tortured and mysterious detective who profiles serial killers by putting himself in their shoes on a level that threatens his own sanity. And of course, Hannibal Lecter! I watched the fantastic Michael Mann film first. So I knew what was going to happen in many places. But the book was still very surprising because Mann did not incorporate everything in the book into his film.
