I'm not sure what to make of this book. It was certainly an inspiring read. The author pays tribute to her mother who's faith helped her family to survive the horrors of a Japanese concentration camp during World War II. To her credit the author admits in her preface that her memories may not be entirely accurate She was only about 8 or 10 during the period of much of the book. It was a little frustrating not knowing how much of the book actually happened and how much was faulty memory. I know that people tend to remember traumatic experiences in great detail even when they occur at a young age. The book tends to meander a bit towards the end and may have been better as a series of talks (from which the book was taken.) Overall a good read though. A word of warning, there are some disturbing parts, as can be expected in a book about the horrors of war, so not recommended for children.
