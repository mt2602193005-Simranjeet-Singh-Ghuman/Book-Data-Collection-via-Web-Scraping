This book could not be written today. It ignores many essential components of the Jacksonian era, in particular the expansion of the frontiers and its violent nature. Slavery only gets brief treatment towards the end. And yet despite some of these omissions, which seem much more glaring than in the 40s, I found the book to be a true pleasure to read. Schlesinger is excellent at prose writing, simultaneously rich with detail yet smooth to read. Even though his central thesis in this book, that of Jacksonian politics being driven from the East, has been chipped away over the years, I found many of his other arguments and perspectives valuable. In particular, the final sections covering the transformation of the Whigs and the social institutions of the early republic (such as law and religion) were very valuable. Given the essentially dated nature of the book, I'm not sure I would recommend it to someone seeking to understand the era as a port of first call. However, the prose and arguments were well made and I have to give it due credit for those reasons.
