It felt sort of like reading the love child of Lucifer's Hammer and the old TV series V, with a bit of the Matrix thrown in. Silverberg provides a strong message about human motivation and the need for a connected society, yet the narrative jumps around quite a bit over an overly extended timeline, and his naming conventions for the family of main characters can be confusing at times - I found that there were way too many characters named Anson, for example. Still, the story moves forward at a brisk pace and it is an enjoyable read.
