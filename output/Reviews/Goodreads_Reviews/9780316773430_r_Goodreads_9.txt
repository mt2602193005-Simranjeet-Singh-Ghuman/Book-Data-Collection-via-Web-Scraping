Arthur Schlesinger is a giant among American historians, and this book lives up to that reputation. It’s very readable, and very intelligently written. Even 80 years after it was published, it still provides a useful perspective on American history and on contemporary America. I think Schlesinger’s views on antebellum politics are convincing and correct, despite the fact that they have fallen out of favor in the modern interpretation of history. Therefore, it is a useful and important book to read, in my view. That said, this book is probably one of the more clearly biased history books that I have ever read. It’s not as though he makes stuff up, he still sticks to the facts and makes effective arguments, despite his bias. But clearly Schlesinger gives no credence to the views opposing Jacksonianism at all, and automatically assumes pure intentions of the Jacksonians. It doesn’t really get in the way of the crux of the book, but it is still very obvious. The one (out of quite a few) example that stands out in my mind is Schlesinger implying that Silas Wright was literally Abraham Lincoln. That’s kind of egregious. Also, this book was written 80 years ago, so Schlesinger’s priorities are much different than they would be had he written the book today. Race is hardly talked about (though he talks about slavery extensively), and Indian Removal is not touched upon at all, which is not something that would, or should fly in an Antebellum history book today. So, one must prepare themselves for that, although I would argue that Schlesinger’s arguments are still valid, despite him overlooking those fundamental issues. This is a great book overall, one of the best I have ever read. This is an absolute must read for anyone who seeks to understand Jacksonian politics, and to trace how we got to where we are politically today. Would highly recommend. Two thumbs up.
