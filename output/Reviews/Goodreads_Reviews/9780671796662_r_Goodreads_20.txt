He can do whatever he wants. It all comes so easy to him. He’s just not a basketball player. The Jordan Rules follows the Chicago Bulls during the 1990/91 season. It's a chronological accounting of what happened and why. I found the book boring at times, wishing the author would've decided to skip some of the less exciting months. Or at least decided to give them less focus. The two most interesting characters are Jordan himself and Phil Jackson. Jordan comes off as a flawed human being, who's sometimes an asshole (to no surprise of this reader). Jackson comes off as a smart coach, whose mind games must've have been annoying at times. Overall, I found this book to be interesting and good, but not great. If you're enamoured with the Jordan era Bulls, you might like it more.
