This is the first book I've read about dyslexia and I found it a very informative introduction. The writer runs a reading institute that helps dyslexics learn to function normally. Since I'm unfamiliar with the different management strategies for dyslexia it's tough for me to wholeheartedly recommend this book. Positive aspects are the way in which dyslexia presented as merely a different way of thinking and not a disability. Davis believes that dyslexics are visual thinkers and in order to adapt to a verbal thinkers world they create solutions that present with various symptoms and disorientation all under the name dyslexia. The premise of his institute is to teach a Dyslexic to orient themselves and remove the bad habits that have been formed to cope. His teaching ideas seem to have a lot of merit to them. Negative aspects are the lack of reference material. He makes a lot of claims about dyslexia that are unsupported and almost sound like an attempt to build up the dyslexic by telling them how awesome they are. While that's a great approach with someone who probably has a low self-esteem I'm just not sure how truthful it is with out anything backing it up.
