I wouldn't call it a book, merely a text posing as a memoir, since reading it is an actual stylistic nightmare... however it is still worth reading for few interesting facts: 1) Ulam, being inherently Dionysian in his academic approach, correctly and repeatedly forecasts the inevitable rise of Apollonian strategists in academia. "There are so few of us, and so many of them" as he points out. 2) This could be a poorly written biography of von Neumann and even poorly written it's always a pleasure to read about this guy. 3) Getting an insight into Los Alamos dynamics and people who created it. Not being a physicist or a mathematician myself, I greatly appreciated being introduced to Gamow, Fermi or Everett.
