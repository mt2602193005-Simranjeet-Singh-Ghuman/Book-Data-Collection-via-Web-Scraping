Gothic Romance I first read this book sometime in the early 70s as a young teen. I had a friend who loved all forms of romance and this was one of her favorites. I remembered thinking it was a deep novel, but then I was all of 13 or 14 at the time. I decided to try it again, and found it to be not quite as perfect as I remembered, but enjoyed it nevertheless. Helena is an English girl with an Oxford educated father and a mother from the Black Forest of Germany. On the night of the seventh moon, revellers would go into the street celebrating the night that the norse god Loki was afoot causing mischief. She had been at a convent where her mother was also schooled at the time, and a man took her off to his hunting lodge. It was a magical night that allowed Helena to keep her virginity only due to a warning from the family housekeeper. Years later, Helena would meet Maximilian again, and they were married. Before their honeymoon was over, Max had the leave to go to the aide of his father, the Duke. She was told that their love must remain a secret. Not long after Max left, a note came supposedly from Max telling her to go stay with friends. She was drugged and eventually convince that her idyl in the forest never occurred. Of all the gothic romances I have ever read, the plot of this story was one of the most complex and detailed. That was both a blessing and a curse for the readability of the story. It was well written, but at times a little too convoluted to be completely enjoyable. There is a HEA after a maze like tale and much quiet gnashing of teeth, and any physical love was no more than suggested. Four stars for an oldie, but goodie.
