A detective novel that takes place in a far-future Earth where humans have been forced to live underground so that the entire surface of the planet can be used for farming. This is an overpopulated Earth, where as a matter of courtesy, you don't look at your neighbor just so that you can give each other some sort of privacy. It's also an imagined Earth, but a frightening possible one. I liked this book. I'm a fan of Asimov's work but this one is a little different. And it's hard work to make a detective novel that feels like an actual mystery when you can spirit any technological solution out of nowhere to explain how you caught the bad guy, which Asimov is careful not to do. A perfect weekend read, scratching the itch of those who love crime novels and those who love Sci Fi because both genres fare well in this book. Of course, as all Asimov novels, it shows its age here and there, at times even feeling a bit Jetsonian, as it pretty much portrays the sci-fi visions of the 60s. Which, I happen to think, made it even more entertaining.
