I originally started this book in Feb 2013 and didn't get far before I set it aside to possibly attempt another time. It was a challenge that was the deciding factor in pulling it back off the shelf and wiping the dust off the cover. I'm glad I did. I enjoyed this intricate tale of three people embroiled in the dynamics of a marriage with two wives amidst the backdrop of a time when having a second wife was beginning to be socially frowned upon. That alone could have made an interesting story, but add the maelstrom of the Partition in the 1940's India and you have a volatile tale. At times the story was riveting, yet at other times a bit dry and I found myself slogging my way through. My loyalties between the two wives shifted from the beginning of the book to the end, and I was much more involved in the power struggle between them than I was the political power struggle. Yet, towards the end of the book, I was drawn into that as well. The immediate days surrounding Partition in August 15, 1947 were horrific. 10,000,000 people were relocated without any organization. One day they had a home and the next they did not. 2,000,000 people (men, women & children) died in the violence that ensued. India was divided by religion. I placed this on my "Canadian connection shelf" because the author was born in Canada.
