Sometimes recommended as a definitive work about the Thai-Chinese experience, I’m not sure how well this sits with me as a reader in the here and now. Basically, it’s a mid-century Thai version of a Victorian epistolary novel of the sort that once entranced girls in nightgowns around the world. The kind that opens with a starry-eyed young immigrant who quickly becomes an increasingly cantankerous, conservative, but ultimately good Chinese immigrant father (how many Sundance movies you seen that in?), and finishes with the best of the future, as represented by a successdaughter who speaks four languages and openly discusses the pill (it was written in the ‘60s) and gets the presumably hot Dark Academia husband (thanks Jo March). That paragraph came out bitchier than expected, but I can’t say this is a bad novel or anything of the sort (and the translation is excellent as well, did my best to cross-reference, solid work for the parts I saw and within my limited knowledge of linguistic subtlety). And I think it does in many ways reflect, honestly, the immigrant experience in Thailand, at least as it is reflected in the people I know (see: my friend’s grouchy Chinese dad who counts the mangoes in the trees to make sure no one’s taking them and grouses about how his family won't eat the sea cucumber). It’s just totally not my speed. I am insufficiently nightgowned.
