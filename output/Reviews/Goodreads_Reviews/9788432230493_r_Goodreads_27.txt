I loved Russian novels when I was young and read and reread every Tolstoy and Dostoevsky and other 18th and 19th century novelists I could get my hands on (in English). When I was in my late twenties, I remembered being so thrilled to have found a "major" Tolstoy that I hadn't read or even known ( Resurrection , which actually turned out to be sort of mediocre). I think I might even have called in sick to stay home and read it, straight through, twice. Finding this book was similar, except better - I've discovered a book that has rocketed right to the top of my most cherished works. It's like an earthquake has changed my mental landscape, making room for the places and people of this book, the story and the issues Vargas Llosa covers, the humanity and heart, spirit and community, good and bad that he packs into the pages. I'm overwhelmed.
