Let’s be fair: Hermann Hesse is working with some genuinely important themes here. The destructive nature of hyper-ambition, the illusion of academic success, and the crushing weight of a rigid, soul-sucking educational system are all fantastic ingredients for a great novel. Unfortunately, Hesse handles these delicate themes with the subtlety of a runaway steamroller. The tragic trajectory of our young hero, Hans Giebenrath, goes something like this: he gets stressed out by his homework, fails to memorize a few lines of classical text, gets so psychologically paralyzed he can't leave the house, drops out, becomes a blue-collar worker, discovers beer, and promptly drowns himself because his veins are apparently now filled with pure alcohol instead of blood. There is absolutely no middle ground in Hesse's universe. You are either a pristine, shimmering academic elite, a radiant genius who memorizes Hebrew verbs, or you are a clumsy, miserable outcast, a broken, alcoholic blacksmith floating face down in a river. It makes you wonder if Mr. Hesse couldn't have chosen a slightly more tangible, plausible situation to show his aim. Instead, the only thing he successfully puts "beneath the wheel" is the poor reader’s interest, intellect, and patience. The book does have a few sparse, genuinely beautiful moments, but they are entirely swallowed by a plot that feels utterly undetermined. It reads as though Hesse started writing with zero outline, lost his notes halfway through, and spent the rest of the novel panicking: "Okay, what now? Let's make the kid sick. Yeah, that sounds deep. What next? He has a bad dad. Good, good. Now let's make him hit some iron. Oh, he’s clumsy at that too? Perfect. Now what? Well, all the great writers kill their heroes at the end of their masterpieces, so let's throw him in the river to ensure my nonsense gets recognized as high art, too." If you are looking for a nuanced, substantively heavy exploration of youth and society, look elsewhere. But if you want to watch a classic German author throw a massive, beautifully written tantrum because high school was really hard, this one is for you.
