My least favourite GH by a long shot. She certainly tried something here, but I'm not sure who the target audience is supposed to be. Half the book follows the romance between a self-absorbed widow and a golden retriever military man. The other half focuses on the Duke of Wellington and the battle of Waterloo, contrasting the shallow regency lifestyle with the atrocities of war. This book was at its best when highlighting these opposing realities, how the upper classes continued on their merry lives in Brussles while the front was moving ever closer. But neither of the actual storylines was very compelling to me. Barbara was such a horrible person for most of the book that I really didn't understand how Charles could feel anything for her. Sure, she's beautiful, but what exactly do you like about her character? There was nothing redeemable about her until the latter chapters, by which point I had basically stopped rooting for the romance regardless. And while I'm not opposed to weaving history lessons in otherwise fictionalized stories and bringing the setting to life, I really didn't require several chapters worth of military scenes, describing the minutiae of the battle down to the movements of individual units. I will be honest here, there was definitely some skim reading happening on my part. Otherwise I would have fallen asleep. It feels unfair to critique this aspect of the book, considering how much research GH clearly invested into it, but at the end of the day it was neither a very good romance nor a very good historical novel - even if I had wanted to read about battle scenes, the level of detail reminded me more of a textbook than any sort of reading-for-pleasure experience. It took me a long time to convince myself to finish this and while I'm glad I did, this will definitely not go on the "to reread once a year" pile with most other GH books.
