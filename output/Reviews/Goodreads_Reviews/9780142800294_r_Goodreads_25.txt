Caroline Alexander’s The Endurance is one of my favorite Age of Exploration books, so I had high hopes for this one. Unfortunately, although the story of the mutiny on the Bounty is iconic, this historical examination just doesn’t convey the same dramatic tension as the Shackleton adventure. Granted, that is a ridiculously high bar. Since there are conflicting accounts from the sailors involved we cannot be certain what exactly led to the mutiny, and therefore the story cannot be accurately told in a linear narrative fashion, even though that would have been a more dramatic format. Instead, Alexander starts in media res with the mission of the Pandora to apprehend the mutineers now living in Tahiti. She then rewinds to provide biographical histories of the principal characters before finally describing the voyage of the Bounty and the mutiny itself. This is followed by a recounting of the court martial of the captured mutineers, which gets a little tedious as each of the participants have their own version of events. I found the most interesting section to be the history of the primary mutineer, Fletcher Christian and some of his shipmates establishing their own settlement on Pitcairn Island with a group of islanders from Tahiti. Again, there are conflicting accounts on what occurred there. Some believe that Christian was eventually killed when the Tahitians resisted being treated like slaves, while others believe his death was the result of violent power struggles between the mutineers.
