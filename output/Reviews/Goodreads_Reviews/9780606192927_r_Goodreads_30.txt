I know this book was meant as a bit of an outlet for Koontz after coming off more exhausting writing like "Dark Rivers of the Heart", but I think it's a disaster. His intent was to make sort of a screwball comedy, and while I like the comedic elements in his book "Mr. Murder", I found this one exhausting for me to read. The main problem is the lead female character Del. She's annoying. INCREDIBLY annoying. Imagine if every time you talked to a friend or significant other, he/she said something like, "Well, Steve would agree with that." But you had no idea who Steve was and had to ask. Again, imagine this happening EVERY time you talked. If I was the main character, I would be making EVERY effort to get as far away from this person as possible. When you finally reach the end of this dreck, you learn something REALLY incredulous that somewhat helps to explain why Del is such a weird individual. As if a cloth doll coming to life wasn't incredulous enough. And that's problem #2 - the doll/entity is a VERY small part of the story. It's just a backdrop for the main character, Vietnamese-American Tommy, to have conversations with Del. I would estimate 90% of the book revolves around silly conversations and 10% is action. No joke. Added bonus: I listened to part of this as an audiobook, and while the reader did NOT give an accent to Tommy, he DID give one to his older brother (same parents). This is probably the worst Koontz book I ever read, or at least in the Top 5. I am starting to wonder if I would like "Mr. Murder" if I read it now since I first read it in my 20's. Seems my taste in Koontz's humor may be changing. It won't be the last Koontz book I read, but I certainly will choose the next one more carefully.
