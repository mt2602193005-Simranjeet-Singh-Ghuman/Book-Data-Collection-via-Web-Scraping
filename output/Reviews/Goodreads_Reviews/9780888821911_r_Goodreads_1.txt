Well, according to Jo-Anne Christensen, who is the compiler and reteller of Ghost Stories of British Columbia (1996), British Columbia has been called Canada's most haunted province. And while this is of course impossible to prove and that I personally kind of tend to consider Atlantic Canada as likely possessing (ha, ha, ha, with me definitely trying to be a bit punny here) a richer and also a more versatile and varied tradition and culture of "true" ghost stories and paranormal happenings (in general terms) than B.C., I am as such hugely, majorly glad that Christensen both does not ever actually expect potential readers to consider the above mentioned claim as in fact being gospel and also never expects us to actually consider ghosts being real, as actually and truly existing either (although Jo-Anne Christensen points out in her introduction for Ghost Stories of British Columbia that she in fact is a believer albeit she herself has actually never encountered an experienced anything ghostly or paranormal). Therefore and happily, fortunately so, there is thus never an author and/or compiler attitude of categorically demanding belief in the paranormal emanating from Jo-Anne Christensen's pen and present in Ghost Stories of British Columbia (something I have often found with regard to collections of paranormal and hauntings accounts that are supposed to represent reality, and which always does make me feel more than a trifle uncomfortable and more to the point annoyingly being preached at and sometimes even actively denigrated if not entirely on board so to speak). And indeed, the detailed and pretty massive array of supposedly "true" paranormal occurrences tales, of ghostly apparitions, of time-slip accounts, of premonitions etc. that have been chosen and are being featured by Christensen in Ghost Stories of British Columbia (both from the past and equally more modern, more recent scenarios and experiences as well), this is basically and simply a comprehensive collection of paranormal stories, drawing from British Columbia's history and from its people that is thankfully and very much nicely meant first and foremost to be entertaining (and that if you believe these accounts and their ghosts to be true, this is fine, but that it is equally just as fine to not believe or to only consider some of Christensen's selections as being possibly believable). Fun, versatile and for the most part engagingly penned is Ghost Stories of British Columbia , and that yes, I really do majorly appreciate how albeit Jo-Anne Christensen provides both archival photographs and historical background information for the presented accounts of Ghost Stories of British Columbia (as required and where possible), she happily does not engage in frustrating and tedious information dropping either. Therefore in Ghost Stories of British Columbia , the ghostly tales themselves are front and centre and are not ever annoyingly lost or hidden under barrages of historical facts and ridiculous explanations of paranormal phenomena (not specifically conceptualised with children in mind to be sure but that because Christensen textually keeps her stories as not too creepy, is never gratuitously violent and horrifying, in my opinion, Ghost Stories of British Columbia are nicely suitable for readers from about the age of ten or so onwards, and that Jo-Anne Christensen's penmanship combined with her appreciatively detailed bibliography makes my rating for Ghost Stories of British Columbia solidly four stars and also as such warmly and very much recommended).
