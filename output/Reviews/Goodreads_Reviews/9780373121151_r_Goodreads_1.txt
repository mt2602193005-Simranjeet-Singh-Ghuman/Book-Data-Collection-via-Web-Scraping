Lots of telling rather than showing, and (unusually for Bianchin) the heroine is a single mom - which is unfortunate because Bianchin has zero interest in writing a realistic child. Hello, tiny plot moppet; how convenient that you instantly adore the hero, never throw a temper tantrum, and can be parked with willing teenage babysitters at a moment’s notice so Mommy can huff and puff and fuss over the fact that some hussy is throwing herself at your New Daddy - despite the fact that New Daddy isn’t interested in her and has done everything but throw Mommy over his shoulder and drag her back to the cave. The hero displays zero interest in the child until it becomes necessary for him to win over the heroine, instead demanding that she park the kid with a babysitter so that she can attend fancy dinners and glare at him. It just feels more than usually paint-by-numbers, with wooden characterization and not much zing; the fact that the heroine refuses to acknowledge that the hero is interested in her for most of the book is more frustrating than anything else. There is, bizarrely, a scene towards the end of the book where the other woman declares that the heroine can never have the hero and the heroine walks away… and that’s it, we never see or hear from the other woman again. It’s just very slapdash, like Bianchin had a contract and a deadline and she was going to do her job dammit.
