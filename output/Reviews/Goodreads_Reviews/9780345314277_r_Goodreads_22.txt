A topic as complex as the modern politics of Palestine is always bigger than one book. With that in mind, Tuchman's commentary of the traditions, history and - ultimately - the imperial policies that lead the British Empire to issue the Balfour Declaration in 1917 is a compelling read if still a clear product of its time. It is important to note that Tuchman is not a historian and her work here is best described as well-researched feature journalism. In answering its central question (Why would England support a home for the Jews in Palestine?) it inherently focuses on two factions at the expense of others that had a part in the history and a stake in the outcome. Ultimately, the book is a fascinating look at how the myths we tell ourselves and the traditions that shape us may not be based in history, but they sure do shape it.
