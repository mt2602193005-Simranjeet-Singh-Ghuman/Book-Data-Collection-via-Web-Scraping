-Adult -Poetry, Philosophy, Reflection –A collection of poems where Whitman pours his soul out about everything from love, death, war, nature, the body, the self, to the universe itself. -The man literally starts with “I celebrate myself,” and then says, “every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you.” Like sir?? You’re making me cry and overthink my own crying. -This is not a book you read, it’s one you feel. Some lines miss, but when they hit, they really hit. Trust me. -It’s slow at times, a bit heavy on patriotic USA vibes, but his intention is a deep love for all humanity. I DO NOT HAVE ANYTHING AGAINST MY USA FOLKS, OK? IT’S JUST SOMETHING I NOTICED AND I AM POINTING OUT. I LOVE YOU ALL. -Lots of sensual imagery, and very spiritual too. -You need a bit of patience. Not super modern in language, but once you’re in, it flows beautifully. -Some quotes make you want to cry, or just be thankful to exist. -It’s not a plot-driven book, more like a totally emotional ride. -Very introspective. Definitely re-readable, if you’d like to take more things out. I think that no matter how many times you read it, you’ll always get something more of it. -Also, Whitman was lowkey in love with himself and honestly? Valid. What a diva. -“Do I contradict myself? Very well then I contradict myself, (I am large, I contain multitudes.)” SING IT.
