This is not Tiptree/Sheldon's best work, but it has its moments. Pros: compelling premise, effective worldbuilding, good suspense (at times), and thorough characterization. Cons: pacing issues (mainly scenes going on too long); clunky exposition; and often quite awful dialogue. I don't know...I had mixed feelings over this one because of its inconsistency. The characters' relationships to each other were also a bit weird. For example, Bram can't decide if he should convince Linnix that he's her longlost father (she suspects he is) or if he wants to sleep with her . Then there are the 'documentary' filmmaker and his relationships with his troupe of very young adolescent actors/sex workers, which gave off a Dickensian vibe of street urchins and pimps, except this pimp is soft at heart. Throughout the book, Tiptree veers between stiff, proper prose and freewheeling farcical chaos, but not in a good way. GR reviewer Ian suggested that Tiptree could be parodying the space opera, which would make sense given the quality of her other work, but it feels so overwritten in a lot of places that any sense of parody is drowned out. The plot and themes could have easily been condensed into a novella, which is not surprising, since Tiptree excelled in the short form. Perhaps she was under contract to produce a novel and decided to stretch this one out? (2.5 stars)
