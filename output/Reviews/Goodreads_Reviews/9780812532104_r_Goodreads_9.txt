This novella was first published in 1968 or so, and I vaguely remember enjoying it as a 'tween, when I was also making pencil sketches of jet aircraft for fun. As an adult, I realize that Ben Bova's character development here is bit wooden. What the book says about laser weaponry was spot-on for its time, yet Bova says comparatively little about the airframe of the experimental Mach 3 fighter featured in the story. I'm geeky enough to want to know why that plane's pilot and co-pilot sit side-by-side rather than in a more aerodynamic tandem arrangement. There are two heroes in the book: a materials engineer and a major in the U.S. Air Force, and their grudging friendship with each other helps the simple plot. Out of the Sun was basically Cold War fiction for young adults. Understood in that light, it still holds up well.
