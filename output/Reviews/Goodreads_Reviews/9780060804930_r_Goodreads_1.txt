This is the tenth in the Nigel Strangeways series, published in 1953. In this novel, we get the sense that Strangeways is actually dropping the pretence of being the disinterested hobbyist, as he is employed by financier Arthur Blick to investigate a spate of poison pen letters in the village where his sons live. Strangeways goes to Prior’s Umborne to meet a wonderfully eccentric group of characters. There is Stanford Blick, the favoured older son, with younger brother Charles; plus the two sisters they are entangled with. The crippled Celandine and her sister, Rosebay, along with their illegitimate half-brother, the creepy Daniel Durdle and vicar, Mark Raynham. Nicholas Blake weaves together a story of secrets, jealousy, resentments and a tyrannical father. This is not the best in the series, but it is still a good addition to a much neglected Golden Age mystery series.
