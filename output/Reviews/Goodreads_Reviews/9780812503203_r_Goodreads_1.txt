The plot unwinds clumsily, and the characters are a bit hollow, but the main idea is gripping. Half ghost story, half sci-fi story, there is much in the overall morality of the story that can be overlaid on what is going on the world today. I will read more Schenck, and have added A Rose for Armaggeddon to my "to read" list, even though from its description, it seems to has a female protagonist, and I didn't like the development of female characters in Chronosequence . Chronosequence 's copyright is 1988, but yet there were examples of annoying 60's/70's-style "females idealized by a male science fiction writer," and "rape" used as a playful term. Yuck. However, Rose is a part of Pocket Books' Timescape imprint , and I've wanted to read more of those.
