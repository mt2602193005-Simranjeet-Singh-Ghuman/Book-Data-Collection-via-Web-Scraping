In his introduction Michael Blades reflected the aesthetic draw to being in a sea kayak that mirrored my own experience. This is probably why I gained so much enjoyment from reading, and in the past years, re-reading his stories of a novice kayaker getting his feet wet and exploring the hard to get to places on my beloved west coast. His writing style jumps about a bit. But even though some of his tales lack that polish they only add to the flavour of the book and all the stories carry an element of authenticity and self-affacement that most autobiographical outdoor books do not have. As a kayaker, I know how he felt in those bigger waves, and have been blessed with so many of the quiet moments of reflection while camping on some far-flung beach nestled in the rugged coast and backed by dense impenetrable forests. Wolf and cougar tracks around my tent by morning and the knowledge as I go to sleep at night that only Mother Nature and not my own ego will determine the following days agenda. The Day of Two Sunsets is a kayakers book indeed but any one of us needing a dose of what it is to be in nature, part of it and regain a small amount of what we have all become so seperated by, read this book.
