Simmons is an accomplished writer in a variety of genres, so while I’ve always been most impressed with his SF - the middle two Hyperion books are my favorites - I’m also willing to give his other work a try. This one is tough to classify, so I guess I’d call it general fiction, edging toward literary. It reminded me more than anything of Michael Gruber’s less speculative writing - the style is strikingly similar. The atmosphere is languid and focused on protagonist Baedecker’s sensations, including, yes, gravity, but also the sights, sounds, and scents of his past. The late 80s are clearly evoked, not in the sense of pop culture, but as an end point in the segment of aerospace history that began in the 50s. Given the relative lack of speculative elements, combined with Baedecker’s flat and borderline morose personality for most of the book, I’d rate it a three point something. However, I’ll go ahead and round up, since I did end up enjoying the reading experience. Maggie is a great character, as is Dave, and the story, while slow moving for the most part, has some great high points, and overall it was interesting and engaging enough to make for a quick read. Glad I read this.
