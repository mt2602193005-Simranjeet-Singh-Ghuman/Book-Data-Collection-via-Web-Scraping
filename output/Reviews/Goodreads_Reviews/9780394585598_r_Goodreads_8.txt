Genuinely inspiring how Tanenhaus does not even once ask/answer the question “who cares about Whittaker Chambers?” I keep asking people if they know who Chambers & Hiss are, and people’s recollection is vague at best (not even worth asking abt people like Koestler, Schlamm, etc.). Even more so, Tanenhaus definitely doesn’t directly address my biggest Q: why should non-conservatives care abt this mid-century conservative darling?? In answer, Tanenhaus is really just asking you to care on the emotive level with a shlubby guy who’s got bad teeth and got really into communism for a lil while. Chambers the man is 3-dimensional and awkward and tragic and compelling in so many ways. And Tanenhaus paints Chambers the Political Figure in similarly moving terms. I mean, the middle of this book is unadulterated political thriller—spies, assassinations, covert meetings, quick escapes, pumpkin papers! I think I’m much more receptive to this because it’s more of a “Witness the man in full” than a political argument. Fascinated by how fascinated Tanenhaus is with Chambers despite his (broadly left) political leanings coming through. Even he seems drawn in by Chambers the man / Chambers the news article divide. It’s kinda interesting to read something that’s been engineered in a lab for your soft Republican dad to enjoy.
