It isn’t the oceans which cut us off from the world—it’s the American way of looking at things. Nothing comes to fruition here except utilitarian projects. You can ride for thousands of miles and be utterly unaware of the existence of the world of art. You will learn all about beer, condensed milk, rubber goods, canned food, inflated mattresses, etc., but you will never see or hear anything concerning the masterpieces of art. This is a brilliant two hundred and fifty page rant in the guise of an American travelogue. The level of detail and the word smithing that Miller exhibits here reminds me of other literary giants who penned excellent place centric non-fiction: George Orwell - Homage to Catalonia John Steinbeck - Travels with Charley (parts are fictional) David Foster Wallace - Ticket to the Fair Tom Wolfe - The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test 5 stars. I really loved this curmudgeonly read.
