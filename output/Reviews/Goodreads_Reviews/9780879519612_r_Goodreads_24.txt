As a northerner who went to Oxford via a state school, Jill was like picking at a not-quite-healed scab… the imposter syndrome which culminated in John Kemp's retreat into fantasy is rendered with uncomfortable precision. The novel is a quietly devastating portrait of aspiration and class anxiety, let down only by its climax, in which the protagonist appears to confuse unrequited desire with sexual assault of a minor. A product of its time, perhaps - I like to think attitudes to consent at university have changed since Larkin's own Oxford days. Then again, based on my own experience there, maybe that's too much to ask for.
