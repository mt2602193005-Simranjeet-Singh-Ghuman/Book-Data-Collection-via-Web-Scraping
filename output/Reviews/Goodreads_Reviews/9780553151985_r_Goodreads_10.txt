Originally published 1956. Back in the 60s a classmate in elementary was obsessed with this book, to the place the school librarian nearly begged her to check out other books and took the time to draw up a list of other books she might like instead of always checking out this one. For decades my memory thought it was an instalment in the Dorrie series, and I "remembered" the title as Dorrie and the Blue Nose, which does not exist. This morning messing around on Pinterest I saw this cover! And joy of joys, it was available on the Internet Archive. Another scrap of memory laid to rest. It's a mild little story of how Blanche, the little girl witch who's always late, meets up with normal kids out trick-or-treating. Her magic is basically flying around and making her nose light up (whatever purpose that may serve), but then she's only gotten started in training. She speaks of the other witches as being "dreadful" but there is nothing about their grown-up doings. As usual in most real life clubs, two or three of them are in charge and do most of the important stuff (never described) while the rest stand back and applaud. Madame, the head of the "scurry" (coven), rides on a noisy vacuum cleaner instead of a broom. This book was published 6 years before Sabrina joined the cast of the Archies comics and cartoon. Ruth Chew used the idea in The Wednesday Witch , but that book didn't come out until 1969, seven years after Sabrina appeared and 13 years after Embry's book. I think it's safe to say Embry's "scurry" of witches is the first instance of a witch on a vaccuum cleaner. There now. Two mysteries cleared up in one very short book.
