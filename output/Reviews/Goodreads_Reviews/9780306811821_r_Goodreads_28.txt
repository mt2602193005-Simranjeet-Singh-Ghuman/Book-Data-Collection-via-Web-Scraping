Nick Kent, as well as being one of the legendary UK music journalists, is also something of a rock'n'roll insider (former lover of Chrissie Hynde; performer on one of the great, lost post-punk singles, "My Flamingo" by The Subterraneans), as well as having a well-documented history with illegal substances. It comes as no surprise, then, that this book, a collection of features and revisited interviews, largely focuses on the damage wrought by drug use. Beginning with a novella-length account of the nightmarish history of the Beach Boys, Kent provides us with detailed impressions of several troubled geniuses (Miles Davis, Syd Barrett, Kurt Cobain, Sly Stone), as well as measured takes on the lives of less self-destructive heroes (Morrissey, Neil Young, Elvis Costello). Naturally, several luminaries come across as thoroughly unpleasant (Brian Jones, Jerry Lee Lewis, Phil Spector); and even Kent's elegantly workmanlike writing can't make someone like Izzy Stradlin appear interesting. There are some ripe descriptions and clever insights here, but "The Dark Stuff" is best avoided if you wants to keep your illusions intact.
