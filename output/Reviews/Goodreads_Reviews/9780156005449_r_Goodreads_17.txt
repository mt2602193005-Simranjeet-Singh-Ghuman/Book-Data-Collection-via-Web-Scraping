I had to read this for an ethnic lit class I’m taking over the summer. On seeing the rating on here, I thought there was no way such an important and interesting—albeit heartbreaking ofc—topic could be turned into a story poor enough to only get like a 3.5. Goodreads and Letterboxd ratings don’t mean much, but that’s still pretty low—especially for Goodreads. Sure enough, it was. There’s a ton of good research on display, and it was good to read on a subject that I knew so little about, but it is just so repetitive. Maybe that’s the point, maybe the repetition is part of why it was so tolling on the Cherokee people, emotionally and physically, but it didn’t make for a very interesting read (which is hard to do for a topic like this). I felt like someone was telling me that the trail of tears was heartbreaking and terrible, instead of actually making me feel the pain the Cherokee felt. The closest thing I can think of that this could’ve been is Schindler’s List. It takes a topic that has been discussed and done many times, but still finds a new angle to make it feel different and chilling and horrifying all anew again. If you’re just curious to learn more abt the trail of tears in a cursory way, this isn’t a bad book, but I can’t help but feel that there must be a better one out there
