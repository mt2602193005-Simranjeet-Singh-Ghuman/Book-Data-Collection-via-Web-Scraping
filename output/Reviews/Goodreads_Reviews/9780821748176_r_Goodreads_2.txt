A very satisfying read, as well as considerably longer than most traditional regencies (though the plot certainly held). I am a big fan of epilogues that tie up all the loose ends and provide a glimpse of our beloved characters' lives in summary, and this one has all the hallmark of a good ending. The author is obviously very interested in history, as the plot made well mention of the sedition situation of England at the time (1820s is the setting), as well as the civil and religious reforms taking place for Unitarians and Dissenters. There were a lot of mention on poetry and political freedom as well, so one could learn quite a bit from just reading this! The plot can be summed up in two main parts: who is spying on Wendall, and the cavalry to convince Mr. Worthing to allow his daughter to marry an aristocrat. The first part is understandably confusing, but nicely puts all the romantic relationships in motion, and the second was just a delight to read as one obstinate father is convinced that a mere title cannot be one's only objection to a good match, quite the opposite of the idea that every wealthy tradesman wants to marry into titles. ;) Show more
