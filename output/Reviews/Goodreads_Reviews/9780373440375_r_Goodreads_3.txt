“Wife is a 4-letter Word” is the story of Alan Parish, who is on his way to his honeymoon…alone. Ditched at the alter by his fiancé, he decides to spend the week with the Maid of Honor, Pamela Kaminski, a real estate agent from a troubled past. Bond shines as a gifted writer and plotter. As I was reading, I was surprised by how ‘wrong’ this honeymoon went. If anything could go wrong, it did go wrong, and Pamela usually had something to do with it. Pam was a unique heroine who got drunk, swore, and seemed to have more sexual experience than Alan. I knew I was in for a roller coaster of a read when Allen and Pam got drunk at the airport and then she ended up throwing up from the flight turbulence. Um…okay. And then near the end of the book, they got drunk again and Alan got a tattoo of Pam’s name on his hip. I’m glad this is fiction, otherwise I hope AA sponsors their wedding. While the writing and plot details were superb, I have to give this book 4 stars because Bond missed an opportunity to show character growth and conflict. There were several instances where Pam reflects on her disadvantaged background, how she got kicked out the private high school she and Alan attended, and how she supports her sister’s education. There were also several times where Alan is described as someone who is used to a richer lifestyle than the rest of us plebians. While Pam gets Alan to appreciate the not-so-finer things in life, I felt that the social class conflict could have been strengthened. Pam obviously felt strongly about it, but they never seemed to discuss it or maybe have a family member protest their relationship. Most of the conflict came from Pam’s friend, Jo, the ex-fiancé. Sure, she dumped him, but he jumped into another woman’s bed real quick. How solid was that relationship? Rebound much? Overall, a light-hearted read, but I was also looking for a deep emotional connection between the two characters that wasn’t there.
