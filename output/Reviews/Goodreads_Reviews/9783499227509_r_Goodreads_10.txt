Read for my Intercultural pedagogy class. It is very simple and could be useful when first broaching the subject with kids, but it has some dangerous ideas sprinkled throughout. Firstly, there's the idea of not seeing race. Race may not be a biological fact, but it is a sociocultural one that impacts the formation of one's selfhood, and it is just reductive in terms of choosing not to acknowledge its history. Secondly, and this I think is more sinister, the author perpetuates the myth that wearing a hijab is a symbol of oppression, and not an individual woman's choice. Even though he earlier states that religion is not rational, but a concept tied to belief, and is thus always up for interpretation, he sticks to a rigid view of the Quran and selectively chooses quotes to support his view. If I could've chosen my own reading for the topic, I would've gone with Between the World and Me , which is far more complex, demanding and impactful.
