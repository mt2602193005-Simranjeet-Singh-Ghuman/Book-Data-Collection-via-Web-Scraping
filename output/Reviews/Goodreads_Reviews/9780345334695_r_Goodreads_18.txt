I grabbed this off my shelf at random to re-read. Turns out it was pretty good choice. It covers a big swathe of short stories that were to become Niven's "Known Space" which includes some of his greatest novels. The stories also cover a big span of space exploration. Niven tries to stick to known science where possible and he freely admits that several of his early stories set on Mercury and Mars are now outdated. Niven's introduction and exposition are also interesting, showing the process where he realised the stories were set in the same universe, or could be made to fit, and how inventions in one then had to be considered as solutions in all later stories. Too few authors have this level of attention to detail and consistency. As for the stories themselves, first there are a couple involving a human brain implanted in a spaceship. This predates McCaffrey's The Ship Who Sang by several years. You can see the genesis of some classic SF novels, including the seeds of The Integral Trees (pun intended) which shows how long some ideas need to mature. Others cover human interaction in different environments but predominantly they are driven by technical problems and their solutions. It's old school hard SF and a good example of it.
