What a treat to find under the Christmas tree this year, Jon Hassler's Staggerford Flood. Published in 2002, this novel, one of Hassler's last about the beloved northern Minnesota community he created decades ago, finds most of the characters mellowing. At the center is outspoken, community leader retired Catholic school teacher, Agatha McGee, now in her 80s dealing with health and aging issues. Hassler loves his characters as do his readers and thus he brings them all back for us for an interesting week. Like most small towns, people rally in a crisis and so they do in Staggerford, dealing with a once-in-a century river flood. Agatha ends up hosting seven women at her home. Hassler's gentle humor comes through as Agatha deals with a moral issue during the crisis. I enjoyed getting reacquainted with all the townspeople again, but the plot didn't grab or feel as sharp as some of his other novels. Perhaps, Hassler was mellowing, too. Still, a satisfying read about women and friendship.
