Classic Higgins. While “normal” conversation – itself often criminal and hilarious at the same time – flows, and flows some more in the foreground without ever seeming to ebb, the action faintly plays in the background like a TV in the corner with the sound turned way down at the ol’ boys’ regular gathering. This actually happens in one extended scene. There is a good story here. But as always with Higgins the pleasure lies in what he does with Boston dialects. First rate. Nearly as good as Friends of Eddie Coyle and Cogan’s Trade.
