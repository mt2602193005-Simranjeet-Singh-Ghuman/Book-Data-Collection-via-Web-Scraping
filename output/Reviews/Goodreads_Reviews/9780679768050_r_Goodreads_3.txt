There is no filter of sophistication in this novel - everything is from the point of Anna Ferrera, the only woman delivery driver for HomeMade Cakes in New York City. Anna is (perhaps typical of New Yorkers) a very tolerant person, but when she finally gets mad, she goes ballistic. Anna has a misogynistic and all round jerk of a boss, but she manages to handle him. She is threatened with sexual abuse from some of the storekeepers. Her co-workers initially ignore her, and completing her route in a timely way is difficult. Yet Anna navigates all sorts of hazards because she needs to support her two little boys. The various characters are very well-drawn - her boss Jed; delivery drivers including the very needy Baby Huey and crooked Mario the Mole; storekeepers, the flirtatious Angel and the horrid Rudy; her friends Lily and Pudgy, the latter with whom she has a most unusual relationship, a convenient one for her and kind of a sad one for him; her adorable boys, Nick and Joey; and Tom, her very self serving and smug husband, soon former husband. One great scene is when she visits the family home of Baby Huey, and realizes he grew up in an abusive home, as she did. The abuse in her background may have something to do with her lack of judgment in romantic partners. Yet despite her faults, I was always rooting for her. I believe the novel really captures a working class sensibility. I haven’t found many books with convincing characters who are working class. There is more written about drug addicts, drunks, and criminals (who I find very interesting as well). Not so much is written about people who work long hours at tedious, unskilled jobs, especially if descriptions of their jobs and their coworkers is essential to the story. That’s why I think this novel is a little gem.
