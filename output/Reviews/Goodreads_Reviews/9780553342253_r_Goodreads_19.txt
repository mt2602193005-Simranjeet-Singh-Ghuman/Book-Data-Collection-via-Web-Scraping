Giddings hits the nail on the head all the way through this essential woke-ology text. I would like to see something like this more globally, a history of Black Women’s activism throughout Africa and the diaspora. On the one hand, it’s wonderful news to discover that time and again Black Women activists have recognized that no one is free until all of us free. While others seek expediency, or are willing to sacrifice the poor, or the darker-skinned, or non-male, Black Women routinely take a maximal approach. “Race” or “Sex”... porque no los dos? The beginning chapters — slavery, reconstruction, lynchings, on through women’s suffrage — read as exciting history, a familiar story told from a for me unfamiliar perspective. But as we get closer to Giddings own lifetime the quiet, calm rage reaches a boil. After all the amazing work, the heroic struggles, the victories, to be betrayed by white feminists and black nationalists, to catch hell from all sides and to be told that it is precisely your strengths, your victories that make you a “matriarch,” a problem, the Problem... arrgghh! In the end, a conclusion that quotes Sojourner Truth that Black Women can turn the world right-side up, rings true. The rest of us should just get out of the way and let them get on with it. I love when a book points me at further reading, and although the selected bibliography here is of course sources from the 1970s and earlier (the book came out in 1982), there are so many amazing personalities, struggles and campaigns covered in such few pages that an appetite for more can’t help but assert itself. At the same time, certain other doors close, and I am thankful for the impetus to weed some male authors from my to read list. Bye bye, Paula Giddings destroyed you!
