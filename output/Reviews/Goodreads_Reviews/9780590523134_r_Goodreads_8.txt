The author in this one really wrestled with the complexity of emotions in seeing a parent who walked out of their lives when they were little, for better or worse. As a reader, I liked that it left you a bit unsettled. Also as a reader, though, I find myself wondering a lot more about the "why." And though I understand Life doesn't always give us satisfying explanations, and maybe that was the point, I would have at least liked some sort of rationale behind everything that happened. How can a father just invite his kids back into his family without any acknowledgement of what he did, like no time had passed? ("I had to" walk out and "you didn't keep in touch with *me*" don't really seem like much. Did Patrick not come up with some sort of rationalization over the years for what he did? Does he feel bad about it? We never know because he isn't developed enough.) And can't the writer throw us a bone about why David Michael wasn't invited from the guy who didn't invite him? Every time they got close to having any sort of talk, the writer seemed to pull back, which was frustrating.This book is written for tweens, assumedly, so you would think there would be a little "more," so to speak, since kids have a little more trouble with uncertainty. It all just felt like the author was avoiding the issue because she didn't feel like doing the hard work of inventing a back story. I did appreciate all the baseball talk - though I can't see why anyone would want to walk down the aisle to "Take Me Out to the Ballgame."
