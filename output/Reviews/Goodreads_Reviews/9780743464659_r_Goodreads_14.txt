I honestly don't what to say about this book & I struggled to give a rating. I finally settled on 4★ I know I read this one many years ago & absolutely loved it. I think what I liked was that Charlie (Charmian) was the main protagonist. This time I can appreciate what a wonderful writer le Carré is, but I had a few problems with it. Not le Carré's fault, but now isn't the time I want to be reading about terrorism, spying & innocents suffering. (Yes, I should have realised this before I started the book!) Sadly, some of this almost felt current! Joseph and Charlie were formally introduced to each other on the island of Mykonos, on a beach with two tavernas, at a late luncheon in the second half of August, just around the time when the Greek sun hits its fiercest heat. Or, in terms of the larger history, four weeks after Israeli jets bombed the crowded Palestinian quarter of Beirut, in what was afterwards declared to be an effort to destroy the leadership, though there were no leaders among the several hundred dead- unless of course they were the leaders of tomorrow, for many were children. Sound familiar? The book was very slow moving at the start & some of it was quite misogynistic. Charlie was a sexually free woman of the 80s & was repeatedly (even on the dust jacket by the blurb writer) defined as promiscuous. Not a description given to any of the young men. With this book her sexuality defined Charlie even though she was many other things as well. In one scene the violence Joseph used against Charlie shocked me. It was in response to Charlie making a deliberately racist & offensive remark. His response was swift & brutal - yet there was no mention of Charlie having a bruised face afterwards which surely other people would have noticed. Maybe she applied heavy make up. The last third of the book was complex and enthralling - I had to have my wits about me to keep a track of plot. The ending was a bit Mills & Boon & frankly I don't think Joseph deserved Charlie! To be honest, not likely to read any other le Carrés in the near future and I'm very glad I ignored the large pile of them at the last Book Fair I went to.
