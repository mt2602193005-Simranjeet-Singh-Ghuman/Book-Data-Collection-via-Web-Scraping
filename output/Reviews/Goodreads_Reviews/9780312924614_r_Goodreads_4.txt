I was expecting something more along the lines of Rosemary Roger's "The Insiders", where it's one big, trashy soap opera of the hollywood elite (which I was in the mood for) but this turned out to be quite moving. I'm not going to claim it's an elegant piece of literature or anything, but I think the author did a good job giving her characters a complex psychology and development. She also made you care about and root for the romantic leads. Matthew and Leigh have both been horribly abused and traumatized and therefore have natural distrust and coldness towards people, yet when they are together they are able to develop some amount of tenderness and comfort. I found their desire for redemption in the face of so many greedy, malicious people very touching. Both seem to be on a journey for truth and relational stability but are trapt in a world where everyone gets by on lies and manipulation. The author sort of tortures the reader though by putting their pure-hearted relationship through every worst case scenario and it was gut wrenching at times. I'll admit that some things about the book were very far-fetched (like Matthew being an abandoned baby orphan, abused foster child, ptsd-ridden vietnam vet, and a poor bartender in one lifetime!)but overall I was really riveted by this book and surprised many times, which is always a good thing.
