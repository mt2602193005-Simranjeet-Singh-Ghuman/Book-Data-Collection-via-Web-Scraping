This is Jude Deveraux's first book. As always, I’m impressed with Jude’s easy writing style. She pulls you right into her story effortlessly, introducing several characters, like the Montgomery brothers, and you get to know them and care about them pretty quickly. I think Judith is one of Jude’s best characters. She’s a strong woman, hard working, and pretty ingenious. I liked her a lot. I like the fact that she had ambitious plans to be an abbess, although I think Deveraux probably should have made her a little bit more religious if that was what the character wanted. I think one of the problems with this book is Gavin is so damn clueless. It’s like he’s walking around with a bag over his head. Everybody tells him that Alice is playing him for a fool, but he never believes. He has no idea how to talk Judith, and I think the rape after the wedding was oddly out of character for him, because deep down he’s a gentle guy. Maybe if his motivations at the wedding had been a bit more dire, I would have understood it better. Originally he doesn’t even want to get married, so for him to force the consummation didn’t really fit. He’s not even a guy who can’t control his passions. He’s more pragmatic than that. Anyway, it didn’t make sense to me, and I’ve noticed it’s one of the biggest complaints some readers have about this book. Anyway though, I did enjoy this book. Jude Deveraux knows how to write an interesting story, likeable characters that are flawed, and fun dialogue. There was more than a little tragedy in this book, which surprised me.
