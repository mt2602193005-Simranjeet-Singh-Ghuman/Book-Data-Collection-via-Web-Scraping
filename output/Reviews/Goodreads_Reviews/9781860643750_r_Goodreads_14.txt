A remarkable history, told in a remarkably straightforward manner. The best biography of Genghis Khan this reader has come across. It's a curious thing, the better press Genghis Khan has gotten. There's little valid reason for this. The Mongols left a trail of death and destruction that permanently altered some ancient cities and peoples in Central Asia and China. Massacres and atrocities were not one-offs with Genghis Khan and his hordes, they were their MO. Cities that had held a prominent regional place in these ancient civilizations were smoking ruins and piles of skulls after the Mongols washed over them. Nevertheless, from a purely military standpoint, Genghis Khan and his sons, along with a handful of supremely skilled generals and assistants (Genghis was an astute judge of character and talents) amassed an extraordinary record. His personal odyssey is a remarkable one. As for what he wrought, hmmm. The religious tolerance they exhibited (this being one element of his improved status in world history) was largely irrelevant in places they left absolutely devastated and depopulated. The Mongols "pony express," on the other hand, was an enduring legacy that did help trade along the Silk Road. All of this makes a fascinating story. The terror the Mongols inspired, matched perhaps only by their conscious imitator Tamerlane a couple of centuries later, has made a permanent impression. The swath they cut through the Islamic world and portions of eastern and southeastern Christian Europe has no real parallel. Indeed, for Russians, the Mongols and the Bolsheviks stand as the two most brutal overlords their culture and civilization have ever known, and there's an incalculable, generational warping, I think, in their legacies.
