Zettel has created an interesting alien race and society, or I should say societies, since she wisely avoids the SF cliche of everybody on an alien planet having the same culture. The book starts out slowly, with too many obvious attempts at avoiding expository lumps, the introduction of too many characters who end up not mattering much, and way too much discussion of what might happen, what could happen, what various factions want to have happen etc. But once the plot actually gets going, it becomes a decent (but not all that innovative) story that also raises questions of the influence of a high-tech culture on a lower-tech culture, and issues of war and peace and inherent tendencies toward violence. However, the prose is often clunky and the structure of the book is clumsily put together. I give it a B+ for concepts and worldbuilding, C for plot, and D for technique.
