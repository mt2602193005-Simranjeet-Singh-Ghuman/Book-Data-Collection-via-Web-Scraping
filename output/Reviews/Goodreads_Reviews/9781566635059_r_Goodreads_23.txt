A fantastic collection of essays by a psychiatrist who has worked in hospitals and prisons for decades observing the underclass in Britain. With dry wit and eloquence he describes how the loss of a sense of agency -- the understanding that the choices you make will have an effect, for good or ill,on your life -- creates the underclass. And how the Marxist utopian ideas of the liberal intelligentsia perpetuate their plight by convincing them that they are victims, and therefore not responsible for their actions. The result is a sick culture. He writes in such an engaging and entertaining way that once you read one essay, you'll want to read them all.
