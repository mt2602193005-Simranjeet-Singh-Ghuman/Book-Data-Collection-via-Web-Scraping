I liked the transformation of Karen as she went from giving her coach the benefit of the doubt, i.e., believing that he was only being strict to help his team be the best swimmers they could be, to realizing his tactics were way too harsh. **Spoiler Alert: There were a couple of times I did think Karen treated Coach Carson unfairly. The first was the time he took Terri aside and gave her private instruction. Yes, he could have done it without humiliating her, but she had said earlier that she wished the coach would help her friend, to being upset that he was doing just that. Another was the time he said "Good job, Brewer," to her, and she was upset that he didn't say more. Some people just aren't apt to lavish on praise, and the fact that Coach Carson didn't doesn't mean he wasn't proud of Karen. This book made me think, perhaps being above average to great at your craft makes you a better teacher than being excellent at it? After all, if you are excellent at something, you might have trouble understanding why others have trouble with it, and I think that was the case with Ron Carson.
