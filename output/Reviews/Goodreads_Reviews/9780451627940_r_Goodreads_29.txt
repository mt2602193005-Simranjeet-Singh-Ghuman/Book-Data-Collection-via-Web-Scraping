My problem with this book and nearly all political memoirs written in the first person is their lack of critical analysis. Robert Kennedy writes well, tells an interesting story and provides a critical analysis of his performance, but the book itself is rather self-serving. His brother, JFK, receives no critical review of his performance and every decision the Administration made was correct. Alternatively, every utterance by the Senate leadership, read the other political party< was short-sighted and too hawkish. This analysis may be true, but like all political memoirs I am not given enough information to make my own analysis and decision. I like third party written analyses of political crises, because research is critical and, for the most part, both pros and cons of the decisions are reviewed. This book was interesting to me because I know little about the Cuban missile crisis and I respect RFK, but it is not a book I could recommend.
