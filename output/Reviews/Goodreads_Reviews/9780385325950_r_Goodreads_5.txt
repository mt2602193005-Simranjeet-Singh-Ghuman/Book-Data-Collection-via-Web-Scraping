I hated the protagonist of this book. She's whiny. Stupid. Deliberately blind to the crises around her, and she's NOT coping well. And her solutions to the problem at hand aren't well thought out at all. In short she's a teenager. And I kept wanting her to do what other teens in books of this nature do: GROW UP. Only...that's not real. This is a family that is in a disastrous situation. And she's just a kid. And admitting the problem is too scary and means that they're all really on their own and expecting to find solutions that she's simply NOT prepared to find. My gosh, the horror of this novel... the hunger, the idea of children driving on the expressway alone in the middle of the night...the sickness and coping. It made my heart hurt as a parent because she wasn't coping, and couldn't expect to be. And I hated her for that, because her siblings needed her so freaking much to do just that. Real and disquieting, this isn't a book to take lightly - and shouldn't be taken lightly. Kids are in crises and trying to handle adult problems all over the place. The idea that they're doing so without any kind of help at all, is terrifying and heartbreaking. This is a good book. But I don't want to read it again. Ever. The consequences of mistakes are too terrible to contemplate. The adult trying to help - is crucified for it. It scares me as an adult who wants to help to step in and face that kind of trouble and accusation. The idea of leaving children without help is even worse. It sets up a conflict within that doesn't go away when you close the book, and stays with you for a long time afterwards. I don't want the world of this book to be real. But it is. And it's not pretty. But it makes for a good book.
