This was a weird book. For a good portion of the novel, it's hard to discern whether what's happening to Jacob is actually happening, or if it's all in his head. This is intentional, of course, but in a rare misstep for Piccirilli, it's just not very well executed. I rather liked the ending inasmuch as it somehow managed to buck two trends in horror fiction, being neither happy nor horrible. It just is, and fits the book quite well. If you're a fan of Piccirilli, I'd say this is worth reading to see him playing with some literary devices; he doesn't necessarily do it well, but it's interesting nonetheless.
