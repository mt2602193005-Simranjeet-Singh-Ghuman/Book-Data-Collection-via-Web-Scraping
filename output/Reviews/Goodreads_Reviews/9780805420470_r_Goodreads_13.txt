After my second reading: "Can education influence morality?" asks the back cover blurb. Of course, the musings of an Oxford don seventy years ago could not be relevant to the current state of education in America. Or, could it? For a reader already concerned about the downward spiral of the quality of our education, this book will pour fuel on the fire. The trends Lewis warned of in the 1940s now permeate our schools--all of them. The result may be men with unimaginable power, but no moral compass by which to guide their actions. Unlike many other Lewis books, this is not a Christian apology. In fact, he claims that all historic cultures worldwide stand in opposition to the modern, valueless approach. Original review: This is perhaps the most challenging of Lewis's books of essays, but his thesis is clear: modern education is creating "men without chests", that is, they have brains and bowels (and other lower urges) but no heart.
