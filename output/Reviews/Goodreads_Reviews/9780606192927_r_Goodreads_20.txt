★★½ Tick Tock: The Minutes Drag in Madness This started well, with a creeping sense of menace as Tommy comes home to a strange doll and quickly finds himself under threat. The early chapters build suspense nicely, and once he escapes the house there is some genuine excitement in his attempts to outrun the demon. At that stage I thought I was in safe Koontz territory. Then Del enters the picture and the book takes a turn that is hard to accept. Others have described this as screwball comedy, but I found it more irritating than funny. The humour feels forced and quickly wears thin, much like Del herself. From that point the story loses its footing. It piles on increasingly bizarre events without giving the reader any real explanation. Characters act on knowledge we are never shown how they gained, and there is no effort to anchor the madness in anything resembling logic. The ending pushes things even further, going beyond crazy with the showdown and the fate of the demon. Instead of feeling tense or satisfying, it comes across as overblown. The book is padded with too many meaningless interactions and exchanges, which only adds to the sense that the story has gone off course. I have enjoyed many of Koontz’s novels, including some where he blends humour with the bizarre, but here it overwhelms everything else. Perhaps I missed what others see in the comedy, though I know I’m not the only one who struggled with it. For me this sits below the standard of his stronger work, and it is not a book I would ever suggest as a first step into Koontz. Entertaining in places, yes, but overall a 2.5 star read that shows how far he can stray from the kind of story he does best.
