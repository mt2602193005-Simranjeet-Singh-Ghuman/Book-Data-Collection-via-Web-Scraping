Edinburgh was a sick city in the 1990s. While the industrial and employment crisis raged in these post-Tatcharian years, a group of lost friends managed to survive. This world revolves around dope: heroin, cocaine, weed, alcohol, H, barbiturates, clusters of asteroids in perdition, attracted by the fatal orbit of the powder, which is nothing like stardust. So fixed that we lavish ourselves in mock hugs convulsed with pleasure, humiliating pleas to the local dealer for alloyed merchandise, calamitous weaning attempts, doomed recoveries, little value's dilemma, overdoses, and bad trips. Premature deaths, the specter of AIDS, trouble with the law following a miserable theft to the detriment of small local traders, and fights in pubs. These football derbies turn into the settling of scores; life without the perspective of these boys is challenging. On a soundtrack of Brit-pop music, this polyphonic novel alternates between first-person stories emanating from the various acolytes of this band, featuring chronically unsuitable, endearing, and distanced characters, told in the third person singular, in a slangy and profane language. Colorful and suggestive, striking and practical, like a ball in the face, the alternating differences in tone, disillusioned humor, and self-mockery fully deserve its reputation as a cult book of the '90s.
