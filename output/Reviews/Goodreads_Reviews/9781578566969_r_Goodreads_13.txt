I have loved Liz Curtis Higgs’ books for 3 decades now (!) and read her “Bad Girls of the Bible” series more than once, but this was my first time reading this book. The thing I liked the least about the other books was the fictionalized accounts of the Bad Girls’ stories, but I have to admit, this time it really worked for me and touched me to the heart. I thought she did a wonderful job of delving into a much clearer picture of who Mary Magdeline was when she was still a Bad/Mad girl and after she found Jesus and began walking with him. I love Liz’s sense of humor and her honesty, especially when she is so forthcoming about the way she was changed by Christ in her own life, and I think because of her own life, she is one of the very best and authoritative voices in the world today to be able to talk about Mary Magdeline and all the other “bad girls” that she has written about. She is a magnificent writer when it comes to doing a deep dive into every aspect of the Biblical character(s) she writes about, and leaves no stone unturned. I found there are a few more Girls of the Bible books I haven’t read of hers—one about Ruth and one about the Queen of Sheba -and intend to read them next.
