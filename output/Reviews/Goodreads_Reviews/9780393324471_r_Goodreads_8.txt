Ostensibly a reflection on marriage and an appreciation of the harsh beauty of El Salvador (with an amazing amount of information on the cultivation of coffee thrown in for good measure), the book appealed to me largely for its thoughts about story, memory and language. It is beautifully written. "Experience is sight, sound, smell, taste, the burn of muscles, the precipitance of words, the rise of passion, the chill of regret...Remembering is about reassembling those connections, and about forgeting strategically forgetting so much that lies between, and he ore we reassemble our memories...the truer they seem to us, the more certain and more bedrock. Our memories tell us who we have been and therefore who we are." (126)
