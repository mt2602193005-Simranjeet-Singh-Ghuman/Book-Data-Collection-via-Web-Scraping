Okay. This book in the series starts to get into the cheesy love stories that I typically associate with Anita Stansfield and that I don't really enjoy. The previous books in the series were a little more "historical fiction" -ess and I could deal with the cheesiness when it's dated in the late 1800's or early 1900's because that's sort of how I picture those romances anyways. However, this book is more in the present time and falls into the LDS romance I-have-something-really-bad-happen-to-me-but-my-faith-gets-me-through-so-I-can-marry-the-man-of-my-dreams category that doesn't really appeal to me. It's not very realistic. It is a clean romance though which I greatly appreciate.
