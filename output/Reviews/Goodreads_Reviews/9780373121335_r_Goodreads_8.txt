The writing and plot here were actually pretty decent in the beginning, sort of modern-day Gothic, with characters who seemed approximately like real people, but the book is spoiled by the fact that the hero is a douchebag: he has a perfectly nice girlfriend throughout the book. Natalie is a fashion model, she feels he does not spend enough time with her (he doesn't), she would like to meet his family, although she is suspicious of his "beautiful, brainy" stepsister since he informed her that when they were teenagers he had sex with said stepsister (when she admits this he, lying, says he was just teasing Natalie and it never actually happened), and... none of this stops the hero from mauling the heroine (his stepsister) at every available opportunity, attempting to initiate sex, etc., while lying to Natalie about his reasons for constantly leaving London. I mean... this is not heroic behavior, right? At the end we learn that Natalie is now engaged to a Greek shipping magnate so I can only assume that Mather actually meant to write that book - in which gorgeous model Natalie is in love with a louche international photographer who turns out to be a cheating cad and is swept off her feet by a handsome Greek shipping magnate with cheekbones of steel on the rebound, very standard Harlequin Presents stuff - and then somehow got confused and found herself writing the cad's book. That must be what happened, right?
