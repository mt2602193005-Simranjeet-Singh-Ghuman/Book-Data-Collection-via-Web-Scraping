In Nightlife, narcotraffickers steal a powerful South American drug that turns its users into monsters with the primordial instincts to hunt and kill and generally dominate everything around them. It’s a kind of cool idea with a decent structure–back and forth between various cartels, a Venezuelan native on the trail of revenge, and a harried love story–and some fun carnage. But as the second book in Dell’s Abyss print–their ostensibly more cerebral and experimental line of books–it’s a bit of a disappointment. The book raises the question of whether we, as a species, have lost something in the advancement of civilization, as it depicts an aboriginal tribe that is simultaneously venerable and misogynistic and a modern world that has made progress but lost its way. But the answers it provides are unsatisfying, muddled, and superficial. They do little more than briefly toy with lost rituals and lost connections. Otherwise, the love story, which involves a guy begrudgingly forgiving a girl for doing nothing wrong (being forced to star in a porn film), is forced. And while there is some inspired and nasty violence, I have to admit I was disappointed the drug never got released in a dance club for one massive orgy of blood. All in all, it’s nothing that great, but just enough to scratch that itch I often get for pulpy 1980s/early 1990s horror.
