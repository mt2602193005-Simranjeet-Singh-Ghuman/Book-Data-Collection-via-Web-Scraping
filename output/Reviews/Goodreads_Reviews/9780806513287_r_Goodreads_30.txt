According to PKD, "The majority of these stories were written when my life was simpler and made sense. I could tell the difference between the real world and the world I wrote about." Given how fractured and bleak many of his earlier short stories already are, this gives some indication as to how much worse he got over the years. The somewhat uncanny and suspicion-driven earlier stories successively became interwoven with stories of personal loss and cognitive disruption as he became "educated to the fact that the the greatest pain does not come zooming down from a distant planet, but up from the depths of the heart." As he acknowledges, "Of course, both could happen; your wife and child could leave you, and you could be sitting alone in your empty house with nothing to live for, and in addition the Martians could bore through the roof and get you." These realities all too clearly shape the final stories, which are fraught with domineering wives and malicious intelligences. Still, the pervading impression is not so much a sense of anger or frustration, but a profound loss of comprehension and connection and the resulting overwhelming sadness. His character's worlds are fracturing, and all of their attempts to put the pieces back together are destined to fail. My favourite stories: "The Little Black Box" (a spiritual predecessor of sorts to "Electric sheep"), "Faith Of Our Fathers" (might be my favourite of all of his short stories) and "The Exit Doors Lead In" (a bitingly funny perspective of our impending future).
