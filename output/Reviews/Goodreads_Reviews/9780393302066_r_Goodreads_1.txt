I can't say I'm a fan of a lot of the poetry included, partially because words are pronounced differently now (and I can't figure out how love was supposed to sound), and partially because the format of the poems was changing so much over this particular century (and sometimes just skill; too many were willing to wrench grammar out of place just to get the rhyme they wanted). But this collection can't take a hit for the quality, it is an anthology, and at that it excels. Sylvester used all of the original texts, so while the spelling is a little hard to decipher at times, the benefits of being able to see it how it was meant to be seen is enormous, and far more important than trying to enjoy the poetry. There is also the added bonus of being able to see the development of poetry as an art form over the course of the century. This is a little less obvious, and not the point of the text, but it is visible and educational.
