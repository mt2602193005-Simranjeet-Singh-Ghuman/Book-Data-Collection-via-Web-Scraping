Where to start on this wonderful book? It is so much more than a travel memoir. I love Grizzuti's prose, her manner of expressing herself. She doesn't just describe what she is seeing but the history behind the sculpture, the piazza, the place. Also what she is feeling. She will give you a new slant on something famous and obviously being well read she will come up with a marvellous quote from Keats, James, Stendhal, even Iris Origo for instance; a writer I have now marked down to read. From reading this book I now have a list of places I want to visit in Italy including: Raphael's Tomb, Rome; the Gardens of St Augustine,Capri; Alberobello with the funny trulli houses and Scanno, a beautiful cobbled medieval village in the south to name just a few. My only complaint concerning Italian Days would be that the section on Rome is too long, mainly because it was the author's favourite place and whilst reading this part of the book I did wonder why I had taken time out of my own writing to read such a long travel book. Shortly towards the end I was rewarded with these marvellous words of Grizzuti's. It will be the opening quote for my new novel and it is a perfect example of why I felt at home with this marvellous writer: "Does one carry landcape in one's body, in one's genes?"
