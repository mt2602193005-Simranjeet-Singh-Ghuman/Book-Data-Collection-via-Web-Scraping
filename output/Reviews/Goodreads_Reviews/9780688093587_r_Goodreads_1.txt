I've always been fascinated with stories of country life and living. And country homes, with their cozy, plaid-curtained kitchens and pewter cookware over warm hearths, French colonial blue kitchen cabinets, dried flowers in wicker baskets hanging on the eaves, old oak kitchen tables and mismatched shaker chairs, perennially-open kitchen windows, herbs and brightly-colored flowers competing with the view, Dutch-stencilled attic bedrooms with heirloom four-posters decked out in the quaintest quilt and multi-sized cushions, centuries-old camphor chests, distressed braided Quacker rugs, and delicate white lace curtains billowing on the windowsills. The country checklist could go on and on, and in case I missed something, this book has showed me how to give practically very nook and cranny of my house the country look--eclectic/shabby chic being the overall motif. And given my penchant for hoarding, mixing, and matching, this would have been the most doable of decorating schemes. But I acquired this book in the early nineties, new to married life, with nada idea of housekeeping nor housework, and no internet to help me out. And the country house treatment, while decidedly applicable in the cooler climates and rustic environs of rural Europe and the US, is downright impractical in the Philippines, where mosquitos and dust compete for space on every windowsill, shelf, nook, and cranny. And yet I learned so much from this book. Even if it's just brightening up a generic side table with something as simple as a spray of wildflowers in a chipped milk jug that has seen better days. This is one book that will always stay dear to my heart. * My edition shows a hearth on the foreground, mismatched chairs surrounding a large dining table topped with a pair of candlesticks, a blue rooster weathervane, and a bowl of fruit.
