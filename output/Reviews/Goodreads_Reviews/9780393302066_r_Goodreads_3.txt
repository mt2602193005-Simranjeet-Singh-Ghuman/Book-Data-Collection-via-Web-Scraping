Ah, the sonneteers, not always brilliant; the progression of the sonnet is interesting and fun. edit Oct 2020 Reread, mostly the Sonnets (avoiding the long poems), after reading Ovid's Amores and the poor Cavalier bros. Wyatt is in over his head, but trying to keep up with Petrarch made him more. Surrey was a notch better. Raleigh even better. Spencer is not right for the sonnet-too thick and convoluted. (Perhaps someone will manage to revive him, but the allegory of the Faerie Queene is too distant, poor fellow is on his way to a footnote.) Sidney was quite beyond his predecessors for a while, but got lost in his project. I would like to read more Campion--his material isn't heavy and he has a light, fun line. Samuel Daniel has nice lines, not flashy or fantastic, more smooth and pleasant than his peers. Drayton, also a nice read, some humor. Greville is nice and brings a mature perspective--not to take away anything from Shakespeare (because he is well beyond the others), but Drayton and Greville ease what would be other wise be a major leap directly from Sidney. Among the lot, one could build (and someone should) a nice collection of 40-60 poems from the lot -- add in Shakepeare, 3 from Marvell (though not sonnets), and maybe a few from Donne for a really nice book.
