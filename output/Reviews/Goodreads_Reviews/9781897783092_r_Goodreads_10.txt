As a big Mott The Hoople fan, I am surprised that I haven’t read this before. I remember an extract about Hunter trespassing in Graceland in the music press at the time of publication and that incident is definitely a highlight. Now 1972 seems an age away and sadly air travel isn’t the novelty that it seems to have been then judging by Ian’s frequent impressionistic descriptions of the experience in this book. It certainly lives up to its title and being cobbled together from the author’s field notes during the band’s US tour means that there isn’t much shape to it which seems appropriate given the ramshackle nature of the experience. There’s no glamour on show here; the band are at the mercy of unreliable promoters and they seem to pull out of as many gigs as they perform. Hunter takes the opportunity to scour pawn shops for cheap American guitars that he can resell at a profit back home because he certainly can’t have made much money out of this chaotic trip. I appreciated Ian’s honesty and his good intentions in wanting to give the fans an insight into his mundane life on tour. Even rock legends like Bowie, Zappa and Moon come across as being fairly ordinary.
