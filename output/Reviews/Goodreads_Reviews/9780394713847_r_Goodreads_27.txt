3.5 stars. An interesting, original novel that is in dialogue, apart from a handful of letters and a job application at the end of the novel. The book contains a succession of conversations between Ramirez and Larry, set in New York City. Juan Jose Ramirez, a 74 year old former political prisoner in Argentina, is very ill, with declining mental facilities. He is either in bed or in a wheelchair. Larry John is a divorced 36 year old, former college history tutor who is hired to push Mr. Ramirez around the city most days of the week. Larry suffers from depression. The conversations between Ramirez and Larry include discussion of their past lives. Ramirez feels guilt over the suffering he caused to his son back in Argentina. Larry was abused by his father. The novel explores the issues of honesty and the reliability of memory. An intriguing read about two unlikeable, unreliable characters. This book was first published in 1980.
