It's been a whopping fifteen years since I first read this as a kid. It's a strange and magical thing to return to something a former self has been obsessed with – though, as an adult now, I am not quite the target group for this anymore. This is the story of Tiuri, a 16-year old boy who is to be knighted the following day. His final task is to spent one last night in silence in a chapel. What sounds easy enough, suddenly becomes difficult when someone knocks on the door, urgently asking for help. A secret letter must be delivered to the King living across the Great Mountains. Suddenly Tiuri plays a vital role in deciding the fate of the kingdom, one on which his knighthood depends as well. This is a lovely tale, and Tiuri is the perfect protagonist. I didn't remember how good a person Tiuri was, but in retrospect I am quite pleased that this is the sort of hero my younger self was exposed to. Tiuri is utterly good . To like, a ridiculous degree. His morals are intact, so is his sense of honesty, loyalty and courage. What a guy. The writing is simple, straightforward (and not that stimulating for adults). Same goes for the story – I can't hold it against the book, as I can still recall the feelings this gave me as a kid I can guarantee that if you're a middle-grader, the reading experience will be different. The story probably won't even feel that fast-paced, simply because at that age, it takes way longer to get through a 500 paged book. You know, if you're an adult and don't feel drawn to this for nostalgic reasons, I wouldn't particularly go up to you and force you to engage with this. But you know, if you've got children around, then here's a gripping story with good and subtle morals for you.
