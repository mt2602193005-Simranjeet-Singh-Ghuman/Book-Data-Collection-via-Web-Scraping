This is a strange little pulp novel. After a run-in with a chauvinist penny pincher, the new Doctor Sara resigns from her job and heads to her adoptive parents' small, sea-side town to figure out her next step. Instead of a relaxing break, Sara finds herself facing a life-time job opportunity, more professional sexism, ill children, mysterious romantic paramours, and even a little espionage. One by one Gaddis lines up and quickly (and anticlimactically) solves each issue in just over a hundred pages. Though there are weird strangers, violent mysteries, possibly mistaken identities, and situations of national security, there is not one moment of intrigue. In fact, the climax of the tale is the explanation of the rest -- nothing more or less. The only element approached with care and interest -- showing Doctor Sara to be a principled, skilled, but not super heroic doctor. For 1959, I guess that was the shock.
