This book by Bernard Bailyn makes no pretense of being other than a collection of scholarly essays probably written previously. The reader will need to bear with the author's near-hagiography of Benjamin Franklin in Paris. But this history in interesting if only to show the progression of wild acclaim that Franklin did receive in France. The two most helpful essays for me were The Federalist Papers (chapter IV) and the impact of our creative constitutional experiment in countries across the Atlantic and even down into Latin America (chapter V). A good companion read to this book is Stuart Leibiger's "Founding Friendship: George Washington, James Madison, and the Creation of the American Republic." Leibiger's book is not analagous to Bailyn's work but it does give much needed detail on the characters of both Washington and Madison who only receive passing mention in Bailyn.
