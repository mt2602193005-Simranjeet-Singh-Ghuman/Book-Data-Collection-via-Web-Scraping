3.5 stars. An early Dean Koontz when he wrote under the pen name of Leigh Nichols. The bones of the book is too reminiscent of many others of the same ilk by a myriad of authors. Yet I was still taken in. Koontz wrote a humorous afterword in this edition but also acknowledged that it lacked a lot of qualities that he honed in later novels such as depth of characters, complexity of the plot and intensity. I will say that I thought the pacing was good as the suspense ratcheted up until the final denouement. Here's an example of some simplistic dialogue, yet sufficiently creepy. They stared into the shadows, At length she said, "Do you feel it?" "Feel what?" "I've got this.....prickly feeling." He didn't say anything. "You do feel it don't you?" she asked "Yes." "As if we are not alone." "It's crazy" he said, "but I feel eyes on me." She shivered, "But no one's really there" .....Tina cleared her throat, "It's as if...the night itself is watching us....the night, the shadows, the eyes of darkness."
