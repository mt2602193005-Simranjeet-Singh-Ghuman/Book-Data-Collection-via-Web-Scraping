This is the last of the Pay/Hasford books, following characters from Elkhorn Tavern in 1862 for 70 years or so, sending them out west and back to Arkansas over time. Eban Pay was the star of the last two books, and Weedy Rough finds his son, Barton, and grandson, Duny Gene living in the little town as respected citizens, although we start out with Duny Gene Pay as a boy and watch him grow up. The first two thirds of the book was pleasant enough, but the antics of a young boy in a small town began to get old and boredom was setting in. Then Duny Gene grew up, got into a world of trouble, and his grandfather Eban came to help. The last 100 pages gave this book an extra star. Eban Pay is a cagey old lawyer with a trick or two up his sleeve, and the trial that ensues is grand entertainment. And Eban realizes that even old men can learn new things about themselves. I'm sorry to be finished with this series, but not with Douglas C. Jones. He has become a favorite author (thank you, Howard), but he was very prolific before his death, so I have more to look forward to.
