Three Exemplary Novels presents three case studies of supremely forceful personalities- people willing to pit their wills against the very forces of life and death, shattering social conventions and family ties along their way. In "The Marquis of Lumbria," a woman stops at nothing to bear her family an heir, even though in doing so she must destroy her family's happiness and bend a young nobleman to her will. Similarly, the heroine of "Two Mothers" is obsessed with becoming a mother, but she is past child-bearing age and so must dominate her lover, a fertile young girl, and the girl's family so she can buy, trick, and seduce her way into motherhood-by-proxy. In "Nothing Less Than a Man," a willful girl longing to be loved clashes with her husband, a man too proud to admit that he needs anything, even his wife. Each story progresses according to the guiding passion of its spirited narrator, showing the power of the human spirit... and yet this is not a book celebrating humanity; the tales are too sad, too uncomfortable. Unamuno is a forerunner of existentialism, but there's also an element of the absurd in these stories. His protagonists achieve their goals, but doing so does not seem to bring them any real satisfaction. Perhaps by rejecting established truths, they find themselves in a world without any meaning at all. I enjoyed Three Exemplary Novels but I prefer Unamuno's protagonists who are less willful and more human.
