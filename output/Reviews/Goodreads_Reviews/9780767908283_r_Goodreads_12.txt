Absolutely fascinating book of Australian exploration history. I recently read a kids biography of Earnest Shackleton with my son. It's astounding the difference in quality between these men. Unlike Shackleton, Berke did not have leadership skills, survival skills, or a deep care for his fellow man. He just wanted to be the one to do it and that is likely one of the causes of much grief. The Berke and Wills expedition was one of the most well financed and poorly planned operations I've ever heard of or read about. The author does a fantastic job of telling the facts to the reader as they unveiled in real time. The ineptitude of the committees, the failures in communication, the inadequacies of the leadership, the delays in government responses, etc. On the positive end, she also explains the heart behind the explorer's spirit. Why did Victoria want to cross the continent, what beliefs existed about the centre of Australia? Is there an inland ocean? Is it all just desert? Is it actually just connected to Asia? (that was a real belief at the time). It masterfully brings you along for the journey, forces you to feel bad for shot camels and sick men, and makes you genuinely thirsty. Well worth a read, especially if you like exploration, biography, survival stories, or especially Australian history.
