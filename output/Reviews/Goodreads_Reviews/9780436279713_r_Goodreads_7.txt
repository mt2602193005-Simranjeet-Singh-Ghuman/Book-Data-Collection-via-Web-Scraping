2.5 stars. The picture Michener paints of the Caribbean is a macabre canvas indeed. You'll see very little of what tourists are exposed to in the jeweled Sea. The Caribbean was / is a sordid, violent place. There are small threads that let one sort of follow a few genealogies, but mostly each chapter is a stand alone snapshot of the islands' history. Michener does island hop, so you do learn of each island's past. Michener calls Caribbean a narrative and that it is. I think I finished this saga for spite. Several times I was going to stop reading, but finally the human slaughter stopped long enough to let me breathe a sigh of relief. For sure, you will learn things about Columbus you wish you never knew, but it is about the time frame which begins Michener's history. First, a chapter of an island's savagery, which sets the tone for the entire novel. The tale ends in the late 1980s. Good luck staying with this. It makes the middle ages of England and Europe pale by comparison.
