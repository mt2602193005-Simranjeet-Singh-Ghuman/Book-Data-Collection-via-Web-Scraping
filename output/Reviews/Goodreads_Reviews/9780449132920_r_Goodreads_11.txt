A Chicago neurosurgeon named Geis secretly liquidated most of his fortune into cash before he passed away, and the cash is now missing. His widow Glory is under suspicion of hiding it somewhere to keep it from the IRS and his own children, she having only married him a few years prior and being much younger than the good doctor. With no idea where the money is she reaches out to an old friend, Trav McGee. As Trav reconnects with the woman and starts to poke around the dead doctor's final months, he decides the lost fortune could only be the result of one thing: blackmail. Dr. Geis did have quite a few romantic indiscretions during and between marriages but in this mid-60's era of free love and LSD those didn't seem to embarrass him enough to hide via blackmail. McGee accepts Glory's request for help at his standard rate: half of the money recovered. As with most of the McGee tales “One Fearful Yellow Eye” (1966) has the bones of a mystery and noir crime investigation, surrounded by terror and abuse, and drowns in repressed sexual psychobabble the series always has. And in this episode MacDonald spins a lot of moral gymnastics to explain why a statutory rape was totally understandable and justfineokaydon'tbesuchaprudethisisnormalandthingsaretotesfine. Verdict: A weird mystery-thriller with some moral disconnects and extended focus on repressed adulthood make it more creepy than interesting.
