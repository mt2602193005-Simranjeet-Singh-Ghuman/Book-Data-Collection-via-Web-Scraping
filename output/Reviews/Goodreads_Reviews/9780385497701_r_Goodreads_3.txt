Ackroyd’s London: The Biography is a sprawling monster of a book (802 pages) but then how could a serious history of the city be anything less than sprawling. Information is not presented chronologically after first describing the primordial ooze. It is basically a social and cultural history—very little about kings etc. Ackroyd wanders by subject such as children, noise, darkness, entertainment, markets, alcohol, smells and so on. If you happen to find a topic that is unappealing you could move on, but that didn’t happen much for me and thus it took a very long time to finish. As always, Ackroyd throws out interesting things I’ve never heard of but does have some unifying themes. To him London has an underlying changelessness in spite of fashion, architectural changes, bombings and fire. Roads and neighborhoods often follow ancient patterns in spite of disasters and attempted swaths of removal, erasure and road replanning. For the author, “it is the nature of the city to encompass everything.” It’s an amazing book. If there is a fault for me it is Ackroyd’s trying to squeeze things into themes that have already been talked about such as business and money are the heart of London’s history and unique. It is very true that London became the business center of England very early, but every city probably has commerce at its beginnings.
