5 delighted and funny stars The thing is with Heyer that you never know... or at least, I never know! There is always this sense of gamble whenever I decide to open a (for me) new book of hers, as previous experience taught me that it can go 3 ways: love, mild appreciation or active dislike. The last 2 books ( An Infamous Army & The Convenient Marriage ) unfortunately fell into the "active dislike" category, so I have been reluctant to pick up Cotillion for a while now. But I'm happy I finally have. While Freddy is so very much different from my favourite Heyer-hero (Sir Waldo Hawkridge) I couldn't help, but love him right from the start and noone was more surprised than myself when I realised this. (Other GR friends already made this reference, so I'm just reiterating by saying that he's like the regency version of Bertie Wooster, but with actual Jeeves-vibes, which is an improvement, needless to say. ;) Also, for me this has been Heyer's funniest book so far, even topping The Grand Sophy . I wish the last part of the book had gone on longer, I wouldn't have minded another 50 pages or 100. 😊
