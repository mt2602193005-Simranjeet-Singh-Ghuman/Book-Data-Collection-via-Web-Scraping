Rachel finds a blister on her toe and after her mother can’t rid of it, she enlists in the whole community to come to help Rachel get rid of the blister. Join the priest, Rachel’s mother, her brothers, and others in the search for the cure of the toe blister. They all offer advice of how to cure the blister; from drinking tea, to pouring broccoli on it, to mixing between ice and heat. In the end, it’s up to the Queen to offer her advice. Read this funny, engaging poem and see if the Queen’s advice helps in getting rid of the blister. I would use this book when dealing with poetry in the classroom. This is a fun book that is unique in that it tells a story in poem format of something you normally wouldn't read about. I think this book would be engaging for the students to read and would also provide them of examples of matching the illustrations to the story. I think to incorporate this book into my classroom I would read it aloud and then have students create their own poem about something that may not normally be written about, much like a blister. This to me is a WOW book because i just think it is so great/amusing that there was a poetry book written about a young girl’s blister on her toe. The book is so intriguing and engaging for students or anyone to read. I think that the illustrations are chaotic much like the wording and plot of the story, so it just adds to the excitement of the book.
