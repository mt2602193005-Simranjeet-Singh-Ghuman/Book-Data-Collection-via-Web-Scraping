This book has all of Heyer's trademarks - witty dialogue, likable characters and plenty of confusion. :) Throw in identical twins, a smart heroine, a sweet but irresponsible Mamma and an irascible Great-Grandmother, and you have a really great story. Unfortunately, this particular book is so loaded with slang dialogue, especially at the beginning, that it was tough to get into. In one random page the heroine refers to hersef as "a bumble-broth", "a woolly-crown", with a "skimble-skamble mind" and says "flummery" twice. There were some speeches where the slang outweighed the clear speech by a large margin. The meaning is always clear enough but it really slows me down as a reader, and I find a little of that kind of thing goes a long way. My impression was that at about the 30% mark the slang dropped way down, but it's possible I got better at overlooking it. Either way, I liked these characters and their predicament so much I always wanted to keep reading. But the lingo was a bit distracting at first. NB - I later revisited this book in audio format and LOVED it! The slang didn't bother me at all when someone else was coping with it. I did notice that the humor shows up more in the last half of the book, but I didn't mind because the first half gives us a lot of time with Kit and Cressie together. Which I loved. And Cressie has become one of my favorite heroines.
