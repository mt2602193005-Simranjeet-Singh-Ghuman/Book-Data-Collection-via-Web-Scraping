Holy scripture. I expected to like Flowers in the Attic. I didn't expect it to be Classic Literature with a capital C. This book is truly of the greatest-of-all-time variety. Certainly among the Top 10 finest Gothic novels ever written. Probably more like Top 5. I would gladly take it over Jane Eyre and Wuthering Heights , though there are traces of its descent from these prior masterpieces. I will say I'm appalled by the 40th anniversary edition's tagline at the top: "The classic bestselling novel of forbidden love." Ugh! Did they not even read it? No wonder I went in with the wrong expectations. The romance element is part of the horror, part of the Gothic-ness, of the novel. This is not incest Twilight, y'all. It's about multi-generational abuse, how wickedness runs in a vicious cycle, about the horror of survival. But mostly, it's about mothers. Holy sh!t. Those mothers, all three of them (I count Cathy too). Characters this powerful, this life-changing to the reader, are wonders of the universe. My life has become divided since I closed this book. My knowledge of the universe before reading Flowers in the Attic , and after. It's a darker, scarier place on the other side, but I wouldn't change it for the world. As Cathy explains it in the final chapter: "The wisdom of the attic was in my bones, etched on my brain, part of my flesh." This is me now. The wisdom of Flowers in the Attic is with me, and there's no going back to who I was before. If you're looking for a book that will literally transfigure your entire perspective of the human experience—while also being a thrill ride from beginning to end—this is it. I only wish I'd read it sooner.
