A mediocre book that also leaves a gross taste in the modern reader's mouth. A shame, because I typically like Francis. If you're wondering what kind of industry culture enabled Weinstein for so many years, look no further - this book (unintentionally and uncritically) captures it quite well. The main character, a movie director, whines incessantly about the movie's female star being 'feminist'. He does metaphorical backflips to suck up to his film's male star, but is sorely tried by the effort required of him to merely remember not to sound like a condescending pervert when talking to his female counterpart. He complains about this imposition more than he does about getting stabbed in the chest. The producer, the person who cast that same female lead, is quite obviously using his position to get her to sleep with him. Our hero's reaction is one of envious admiration. Also, several references are made to the 'casting couch' throughout the book. Just... ugh.
