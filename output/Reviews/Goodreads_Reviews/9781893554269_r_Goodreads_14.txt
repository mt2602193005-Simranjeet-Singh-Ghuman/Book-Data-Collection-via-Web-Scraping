"Absolute values, moral shame, and the Greek tragic approach to human existence are either dismissed as reactionary or deemed the embarrassing property of the religious right..." This book was first published in 1998, not so many years from the date of my own birth... It is very much about the fate of the field of Classical Studies on American soil. Its subjects are the institutional domains of which have historically produced honorable people, good character, and good values. These three matters have grown deafeningly ambiguous in America today. I have no professional background in working with the Greco-Roman culture whatsoever and I only possess the personal discoveries I've uncovered reading within the discipline/genre independently. It was in 2018 that I had first read this book and it has really been quite something to reread during the end of 2023. Then I had absolutely no interest in attending to university. It happens to be the case that today I am ironically a student inside the leviathan, and the contents of this work have become more relevant than I could have ever imagined. Truthfully, I hadn't understood then that I had read something that would, as the years pass, grow in importance, value, and accuracy. What was once 4 stars now becomes 5.
