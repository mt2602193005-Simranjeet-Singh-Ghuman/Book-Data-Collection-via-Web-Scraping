This isn’t a traditional biography and should only be read by people who want to know about Luther’s ideas and how he hammered them out. Marius is engaging with other scholars of Luther and the writing while clear and engaging is very dense. Marius is showing the development of Luther’s ideas and how they formed in conversation with others at the same time and the medieval scholarship of the day. He is pushing back against some of the mythology—both those who dislike Luther and those defending him. He reminds us that Luther the preacher had a different agenda than Luther the writer. Marius’s main thesis is in his subtitle—that Luther was mostly concerned with death and salvation’s defeat of that. He seems to hint that Luther didn’t have traditional views of hell and it was death itself that concerned him. He does seem to take on some of the psychological history methods in analyzing Luther, tho it isn’t really a psycho-history.
