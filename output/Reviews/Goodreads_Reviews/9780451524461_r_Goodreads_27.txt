"Struck dead by an angel of God! Yet the angel must hang!" My first Melville read, and it definitely took me some time to settle into his lofty style of writing and his intentions for this short, but ultimately very rich moral tale. "But your scruples: do they move as in a dusk? Challenge them. Make them advance and declare themselves." The setting, plot, characters are all set in service to one powerful, plaintive speech from the pained Captain Vere plumbing the novella's central question of how a moral man must act when conscience, duty, righteousness and the greater good all seem at odds. "You sign sad assent. Well, I too feel that, the full force of that. It is Nature. But do these buttons that we wear attest that our allegiance is to Nature? No, to the King." I also learned some cool boat stuff.
