The book gives a concise explanation of the advaita vedanta philosophy. It covers what it means to be "Enlightened" and how one can reach such a state first through reaching a state of non-attachment and then understanding one's conscious and unconscious mind. The book emphasizes on meditation as a practice to reach such a state, and highlights the importance of simplifying the practice so the mind can be brought to focus internally. However, according to Advaita Vedanta enlightenment can only be attained through the understanding of the non-dualistic understanding of God hence the title is misleading. The book also fails to expand upon this non-dualism and gets away by mentioning that it is something that can only be experienced. To emphasize the highest level of enlightment is to realize the non-duality and yet act according to the established dualistic framework. While concise, the book serves as a good reminder and introduction to the Vedic teachings, albeit for those already aware of the basics, the book does not teach something new.
