A puzzle book - y'know, brain teasers, anagrams, crossword puzzles - done up as short stories. The short story is actually never a short story; it's all chatter, just Asimov showing off his brain by spreading all his knowledge among nine guys in one room. We have a Jeeves paste-in named Henry. Someone tells a story at each meeting of the Club, so you would say "THERE'S your short story, curmudgeon!", as if we are getting a short story inside the opening and closing chatter. But of course the story within the (non-)story is at best a stop-and-start story, because the chatterers just chatter about the story as it is chattered out by the chattering guest. Just know: whatever is going on in the guest's tale, it's just contrived mush, maybe with some vague whiff of some minor crime or not, which just leads to the part where you get to play word-games or hirsute trivial pursuit with Asimov disguised as nine or so two-dimensional fictional mouths attached to proud sections of Asimov brain. Highlights include the opening, "random" chatter almost always and by sheer coincidence being a clue to whatever game is being poked at, once the guest's tale begins, and the stories that are full of astronomy facts and figures because Asimov sold the story to a Science magazine, not a Mystery magazine. I don't read fiction so I can suddenly find myself doing crossword puzzles and word games. Two or three of the tales in this one book are premised on the same gimmick, which really blows ten portions of my own fast-fracturing brain (I'm finished the book, so I should start feeling better). On one level, though, I can see that this type of writing totally hits the mark for a huge audience that does not include me. I need story with stakes and drama...not dinner talk about some contrived thing that happened to some walking cardboard-guy, all because Asimov is amused by the fact that "in car, Nadine" sounds like "incarnadine" (that example is not in the stories - well, not specifically).
