I read this book because I am very interested in the topic of followership, which Robert Kelley has also written on. The chapters dedicated to followership, small "L" leadership, and teamwork were very significant in encouraging the skills of working for and with others. While there is a very clear secular, business aim at "brainpowered workers," with a view toward enhancing one's career, I appreciated that the path of career furtherance outlined by Kelley takes people to a place of benefiting others by our efforts and contributions, including setting aside oneself and foregoing praise, and instead contributing to the bottom line (critical path) of the larger organization as an unselfish team player. I believe such a model can be very useful to those like myself who are not involved in such corporate environments, but rather work in non-profits or education. Kelley includes both practical skills (e.g. giving presentations) along with social & relational skills (e.g. using one's perspective and initiative), making for a well-rounded approach to being an effective contributor to the group, to others, and in benefit of one's own career opportunities. Well worth a read.
