This is really an excellent book, providing a deeply personal account of the effects of Australia's colonial project on an Indigenous family. Written from the perspective of Sally Morgan, My Place tells the story of her family, including her grandmother, mother, siblings, and her own children and relationships. In particular, the story of her family's journey of discovering their identity as Palyku people from Western Australia's Pilbara region. Sally tells the story from her perspective growing up, with the inclusion of her great-uncle Arthur's life story, her mother Gladys' story, and finally, the story of her grandmother Daisy. The telling of each story gradually reveals more about Sally's upbringing, the reasoning behind certain family secrets, and the struggles that her family faced in the context of 1950s-1980s Australia. This is a classic for a reason, and personally, I learned more about the devastating effects of Australia's colonial project from this more personal, family-level account, than I did in 12 years of public schooling. I would highly recommend this to anyone.
