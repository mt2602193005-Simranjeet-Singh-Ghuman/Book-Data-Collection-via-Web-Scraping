This seems to me to be pretty overtly an answer or homage to Dubliners , this time with Washington, D.C. as the backdrop city and working-class Black Americans rather than working-class Irish Catholics forming the cast. Jones's prose, like Joyce's in his own debut, is confidently understated and rich with dialogue, his plots similarly minimal, his conclusions likewise open-ended. Jones also takes after Joyce in that most of his evocation of place is done via the frequent naming of streets and neighborhoods rather than through sensory or spatial description; you'll learn plenty about the people of D.C., but if you're not already well-acquainted with the less touristy segments of the city itself (I confess I'm not) Jones isn't likely to make your mental picture much clearer. Though I believe Jones's later novel The Known World to be a masterpiece I never quite managed to get on board with this, a response which I think owes much more to my ongoing struggle with the Literary Short Story as a form than to any actual artistic shortcomings on Jones's part. He's a consummate craftsman whatever he's doing, I'm just always personally going to prefer that kind of thing to this.
