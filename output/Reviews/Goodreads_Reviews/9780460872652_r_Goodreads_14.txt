What a memorable character is Egil Skallagrimsson, hero of Icelandic saga, Egil’s Saga of the Viking age. Spanning Norway, Iceland and England and written in approximately 1230, Egil’s Saga is an epic family drama, full of feuds, vengeance, killings, love, hatred, friendship interspersed with beautiful descriptive poetry. It’s an insight into the historical drama of the Arctic Scandinavian North and has been an awesome and invigorating first read of 2026 for me. In the introduction to the Penguin’s Classics edition of the sage, Egil Skallagrimsson is described as ‘an ugly, recalcitrant child…. a killer and poet… ruthless… ingenious lawyer and raging drunk…. settled farmer and enemy of kings.” And having read the saga, alleged to have been recorded by Snorri Sturluson, I can concur that Egil is all of these characteristics and more. He’s a rogue and not someone I would want to cross or mess with. What other 6 year old drives an axe through the head of an 11 year old in the context of a ball game because you’ve been roughhoused during play? Not the kind of child’s play that I remember! I’ve got new respect for the Vikings of the time. At times, when I was reading I really felt like I was immersed in a Vikings TV episode! I loved the historical narratives intertwined throughout this saga. Kings of Norway, the Alþingi in Iceland, battles in England and Scotland. It all comes together to paint a picture of the times and I’m now hankering to read more of these, study the maps and read more of the history. Fascinating stuff and very very raw and real. 5 star start to the year.
