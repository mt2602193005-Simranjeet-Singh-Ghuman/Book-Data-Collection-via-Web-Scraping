Four stars but only just. It's three novellas so the writing is by necessity pretty tight. The first two stick pretty well to the basic SF conceit - make one change (in this case improved transplant surgery) and explore the ramifications (organs stolen from kidnap victims). It's told from the POV of a cop hunting the criminals and he's got a twist: having lost an arm in an accident he has developed a "psychic" arm, which is how he visualises his telekinesis. In both stories that psychic arm is critical to the plot and it all works out logically. Well done. The third story features the same cop but this time it's a time accelerating field and its murdered inventor. It's pretty well done but let's face it, if you make up the functions of the machine it's easy to fit a story around it. And Gil Hamilton's psychic arm doesn't really make much difference here. Niven is known for his hard science approach to SF but in these stories, written earlier in his career, it hasn't quite jelled. One egregious example: an 80 story building of tiny, compact apartments has a 1,000 foot frontage (that's 300m!) and only one entrance, lift lobby and supervisor. Sounds a bit impractical to me and I'm sure Niven will catch such impracticalities as he advances.
