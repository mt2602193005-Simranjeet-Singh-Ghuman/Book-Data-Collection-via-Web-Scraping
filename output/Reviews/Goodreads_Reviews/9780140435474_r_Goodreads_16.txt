It’s been reported that Thomas Hardy felt that this was his favorite novel, and I can see why. Of the six Hardy novels that I have now read, I can see the uniqueness in this one. The woodland setting is filled by Hardy with intricate subtleties and his characters intertwine with this setting with their own intricacies. There are no truly dramatic events or people. This novel is filled with life and its this harmony between forest and woodlanders that makes this novel unique. Like most Hardy novels, there are numerous passages that focus exclusively on the nature of the land, its plants, flowers, trees, and wildlife. It makes me believe that Hardy must have wanted to have been a landscape artist more so than a writer, but found that his talents rested in words rather than paint. Within The Woodlanders there are forests and glens of mysterious beauty that Hardy brings to life. The plot sits at the real beginnings of class fluidity. A country girl is made into a sophisticated lady through education rather than birth. And a woodsman, through enterprising work, becomes a wealthy merchant who can’t help but live the simple life of his forefathers. And yet others, remain in life as countless generations before them. It’s the subtle stresses of interaction among all these people that drive the plot and creates not just a novel of interest, but also a novel of life as it existed back then.
