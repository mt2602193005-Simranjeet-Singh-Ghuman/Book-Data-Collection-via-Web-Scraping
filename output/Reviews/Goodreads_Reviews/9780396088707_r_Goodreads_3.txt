The Secret of Chimneys , the first Superintendent Battle novel, is more a thriller than a murder mystery. I'm a mystery fan, so I'm a bit slow to appreciate her thrillers. Most of her thrillers are national and international political intrigues, which, in my opinion, she doesn't write as well as her mysteries. However, I was pleased with this thriller to a certain degree. There were things I missed, but overall, things were good enough to keep me engaged. Let me say simply what I liked about this novel. I liked the hero and heroine - Anthony Cade and Virginia Revel. It was an instinctive liking, and they didn't disappoint me. And I thoroughly enjoyed the humour. The story felt rather far-fetched, too fantastic even. Still, it held a certain fascination for me. I'm a person who goes for plausible plots, proper mysteries, and solutions. :) But I didn't mind their absence here. The characters and the humour entertained me. However, as the novel inclined toward a thriller rather than a mystery, there was limited police procedure, and Inspector Battle played a minor role. Since the series belongs to Superintendent Battle, I like to consider him as the protagonist. And he playing a minor role is disappointing. I do prefer a police detective anytime to an amateur, so Anthony Cade getting the upper hand over Superintendent Battle didn't sit that well with me. I know this sounds a little prejudiced, but I can only offer an apology. Overall, however, it was an enjoyable read. And although this may not be one of Agatha Christie's finely written novels, it certainly is an amusing one. More of my reviews can be found at http://piyangiejay.com/
