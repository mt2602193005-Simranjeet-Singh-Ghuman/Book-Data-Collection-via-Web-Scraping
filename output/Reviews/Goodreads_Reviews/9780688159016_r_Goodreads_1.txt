I suspect I purchased this book (remaindered) more than 15 years ago because I had not read anything by James Purdy. I picked it off my bookshelf, and in spite of the description which did not grab me - mother explores life of dead daughter - began reading this last weekend. Purdy writes well and I finished 70-80 pages at first sitting. The novel does not have chapters - just dividing symbols - but the prose is straightforward. At the next setting I was losing interest because I felt the plot bogged down with new characters. But ultimately I finished the novel (it is only 182 pages) and was satisfied by the experience. Based on this, I would definitely pick up another Purdy novel if I see it. PS - Although I haven't found any confirmation, I suspect that the dead daughter, Gertrude, is based closely upon Chicago "bohemian" painter Gertrude Abercrombie. A young Purdy was part of her circle. But I'll leave this to the professors and grad students.
