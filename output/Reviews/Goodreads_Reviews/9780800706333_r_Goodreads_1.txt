This book has some solid advice, but the theology kept me from finishing it. Patterns from Israel’s experience are imputed into our experience as exactly how it will go down— which isn’t true. Concepts, specifically the “promised land” and what Israel went through to get there, are applied to our goals and hopes as our “promised lands”; Israel becomes the blueprint for all of us individually, and the context is ignored. The “promised land” is watered down into whatever we desire to grow toward. I don’t stand with the interpretation of the Old Testament, but the author’s understanding of trials still holds some wisdom. All in all, 3 stars seemed adequate
