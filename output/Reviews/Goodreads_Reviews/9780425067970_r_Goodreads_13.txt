Hollow (2.5 ⭐️) I’m honestly not sure how to feel about this one. The political touch and the boldness of the central theme were definitely risky and daring, which I appreciated. The storyline itself was decent, and I liked how Christie tied certain threads together at the end. That said, something was missing. I felt detached from the characters; they seemed more like random figures with loosely connected stories than people with depth or proper backstories. Even though Christie worked to link them later on, it never fully clicked for me. The execution fell short on several levels, and the motive behind the murder struck me as almost ludicrous. Add to that the double marriage subplot, which was hard to reconcile with the way the man was simultaneously framed as one of the most honest and admirable people in the story—it just didn’t align. Overall, I could see the premise and the vision, but the delivery didn’t land as strongly as it could have.
