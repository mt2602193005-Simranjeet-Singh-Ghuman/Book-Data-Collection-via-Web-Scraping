If your parents are dicks, your life sucks. If your characters aren't realistic, your book sucks. Marilyn and James are the dick-i-est of parents since Fos and Opal in Evidence of Things Unseen . (Fos and Opal committed suicide on the eve of their young son's birthday so their bodies were the first thing he saw when he got up. What a birthday present, huh?) I started out disliking Marilyn and James, but by about a quarter of the way into the book, I found them too ridiculous to take seriously any more. Under Ng's heavy-hand, they became little more than badly sketched caricatures of self-centered assholes. Then there are the three children. One is favored to ridiculous extremes. One is berated and made fun of at every turn. The third is half-forgotten and ignored--which, considering these parents, isn't that bad a thing. Once again the author goes to such extremes that she takes what could have been interesting characters and flattens them into uninteresting, two-dimensional parodies. These weren't kids--they were wind-up toys working through their set pieces over and over and over and.... As for the writing, it is boring and over-blown. It gave a feel of being over-worked, stilted--as though the author had spent hours writing and rewriting until it wasn't just perfect, but too perfect. I struggled through this in the hope there would be some redeeming big reveal at the end. There wasn't. There was just more of the same. And that's a shame. The premise was good. The problems of mixed race children was extremely interesting. It's just too bad the writer got too carried away with herself and her words to let her characters live and breathe and speak for themselves.
