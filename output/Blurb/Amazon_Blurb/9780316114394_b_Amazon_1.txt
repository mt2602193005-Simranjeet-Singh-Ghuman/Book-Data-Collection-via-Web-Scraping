Airborne is a story seamlessly intertwining travel and reflection and beautifully depicting the vast and other-worldly quality of the ocean - a space untouched and unmarred by human habitation. William Buckley recounts his life-affirming adventure with his son, Christopher, as they sail across the Atlantic aboard his sailboat, Cyrano. Their voyage brought back to life as Buckley outlines the perils, wonders and mysteries of the sea with vivid descriptions of the pair's daily musings; allowing us to join the father-son duo in enjoying the ebb and flow of sea life. Buckley considers his successes as both a seaman and a father. Through interweaving narratives, what at first appears to be a chronicle of sailing, transforms into an introspective reflection of life as Buckley reminisces on his past and previous sailing experiences, recounting stories from as far back as when he was just thirteen. Buckley Jr. (Nov. 1925 – Feb. 2008) was an author and commentator born in New York. He founded the National Review magazine in 1955, as well as writing over fifty books covering a variety of topics from history and politics to sailing.
